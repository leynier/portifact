How long artifacts stay online

  • An artifact stays online for 30 days after you publish it. Every update or renewal starts the 30 days again. On a plan with pinning you can pin an artifact so it does not expire while it is pinned.
  • After that it is archived: it goes offline but is kept for six months on the free plan, or 12 months on Pro and Team, and you can restore it at any time during that period. The period is set by the plan the space has when the artifact is archived, and a later plan change does not move it.
  • At the end of that period, it is deleted for good. You can also delete an artifact yourself at any time, which cannot be undone.
  • A workspace's policy can archive its artifacts sooner, as described under Workspace policies.

Portifact is not a backup service. Keep your own copies of anything you need.

Workspaces

A workspace lets an organization own artifacts together. Workspaces are part of the Team and Enterprise plans, and we create them on request; you cannot create one yourself yet. The organization or person that asked for a workspace and accepted these terms for it is the workspace's customer, and its admins act for it. Each member acts in a workspace with a role:

  • Admins manage members, invitations, roles, groups, verified email domains, service tokens, policies, branding and everything editors can do, and read the audit log.
  • Editors publish and manage every artifact of the workspace.
  • Members read and comment on what is shared with them or with the workspace, and manage nothing.
  • Admins and editors can open, change, share, archive and delete every artifact of the workspace, see every comment on them and read their access logs, whoever published it. Only admins can read the audit log, which records what each member did.
  • The workspace's customer controls the access, management and retention of content published in or moved into the workspace. Leaving a workspace or being removed does not delete its content. Deleting your account does not delete the workspace or the artifacts you published in or moved to it, which the workspace's customer continues to control. Account deletion does remove your comments and review verdicts on workspace artifacts; some comments are replaced by a "message deleted" marker, as the Privacy Policy explains. None of this by itself transfers copyright or other ownership rights; those depend on the law and any separate agreement with the customer. Only an admin can move an artifact out of a workspace.
  • The plan's seats limit the members plus the pending invitations. A lower plan or fewer seats removes nobody, but new members and invitations are refused until the workspace is under the limit.
  • When an artifact moves to another space, the people who manage that space take over its history too: its versions, comments, access log and view counts.
  • The last admin of a workspace cannot leave it or delete their account until another member is an admin or the workspace is deleted. We delete a workspace at its admins' request; its artifacts are then deleted like those of a deleted account.
  • Admins can put members in groups. An artifact can be shared with a group, and the admins who fill a group decide who reads every artifact shared with it.
  • An artifact of a workspace can be shared with every member, or with the workspace's verified email domains: then anyone signed in with a verified address at one of those domains can open it too. Admins who verify a domain must be entitled to act for it, and the workspace is responsible for who holds addresses at its domains. We remove a domain when its DNS record is missing for three daily checks in a row or when another workspace proves control of it.
  • The people who manage an artifact on a plan with approval reviews can ask named people to approve it or request changes. A request does not give anyone access to the artifact.
  • On a plan with content search, the workspace's admins and editors, and its service tokens within their reach, can search the text of the workspace's active artifacts.

What the workspace customer is responsible for

  • Telling its members, and the people it invites to read or review its artifacts, before it invites, lists or provisions them, what its admins and editors can see (every artifact of the workspace, every comment on them and who opened them, and for admins the audit log of what each member did), and having a lawful basis to process their data that way, including as an employer.

Workspace policies

On the Enterprise plan, a workspace's admins can set policies that bind every member, admins and service tokens included. They can forbid public access, choose the access new artifacts start with, limit readers to some email domains, require a password for share links or forbid them, limit how long share links live, limit the networks people and service tokens can act from, ask people to sign in again after some hours, and limit which kinds of apps act on the workspace.

  • Confirming a stricter policy changes existing artifacts: public artifacts become workspace-only, readers outside the allowed domains are removed, and share links are revoked or stop opening. The dashboard shows what will change before an admin confirms.
  • Maximum life of artifacts. Admins can set a number of days after which every artifact of the workspace is archived, counted from its last publication, restore or move into the workspace, including artifacts its members published. Pinning is not available while it is set. A new or shorter maximum takes effect 7 days after it is saved. Archived artifacts can be restored until their archive ends, and are then deleted like any archived artifact.
  • Comment retention. Admins can set a period, from 30 days to ten years, after which comment threads on the workspace's artifacts are deleted for good with every message once their last activity is that old. This includes threads written by people outside the workspace who were invited to read or review. Open threads on an artifact's current version are kept; once a new version replaces it, they get the whole period again from that moment. It takes effect 7 days after it is set, and the admins are told by email. You cannot recover a deleted thread through Portifact. Earlier copies may remain in database backups for up to 30 days, out of reach of the service; if we restore a backup, the deleted threads are deleted again before access reopens.
  • Available workspace policies keep applying after the plan ends, until an admin lifts them, except comment retention, which is removed when the plan no longer includes policies; no thread is deleted by it after that.

Share links

  • On a plan with share links, you can create links that let anyone holding them read the current version of one artifact without an account, optionally with a password and an expiry of up to 365 days. A link does not let its holder comment.

  • Links stop opening while the plan does not include them and work again when it does. Suspending, archiving or deleting the artifact stops its links.
  • People who open an artifact through a link accept the Acceptable Use Policy by using it, like anyone who opens a public artifact.

Branding

  • On a plan with branding, the admins of your account or workspace can show a name, a logo, an accent color and a link on the Portifact bar above your artifacts.
  • You must have the rights to the name, logo and link you show, including any trademarks. A brand may not imitate Portifact or another person or organization.
  • The bar always says that the page is hosted on Portifact, shows the artifact's real address and offers a report link.

Service tokens and publish keys

  • On a plan with service tokens, a workspace's admins can create tokens that act for the workspace from automation such as continuous integration, with the editor role, the scopes they choose and an expiry of at most 366 days. A token can be limited to the artifacts it created or to artifacts whose publish key starts with a prefix.
  • The workspace is responsible for everything done with its tokens and for keeping them secret. Revoke a token you no longer need or that may have leaked. A token stops when it is revoked or expires, while the plan does not include service tokens, and when the workspace is deleted.
  • A publish key is a name you choose for an artifact, so automation can update the same artifact every time. It is kept with the artifact and freed when the artifact is deleted.

The workspace is responsible for what its directory sends and for having a basis to provision its people.
  • Audit streams. On the Enterprise plan, a workspace's admins can send its audit log continuously to an HTTPS endpoint they choose and run. Once sent, the data is held by that endpoint under the customer's own terms and responsibility.
  • GitHub Actions federation. On a plan with service tokens, a workspace's admins can trust a GitHub repository so its workflow runs get short-lived credentials without a stored token. The workspace is responsible for the repositories it trusts and for what their workflows do.
  • Data kept in the EU. On the Enterprise plan, a workspace can be created so that its stored content is kept in the European Union, as the Privacy Policy will describe. The region is chosen when the workspace is created and never changes.

A suspended account cannot publish, and its artifacts are suspended with it; it can still sign in, export its data and delete itself.

When we restrict content, a feature or an account for illegality or a breach of these terms, we give the person affected a clear explanation no later than when the restriction takes effect, where we know how to contact them: by email to the account of a personal space, or to each admin of a workspace, and in the dashboard. You can ask us to review a decision by replying or writing to abuse@portifact.com.

We report suspected crimes that threaten someone's life or safety, and child sexual abuse or exploitation, to the competent authorities, and we preserve the information the law requires, with restricted access, and delete it when that duty ends. While we must preserve an artifact, it stays suspended and nobody can open, download, move or delete it, its managers included; deleting the account or workspace that holds it deletes everything else, and the preserved artifact is deleted once that duty ends.

Authorities and users can reach us as a single point of contact at legal@portifact.com, in English or Spanish.
