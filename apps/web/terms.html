What Portifact provides

Portifact publishes static artifacts (HTML sites, Markdown and plain text) at their own address, lets you decide who can open each one, and, as it becomes available, lets the people you invite comment on them. It runs no builds, servers or databases for your content.

  • Portifact is free to use on the free plan, which has quotas: today 50 active artifacts, 250 MB per artifact, 2.5 GB per account, 100 publications every 24 hours, and 1 million requests and 25 GB served per calendar month across all of your artifacts. Paid plans raise these quotas and add features, as described under Plans. When the monthly traffic is used up, your artifacts answer with an error until the next month, while you can still publish and manage them. We may change the quotas; when we lower one, we will tell you first, and what is already published stays online until it expires.
  • Portifact is in early development. Features may change, move or be removed, and some described in the documentation are still being built. There is no guaranteed availability or support response time.
  • New accounts cannot make artifacts public during their first 15 minutes.

How long artifacts stay online

  • An artifact stays online for 30 days after you publish it. Every update or renewal starts the 30 days again. On a paid plan you can pin an artifact so it does not expire while it is pinned.
  • After that it is archived: it goes offline but is kept for six months on the free plan, or 12 months on a paid plan, and you can restore it at any time during that period. The period is set by the plan you have when the artifact is archived, and a later plan change does not move it.
  • At the end of that period, it is deleted for good. You can also delete an artifact yourself at any time, which cannot be undone.

Portifact is not a backup service. Keep your own copies of anything you need.

Workspaces

A workspace lets a team own artifacts together. Workspaces are on the Team and Enterprise plans, and we create them on request; you cannot create one yourself yet. Each member acts in a workspace with a role:

  • Admins manage members, invitations, roles, verified email domains, service tokens, branding and everything editors can do, and read the audit log.
  • Editors publish and manage every artifact of the workspace.
  • Members read and comment on what is shared with the workspace and manage nothing.
  • Admins and editors can open, change, share, archive and delete every artifact of the workspace, see every comment on them and read their access logs, whoever published it.
  • What you publish in a workspace, or move into it, belongs to the workspace. It stays there when you leave, are removed or delete your account. Only an admin can move an artifact out of a workspace.
  • The plan's seats limit the members plus the pending invitations. A lower plan or fewer seats removes nobody, but new members and invitations are refused until the workspace is under the limit.
  • The last admin of a workspace cannot leave it or delete their account until another member is an admin or the workspace is deleted. We delete a workspace at its admins' request; its artifacts are then deleted like those of a deleted account.
  • An artifact of a workspace can be shared with every member, or with its verified email domains: then anyone signed in with a verified address at one of those domains can open it too. Admins who verify a domain must be entitled to act for it, and the workspace is responsible for who holds addresses at its domains. We remove a domain when its DNS record is missing for three daily checks in a row or when another workspace proves control of it.

Share links

  • On a plan with share links, you can create links that let anyone holding them read the current version of one artifact without an account, optionally with a password and an expiry of up to one year. A link does not let its holder comment.
  • You are responsible for who gets a link and its password. Anyone who has them can open the artifact and pass them on. Revoke a link to stop it.
  • Links are paused while the plan does not include them and work again when it does. Suspending, archiving or deleting the artifact stops its links.
  • People who open an artifact through a link accept the Acceptable Use Policy by using it, like anyone who opens a public artifact.

Branding

  • On a plan with branding, the admins of your account or workspace can show a name, a logo, an accent color and a link on the Portifact bar above your artifacts.
  • You must have the rights to the name, logo and link you show, including any trademarks. A brand may not imitate Portifact or another person or organization.
  • The bar always says that the page is hosted on Portifact, shows the artifact's real address and offers a report link.
  • We may suspend a brand without notice, which brings back the default bar. A brand is not shown while the plan does not include branding, and it is kept until you delete it.

Service tokens and publish keys

  • On a plan with service tokens, a workspace's admins can create tokens that act for the workspace from automation such as continuous integration, with the editor role, the scopes they choose and an expiry of at most 366 days. A token can be limited to the artifacts it created or to artifacts whose publish key starts with a prefix.
  • The workspace is responsible for everything done with its tokens and for keeping them secret. Revoke a token you no longer need or that may have leaked. A token stops when it is revoked or expires, while the plan does not include service tokens, and when the workspace is deleted.
  • A publish key is a name you choose for an artifact, so automation can update the same artifact every time. It is kept with the artifact and freed when the artifact is deleted.
