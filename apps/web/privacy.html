Who is responsible

Portifact is run by Leynier Gutiérrez González, an individual, who decides how your data is used. For any privacy question or request, write to privacy@portifact.com.

In short

  • We keep only what we need to run Portifact: your email, how you sign in, your sessions, what you publish, the workspaces you belong to and the settings you choose.
  • We do not sell your data, show ads, use tracking or analytics cookies, or train AI models on your content.
  • Artifacts are private to you until you share them. In a workspace, its admins and editors also see every artifact and comment.
  • Cloudflare hosts Portifact and processes data on our behalf.

What we collect

  • Account. Your email address and whether it is verified, and the name and profile picture that Google or GitHub share when you sign in with them. For Google and GitHub we keep the stable ID of your account there. We do not keep their access tokens. We also keep the defaults you choose for new artifacts (who can open them, comments and the Portifact bar), whether you want expiry notices and comment notices, and when you last changed the comment notices setting.
  • Plan. The plan of your account or workspace, its seats, when it ends and any limits we agreed with you. Portifact does not process payments, so we hold no card or bank details.
  • Workspaces. Which accounts belong to which workspace, with which role and since when. For an invitation, the invited email address, the role, who invited and when it expires, and only a hash of the link's token. When an artifact moves between your space and a workspace, or between workspaces, we record who moved it and between which spaces.
  • Workspace email domains. When a workspace's admins verify an email domain, we keep the domain, the value of its DNS record and when it was verified and last checked. When someone opens an artifact shared with those domains, we compare the domain of their verified email with the workspace's domains and record nothing new about the visit.
  • Human check. When you create an account, and for your first comments on other people's artifacts, Cloudflare Turnstile checks that you are a person and not an automated program. It looks at signals from your browser and your IP address, and tells us only whether the check passed. We keep a short-lived cookie saying it passed, and nothing else.
  • Terms acceptance. The version of the terms you accepted, including your confirmation that you are 18 or older, and when you accepted it.
  • Sign-in codes. Codes sent to your email are stored only as hashes. To limit abuse, we count requests per email address (using a keyed hash, never the address itself) and per IP address. We also count how many emails we send each day, without any data about the recipients.
  • Undeliverable addresses. When our email provider tells us that mail to an address bounced permanently or was reported as spam, or refuses to send to it for that reason, we stop sending sign-in codes to that address. We record a hash of the address, a masked form of it (its first character and its domain), the reason and the dates, never the full address.
  • Sessions and tokens. Your sessions on app.portifact.com, with the IP address and browser that created them, and the clients, scopes and tokens you approve for the CLI and MCP clients.
  • Content. The files you publish and any sources you upload, with their names, sizes, checksums and versions, and each artifact's settings, including the email addresses you allow to open it and the publish key you give it. Versions record who published them, a person or a service token.
  • Share links. For each link, its label, when it was created, its optional expiry, a hash of its token and of its optional password, and the day it was last opened. We do not identify the people who open a link.
  • Brand. On a plan with branding, the name, color, link and logo file shown on the Portifact bar.
  • Service tokens. For a workspace's tokens, their label, role, scopes, restriction, creation and expiry, who created them, a hash of the secret (never the secret itself) and when each was last used, to the hour.
  • Comments. What you write, who wrote it, when, and the part of the artifact it refers to.
  • Access logs. When you are signed in and open someone else's artifact that its creator shared only with a list of addresses including yours, we record your account, your email address, the artifact and the time, at most once every 30 minutes for each artifact. Only that artifact's creator can see these entries; for an artifact of a workspace, its admins and editors. We do not record each file a page loads, creators' visits to their own artifacts, or anyone who opens a public artifact, one open to every signed-in account or one shared with a workspace or its email domains: those visitors are not identified. When someone opens an artifact that is not public through a share link, we record only that the link was used in a 30-minute window, never who used it.
  • Analytics. On a plan with analytics, we count page views of each artifact per day, and how many of them came with a signed-in visitor. These are counts only: we store no IP address, cookie or other identifier for a view. For artifacts shared with a list of addresses, the creator also sees how many different signed-in readers opened it each day, from the access log.
  • Comment notices. On a plan with comment notices, until a digest is sent we keep which threads have new activity for each person to notify, with counts and the time, never what was written. We also keep the artifacts each person muted.
  • Audit log. We record the changes made to each account's and workspace's artifacts and settings: publishing and the artifact's lifecycle, access changes (with readers as counts and email domains, never full addresses), share links, branding, members, invitations, roles, service tokens, verified email domains and plan changes, with which account or token made each one and when. It never holds comment text, content, full email addresses or IP addresses.
  • Reports. What you tell us when you report abuse, and your email if you give it. To limit abuse of the report form we count reports per IP address.
  • Technical logs. A sample of requests to our servers, used to fix problems and stop abuse. They never include tokens, cookies, sign-in codes, signed URLs or the full text of comments.

How we use it

  • To run Portifact: sign you in, publish and serve your artifacts to the people you allow, and keep your quotas.
  • To keep it secure: limit request rates, detect and stop abuse, and investigate reports.
  • To contact you: send sign-in codes, a notice a few days before your artifacts expire (at most one email a day, listing each artifact with a link to renew it; you can turn it off in Settings), workspace invitations, a notice before a paid plan ends, and other service notices such as a change to these policies. We do not send these emails to addresses recorded as undeliverable.
  • To send comment notices: on artifacts whose plan includes them, we email the creator side and the people who took part in a thread a digest of new comment activity, at most every 10 minutes. It holds the artifact's name, how many threads, replies and resolutions are new and links, never what was written or who wrote it. It is on by default; turn it off in Settings, mute one artifact, or use the one-click unsubscribe link in every digest.
  • To send workspace invitations: an admin can invite an email address that may have no account yet. The email is in the admin's language. Anyone signed in who opens the invitation link sees which address it was sent to, so they can switch accounts, and nothing else unless the address is theirs.
  • To meet legal obligations.

Our legal bases, where the law asks for one, are performing our agreement with you (the terms), our legitimate interest in keeping Portifact secure and working, and complying with the law.

Who we share it with

  • Cloudflare hosts Portifact (Workers, D1 databases, R2 storage), sends our emails through Cloudflare Email Service, which keeps its own list of addresses whose mail bounced or was reported as spam, and runs the human check (Cloudflare Turnstile). It processes data for us under its data processing terms.
  • Cloudflare's DNS resolver answers the daily checks of workspace email domains, which read public DNS records.
  • Google and GitHub, only when you choose to sign in with them. They tell us your email, name and picture.
  • The people you share with. Readers see the artifacts you open to them. If you comment on an artifact, its creator sees your comment and the name shown with it. By default, the other people with access see only the threads they started and the threads the creator started, so they see your comment only in those threads; the creator can let everyone with access see every thread, or let specific people see all of them. A reply shows to everyone who can see its thread. If you open someone else's artifact as described under access logs, its creator sees your email and when you opened it. Visitors' browsers load a brand's logo from the artifact's own address, with no referrer and no cookie.
  • Your workspace. Members of a workspace see each other's name and email, and its admins see the addresses of pending invitations and the verified email domains. Admins and editors see every artifact and comment of the workspace and its access logs. Admins on a plan with the audit log see which member made each change, by display name; people who left are shown as removed members. When an artifact moves to another space, its access log and view counts move with it, so the people who manage the new space see who opened it before the move.
  • People with a share link can read the artifact it opens, without an account.
  • Authorities, when the law requires it or to protect people from serious harm.
  • A company formed to run Portifact, if the service is transferred to one. We will tell you before that happens.

We do not sell or rent personal data.

Your rights and choices

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, export or delete your personal data, and to object to or restrict how we use it. You can always:

  • see and change your artifacts and their access in the dashboard or the CLI;
  • delete any artifact at any time;
  • sign out of the CLI with portifact logout, which revokes its token;
  • turn comment notices off, mute them for one artifact, or unsubscribe from the link in any digest;
  • download a copy of your data (as JSON, with links to your files) and delete your account from the Settings page of the dashboard, or ask us by writing to privacy@portifact.com. The copy includes your plan, your workspaces and role, the invitations addressed to you, your brand, your share links (without their tokens), your comment notice mutes and the audit log of your personal space; the admins of a workspace also get its verified email domains and its service tokens (without their secrets). It never includes other people's invitation addresses.

When an account is deleted, its sessions, tokens and sign-in identities end at once and its artifacts stop being served; within hours we delete its artifacts and files with their access logs, its comments on other people's artifacts, and the access log entries of its visits to other people's artifacts. A comment that others replied to is replaced by a "message deleted" marker. If you are the only admin of a workspace, make another member an admin before deleting your account. What you published in a workspace stays with the workspace, and the workspace's audit log shows you as a removed member by your internal ID, which names nobody once your account is gone, until each event expires after 90 days. We keep only a minimal record of the account's internal ID and of its artifact IDs, so those addresses are never given to anyone else, and we erase your email from any abuse report you sent. We answer requests within 30 days and do not charge for them. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Features not yet available

Webhooks and custom domains are built but are not available yet, so today we keep none of the data below. We will announce when each one becomes available. From then on:

  • Webhooks. On a plan with webhooks, the admins of an account or workspace can have Portifact send events about its artifacts to an HTTPS address they choose, such as their own server, Slack or Discord. An event holds the artifact's name and links, the event type, times, counts, version numbers, the access mode and the display name of the admin or editor who acted, never comment text, quotes or readers' email addresses. Once sent, that data is under the responsibility of whoever chose the address. We keep the address and its signing secret encrypted, its host, the chosen events, pending events (IDs and counts, deleted once delivered) and a delivery log of 7 days (time, outcome and HTTP status). They are deleted with the webhook or the account, and listed without the address or secret in the data export.
  • Custom domains. For a custom domain we keep the hostname, the artifact and owner it serves, the DNS verification value and when it was verified and checked. Hostnames are shared with Cloudflare to issue certificates, are part of the data export and are deleted when the domain is removed.

Security

Your account and the API live on portifact.com, and artifacts are served from their own addresses on portifact.dev, so a published page cannot act on your account. Cookies are restricted to the address that set them, except the theme and language preferences, which portifact.com and app.portifact.com share and which never reach portifact.dev; sign-in codes and tokens are never logged, and storage is reachable only through Portifact's access checks. The details are in our security documentation. No system is perfectly secure; if we learn of a breach that affects your data, we will tell you as the law requires.

Children

Portifact is only for people 18 or older. If we learn that an account belongs to someone younger, we close it and delete its data. If you believe this is the case, write to privacy@portifact.com.
