Who is responsible

Portifact is run by Leynier Gutiérrez González, an individual, who decides how your data is used. For any privacy question or request, write to privacy@portifact.com.

In short

  • We keep only what we need to run Portifact: your email, how you sign in, your sessions, what you publish and the settings you choose.
  • We do not sell your data, show ads, use tracking or analytics cookies, or train AI models on your content.
  • Artifacts are private to you until you share them.
  • Cloudflare hosts Portifact and processes data on our behalf.

What we collect

  • Account. Your email address and whether it is verified, and the name and profile picture that Google or GitHub share when you sign in with them. For Google and GitHub we keep the stable ID of your account there. We do not keep their access tokens. We also keep the defaults you choose for new artifacts (who can open them, comments and the Portifact bar) and whether you want expiry notices.
  • Human check. When you create an account, and for your first comments on other people's artifacts, Cloudflare Turnstile checks that you are a person and not an automated program. It looks at signals from your browser and your IP address, and tells us only whether the check passed. We keep a short-lived cookie saying it passed, and nothing else.
  • Terms acceptance. The version of the terms you accepted, including your confirmation that you are 18 or older, and when you accepted it.
  • Sign-in codes. Codes sent to your email are stored only as hashes. To limit abuse, we count requests per email address (using a keyed hash, never the address itself) and per IP address. We also count how many emails we send each day, without any data about the recipients.
  • Undeliverable addresses. When our email provider tells us that mail to an address bounced permanently or was reported as spam, or refuses to send to it for that reason, we stop sending sign-in codes to that address. We record a hash of the address, a masked form of it (its first character and its domain), the reason and the dates, never the full address.
  • Sessions and tokens. Your sessions on app.portifact.com, with the IP address and browser that created them, and the clients, scopes and tokens you approve for the CLI and MCP clients.
  • Content. The files you publish and any sources you upload, with their names, sizes, checksums and versions, and each artifact's settings, including the email addresses you allow to open it.
  • Comments. When comments are available, what you write, who wrote it, when, and the part of the artifact it refers to.
  • Access logs. When you are signed in and open someone else's artifact that its creator shared only with a list of addresses including yours, we record your account, your email address, the artifact and the time, at most once every 30 minutes for each artifact. Only that artifact's creator can see these entries. We do not record each file a page loads, creators' visits to their own artifacts, or anyone who opens a public artifact or one open to every signed-in account: those visitors are not identified.
  • Reports. What you tell us when you report abuse, and your email if you give it. To limit abuse of the report form we count reports per IP address.
  • Technical logs. A sample of requests to our servers, used to fix problems and stop abuse. They never include tokens, cookies, sign-in codes, signed URLs or the full text of comments.

How we use it

  • To run Portifact: sign you in, publish and serve your artifacts to the people you allow, and keep your quotas.
  • To keep it secure: limit request rates, detect and stop abuse, and investigate reports.
  • To contact you: send sign-in codes, a notice a few days before your artifacts expire (at most one email a day, listing each artifact with a link to renew it; you can turn it off in Settings), and, later, other service notices such as a change to these policies. We do not send these notices to addresses recorded as undeliverable.
  • To meet legal obligations.

Our legal bases, where the law asks for one, are performing our agreement with you (the terms), our legitimate interest in keeping Portifact secure and working, and complying with the law.

Who we share it with

  • Cloudflare hosts Portifact (Workers, D1 databases, R2 storage), sends our emails through Cloudflare Email Service, which keeps its own list of addresses whose mail bounced or was reported as spam, and runs the human check (Cloudflare Turnstile). It processes data for us under its data processing terms.
  • Google and GitHub, only when you choose to sign in with them. They tell us your email, name and picture.
  • The people you share with. Readers see the artifacts you open to them. If you comment on an artifact, its creator sees your comment and the name shown with it. By default, the other people with access see only the threads they started and the threads the creator started, so they see your comment only in those threads; the creator can let everyone with access see every thread, or let specific people see all of them. A reply shows to everyone who can see its thread. If you open someone else's artifact as described under access logs, its creator sees your email and when you opened it.
  • Authorities, when the law requires it or to protect people from serious harm.
  • A company formed to run Portifact, if the service is transferred to one. We will tell you before that happens.

We do not sell or rent personal data.

Where data is stored

Data is stored and processed by Cloudflare, which runs servers in many countries, including the United States. By using Portifact, your data may be processed outside the country where you live.

Your rights and choices

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, export or delete your personal data, and to object to or restrict how we use it. You can always:

  • see and change your artifacts and their access in the dashboard or the CLI;
  • delete any artifact at any time;
  • sign out of the CLI with portifact logout, which revokes its token;
  • download a copy of your data (as JSON, with links to your files) and delete your account from the Settings page of the dashboard, or ask us by writing to privacy@portifact.com.

When an account is deleted, its sessions, tokens and sign-in identities end at once and its artifacts stop being served; within hours we delete its artifacts and files with their access logs, its comments on other people's artifacts, and the access log entries of its visits to other people's artifacts. A comment that others replied to is replaced by a "message deleted" marker. We keep only a minimal record of the account's internal ID and of its artifact IDs, so those addresses are never given to anyone else, and we erase your email from any abuse report you sent. We answer requests within 30 days and do not charge for them. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Security

Your account and the API live on portifact.com, and artifacts are served from their own addresses on portifact.dev, so a published page cannot act on your account. Cookies are restricted to the address that set them, sign-in codes and tokens are never logged, and storage is reachable only through Portifact's access checks. The details are in our security documentation. No system is perfectly secure; if we learn of a breach that affects your data, we will tell you as the law requires.

Children

Portifact is only for people 18 or older. If we learn that an account belongs to someone younger, we close it and delete its data. If you believe this is the case, write to privacy@portifact.com.

Changes to this policy
