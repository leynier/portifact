Who is responsible

Portifact is run by Leynier Gutiérrez González, an individual, who runs the service remotely from North America. For any privacy question, request or complaint, write to privacy@portifact.com.

Who decides how data is used depends on the activity, not on the kind of space:

  • We are the controller for account administration, sign-in, billing records, the security of the platform and the handling of abuse reports, also when those records concern a workspace. We also decide about the content and activity in a personal space used for one's own purposes.
  • The customer is the controller, or a processor acting with its controller's authorization, for the content and processing it directs: in a workspace, the organization or person that holds it, and in a personal space used for work, its holder. That covers artifacts, the comments and reviews on them, their access logs and analytics, the audit log, groups, invitations, verified domains and policies. We process that data for the customer, as its processor, under the Data Processing Addendum. If your question is about a workspace's data, ask its admins first. We help them answer, and we answer you ourselves where the law requires it.

Joining a workspace means its admins and editors can see every artifact of the workspace, every comment on them and who opened them, and its admins can read in the audit log what each member did. The workspace's customer must tell its members and the people it invites what it sees and why, before it invites, lists or provisions them, and must have a lawful basis for it, for example as an employer.

In short

  • We keep what we need to run Portifact: your email, how you sign in, your sessions, what you publish and the settings you choose.
  • We do not sell your data, share it for advertising, show ads, use advertising or analytics trackers, or train AI models on your content.
  • Artifacts are private to you until you share them; in a workspace, its admins and editors also see them.
  • Cloudflare hosts Portifact and processes data on our behalf. Messages you send us are forwarded to the operator's mailbox, hosted by Google.

Where we get data from

  • From you, when you sign up, publish, comment, review, change settings, write to us or ask for a paid plan.
  • From the sign-in provider you choose: Google or GitHub tell us your email address, name and picture.
  • From other people: the admins of a workspace and, once available, its directory, who add you or invite you; people who list your address as a reader or a reviewer of an artifact; and people who report content, which may mention you.
  • From your browser and network: your IP address and browser, for sessions, rate limits and the human check.

To have an account you must give us a working email address (directly or through Google or GitHub), accept the terms and confirm you are 18 or older. Without them we cannot create an account; you can still open public artifacts and artifacts shared with a link. Everything else, such as a name, settings or content, is up to you.

What we collect

About your account

  • Account. Your email address and whether it is verified, and the name and profile picture that Google or GitHub share when you sign in with them. For Google and GitHub we keep the stable ID of your account there. We do not keep their access tokens. We also keep your settings: the defaults for new artifacts (who can open them, comments and the Portifact bar), your language, whether you want expiry notices and comment and review emails, and the artifacts you muted.
  • Human check. When you create an account, for your first comments on other people's artifacts, and after repeated wrong passwords on a share link, Cloudflare Turnstile checks that you are a person. It processes browser and network signals, such as your IP address and browser, for security. Cloudflare acts for us to protect this service, and decides on its own how it uses those signals to improve Turnstile, as its Turnstile privacy notice explains. We learn only whether the check passed, and keep a short-lived cookie saying so.
  • Terms acceptance. Every version of the terms you accepted, including your confirmation that you are 18 or older, when you accepted it, and how: by signing up, by signing in below the notice, or with "Agree and continue". For acceptances made before we kept this history, we have only the version you held and when you accepted it.
  • Sign-in codes. We store the email address that asked for a code with a hash of the code, never the code itself, also before an account exists. To limit abuse, we count requests per email address (using a keyed hash, never the address itself) and per IP address. We also count how many emails we send each day, without any data about the recipients.
  • Undeliverable addresses. When our email provider tells us that mail to an address bounced permanently or was reported as spam, or refuses to send to it for that reason, we stop sending email to that address. We record a hash of the address, a masked form of it (its first character and its domain), the reason and the dates, never the full address.
  • Sessions and tokens. Your sessions on app.portifact.com, with the IP address and browser that created them, when you signed in and whether you used an email code, Google or GitHub, and the clients, scopes and tokens you approve for the CLI and MCP clients, each with the time and method of the sign-in that approved it, and the records of each authorization.
  • App registrations. When an app (such as an MCP client) registers with Portifact, we store its contact addresses, name, web addresses and registration details. Signed-in people who know its app ID can read its contact addresses and public details. Registrations, including those made without a Portifact account, have no automatic expiry.
  • Plan and billing. Your space's plan, its seats, dates and limits, and, when a plan ends or is lowered, what the 60 days hold and which reminders were sent. When you ask for a paid plan, the emails in which we agree on it, the written confirmation and the record of what was paid. Payment happens outside Portifact, by the method named in the confirmation; that payment provider handles your payment details under its own terms, and we keep only what was agreed and paid.

About what you publish and share

  • Content. The files you publish and any sources you upload, with their names, sizes, checksums and versions, and each artifact's settings, including the email addresses you allow to open it, its publish key and who created it (a person or a service token). Source uploads from the CLI can also include the Git repository address, branch and commit, and whether there were uncommitted changes; the CLI removes credentials from the repository address, and this stays with the version.
  • Uploads and quotas. Upload attempts and quota records linked to the space: artifact and version IDs, upload status, file and byte counts, publication events and their dates. This history stays until the account or workspace is deleted.
  • Search index. For spaces whose plan includes content search, the text of their active artifacts' current versions (Markdown and text documents, and the visible text of HTML pages, up to set limits), kept in the same database as the artifacts. Only the people who manage the space, and its service tokens within their reach, can search it. Queries are not stored or logged.
  • Comments. What you write, who wrote it, when, and the part of the artifact it refers to. We also keep a history of comment actions (created, edited, deleted, hidden, resolved), with the account, thread and message IDs, the action and its time, but no comment text. This history stays with the artifact after a comment or its author's account is deleted, and deleted-message markers can keep the author's internal ID. To handle repeated requests safely, we briefly keep copies of some responses, which can include comment text and author details; they can outlast a deleted comment and are cleaned up after 24 hours.
  • Approval reviews. When the people who manage an artifact ask named people to review it, the reviewers' email addresses, and each reviewer's verdict (approve or request changes), optional note, the version and when it was given.
  • Access logs. When you are signed in and open someone else's artifact that is shared only with listed people or groups, including you, we record your account, your email address, the artifact and the time, at most once per person and artifact in each fixed 30-minute window. Only the people who manage that artifact can see these entries. We do not record each file a page loads, visits by the people who manage the artifact, or anyone who opens a public artifact or one open to every signed-in account, a workspace or an email domain: those visits are not recorded in the access log.
  • Viewing and review authorizations. Separately from access logs, we keep short-lived authorization records that link your account and sign-in session to the artifact you open or review, with their creation and expiry times and, for reviews, the version. Background cleanup removes expired ones.
  • Membership removals. When an admin removes a member from a workspace, we record the account, the workspace and the time, until the person is invited again or the account or workspace is deleted.
  • Analytics. For spaces whose plan includes analytics, how many times each artifact's pages were viewed per day and how many of those views came with a signed-in visitor, as counts that identify nobody. We store no IP address, cookie or identifier for a view.
  • Share links. For each link, its label, when it was created and by whom, its optional expiry, a hash of the link's token and of its optional password, and the day it was last opened. People who open an artifact through a link are not identified: the access log records only that the link was used in a 30-minute window, and nothing about the visitor.
  • Branding. The name, color, link and logo a space shows on the Portifact bar. Visitors' browsers load the logo from the artifact's own address, with no referrer; the logo sets no cookie and nothing is recorded about who loads it.
  • Notice bookkeeping. For comment emails, we keep recipient and thread IDs, activity counts and dates. Scheduling moves this information into an email job. The worker removes the job when it takes it, then checks whether the email may still be sent; a send failure may queue it again. For review emails, which person was emailed about which artifact version and when.

About workspaces

  • Memberships. Which account belongs to which workspace, with which role, since when, and in which groups. Members of a workspace see each other's name and email address. Admins see each group's members; editors see only group names and how many members each has.
  • Invitations. The invited email address, the role, who invited and when it expires, and a hash of the link's token. Anyone signed in who opens an invitation link sees which address it was sent to, and nothing else unless it is theirs.
  • Verified email domains. The domains a workspace verified, the value of their DNS record, and when they were verified and last checked. We check them daily through Cloudflare's DNS resolver. When an artifact is shared with a workspace's email domains, we compare the domain of your verified email with them when you open it.
  • Policies. A workspace's policies hold email domains and network ranges, never people's addresses.
  • Service tokens and publish keys. For each token, its label, role, scopes, restriction, creation and expiry, who created it, a hash of the secret (never the secret) and when it was last used, written at most once an hour. Versions and lifecycle events record a token as their author.
  • Moves. When an artifact moves to another space, who moved it and between which spaces. Its access log and view counters move with it, so the people who manage the new space see who opened it before the move.
  • Audit log. For every space, a record of the changes made to it (publishing and lifecycle, access and readers as counts and email domains, share links, branding, members, invitations, roles, groups, service tokens, policies, plans and reviews), with who made each one and when. It names people by display name and an internal ID, which is pseudonymous rather than anonymous because we can link it to an account, and never holds comment text, content, full email addresses or IP addresses. On a plan with the audit log, a workspace's admins read it, and on Enterprise they can download it as CSV or JSON Lines; starting a download is itself recorded. Once downloaded, the file is the workspace's to protect.

Other

  • Reports. What you tell us when you report abuse, and your email if you give it. To limit abuse of the report form we count reports per IP address.
  • Emails you send us. Messages to our addresses, which Cloudflare Email Routing forwards to the operator's mailbox, hosted by Google, kept as long as needed to handle them.
  • Technical logs. Our own log lines for a sample of requests, used to fix problems and stop abuse. They hold IDs, counts and error labels, and never artifact names, comment text, email addresses, URL paths, tokens, cookies, sign-in codes or signed URLs. The platform's logs of each request's address are off for the website, the dashboard and artifacts.
  • Lifecycle and moderation records. A history of what happened to each artifact (published, archived, restored, deleted and similar) and of suspensions, with account and artifact IDs, actions, dates, reasons and the operator's notes. These records stay after the artifact or account is deleted and have no automatic deletion period today.
  • Storage checks. Checks of our storage keep object identifiers (which contain the space's ID and a hash of the content), what was found and dates, until a later check clears them. Background scans keep progress markers, replaced as scanning continues.
  • Traffic protection. When the service is under unusual load, we count requests per visitor IP address for a few minutes, in memory only, to slow down floods. Your artifacts' monthly traffic is counted per space, without any data about visitors.

Emails we send

  • You can turn these off. Expiry notices, a few days before your artifacts expire, at most one a day (in Settings). Comment emails, on artifacts whose space's plan includes them, a digest of new comment activity on artifacts you manage or threads you took part in, at most every 10 minutes, with the artifact's name, counts and links and never what was written or who wrote it (in Settings, per artifact with a mute, or with the one-click unsubscribe link in every one). Review emails, on artifacts whose space's plan includes them, when your review is requested (one email per version) and, for the people who manage the artifact, when it is approved or changes are requested, at most 20 a day to one person (with the same setting and link as comment emails).
  • You cannot turn these off, because each is tied to something you asked for or to your data: sign-in codes; invitations to a workspace; reminders when a plan ends or is lowered and the end of its 60 days would delete your space's data (we try to send them, but delivery is not guaranteed, so the dashboard shows the dates too); and, for a workspace's admins, notices before its retention policies take effect.
  • Automated service emails are suppressed while an address is recorded as undeliverable. We handle notices required by law or contract separately and use an appropriate available contact method. We send no marketing email.
  • Review emails go only to people with a Portifact account and a standing relationship to the artifact (the people who manage it, its listed readers or groups, or a verified domain it is shared with), never to an address without an account, never because a service token asked, and never to someone who can open it only because it is public.

Where data is stored and transfers

  • The standard service has no contractual restriction on where data is stored. Today, our databases and the files of artifacts are stored by Cloudflare in its western North America location, which Cloudflare chose when they were created and does not guarantee. Cloudflare runs our code in the data center closest to each request, anywhere in its global network, and our background jobs pass through its queues, which can run anywhere in that network. Keeping data in the EU is not offered yet.
  • Data can move through the following routes. The safeguard depends on the processing and the destination. The Data Processing Addendum's transfer clauses apply only within their stated scope and after the required details are completed. Cloudflare's certification covers qualifying transfers to Cloudflare, not the operator or every other recipient:
    1. From you or a customer to Portifact. Where the law requires a safeguard for data a customer sends us, our Data Processing Addendum includes the EU standard contractual clauses, with the UK and Swiss terms.
    2. The operator's remote administration. The operator runs the service remotely from North America, outside the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom. We do not claim an adequacy decision for the operator's location. The Data Processing Addendum's clauses cover the operator's access where that access forms part of a transfer they protect.
    3. From Portifact to its providers. Cloudflare is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, its UK Extension and the Swiss-US Data Privacy Framework, which covers transfers to Cloudflare, and its data processing addendum includes the standard contractual clauses.
  • To get a copy of the safeguards that apply to your data, write to privacy@portifact.com.

When a plan ends or is lowered

When a space's paid plan ends, is removed or is lowered, the earlier plan's rules for how long things are kept keep applying for 60 days: how many previous versions are kept and for how long, how long access log entries, analytics counts and audit log events are kept, and how long an artifact archived during those days stays in the archive. The data those rules hold stays readable and downloadable, and pinned artifacts stay pinned. Data still ages under those rules as usual, and every other limit and feature follows the new plan at once; share links and branding pause only if the new plan does not include them. Archive deletion dates already set do not move.

When the end of those 60 days would delete held data, we try to email the account of a personal space, or each admin of a workspace, when they start, 14 days before they end and 3 days before they end, with how many items of each kind will go (counts and dates, never artifact names) and links to download them. Delivery is not guaranteed. The holder of a personal space and a workspace's admins can see the dates on the Plan and "Download your data" pages, and admins and editors also see a notice on other pages, which they can hide for the day. When the 60 days end, what the current plan does not keep is deleted, and pinned artifacts are unpinned and get the usual 30 days online, unless the current plan still includes pinning. Data still expires under the earlier rules during the 60 days, so export each item before its retention ends, with the "Download your data" page or portifact export; the export register says what each covers, and you can ask privacy@portifact.com for anything they do not.

Your rights and choices

Wherever you live, you can ask us to give you access to your personal data, to correct it, to delete it, to restrict or object to how we use it, and to give you a copy you can take elsewhere. Some laws call these the rights of access, rectification, cancellation and objection. Which rights apply, and their limits, depend on the law that governs the processing. You can always:

  • see and change your artifacts and their access in the dashboard or the CLI;
  • delete any artifact you manage at any time;
  • sign out of the CLI with portifact logout, which revokes its token, and revoke apps from the Apps page;
  • download a copy of your account data from the "Download your data" page of the dashboard, which includes your account, the history of the terms versions you accepted, settings, artifacts, comments elsewhere, review verdicts, memberships and the invitations addressed to you; the same page, and portifact export, download what a space holds that your role and plan allow: retained artifact files and versions, access logs, the audit log, and each artifact's records (its settings and access rules, share link settings, approval review, analytics, comment threads you can see and, for the people who manage the artifact, the history of comment actions without text). For a workspace's admins, the page's space records also hold its members, groups, invitations, email domains, policies, branding, service tokens and integrations, never their secrets. The export register lists each tool with its format and limits. For data no tool covers, write to privacy@portifact.com;
  • delete your account from the Settings page of the dashboard, or ask us by writing to privacy@portifact.com.

Deleting your account deletes your account data and your personal space. Leaving a workspace or being removed does not delete its content. Deleting your account does not delete the workspace or the artifacts you published in or moved to it, which the workspace's customer continues to control (ask its admins about that data), but it does remove your comments and review verdicts on them; some comments are replaced by a "message deleted" marker, as described below. This does not transfer copyright or other ownership rights. When an account is deleted, its sessions, tokens and sign-in identities end at once and its artifacts stop being served; a background job then deletes its artifacts and files with their access logs, its comments and review verdicts on other people's artifacts, and the access log entries of its visits to other people's artifacts. A comment that others replied to is replaced by a "message deleted" marker. An address named on a review request stays on that request as part of the artifact's list. Audit events about you stay under the audit log's retention, and the comment-action, lifecycle and moderation records described above remain; directory copies are described under "Features not yet available". We keep a record of the account's internal ID and of its artifact IDs, so those addresses are never given to anyone else, and we erase your email from ordinary abuse report records, except where we must preserve it for a legal obligation; preserved records are kept separately and deleted when that obligation ends.

Write to privacy@portifact.com to use your rights or to ask for the transfer safeguards. We answer within one calendar month. If a request is complex or there are many, the law may let us take longer; we then tell you within that month why and how long. We do not charge for requests. For data a workspace holds, we pass your request to its admins and help them answer.

Security

Your account and the API live on portifact.com, and artifacts are served from their own addresses on portifact.dev, so a published page cannot act on your account. Cookies are restricted to the address that set them, except the theme and language preferences, which portifact.com and app.portifact.com share and which never reach portifact.dev; sign-in codes, app and service tokens, share link tokens and passwords are stored only as hashes, credentials we must use again are encrypted, browser session identifiers and short-lived device login codes are stored as issued in the access-controlled database, sign-in codes and tokens are never logged, and storage is reachable only through Portifact's access checks. The details are in our security documentation. No system is perfectly secure. If we learn of a breach that affects data we control, we notify the authorities and the people affected when the law requires it. If it affects data we process for a customer, we tell the customer without undue delay, and the customer decides how to notify its people.

Children

Portifact is only for people 18 or older. If we learn that an account belongs to someone younger, we close it and delete its data. If you believe this is the case, write to privacy@portifact.com.

Features not yet available

These features are built but not available yet. A description here does not mean the feature is provided or that any processing for it has begun, except the membership removal records described under "What we collect", which are already kept. Before we make one available, we check that what was built matches this description and give any new notice the law requires. The services a customer connects act under their own terms, and we remain responsible for what we send them on the customer's instruction. When each one becomes available, this is the data it uses:

  • Webhooks. The people who manage a space can have Portifact send events about its artifacts to an HTTPS address they choose: the artifact's name and links, event types, times, counts, version numbers, the access mode, review statuses and counts, and the display name of the member who acted; never comment text, quotes, notes, names of reviewers or readers' email addresses. Once sent, that data is the space's responsibility. We keep the destination encrypted with only its host in clear, the chosen events, pending events (IDs and counts, deleted when delivered) and a 7-day delivery log of times, outcomes and status codes.
  • Custom domains. We keep the hostname, the artifact and space it serves, the verification value and when it was verified and checked. Hostnames are shared with Cloudflare to issue certificates, and are part of the export. Removed domains are deleted.
  • Issue integrations. A space's admins can connect GitHub, Linear or Jira Cloud with a credential of their own (for Jira, an API token with its Atlassian account's email address). We keep it encrypted, with the account name the service reports and the repositories, teams or projects allowed, and delete it when the connection is removed. When the people who manage an artifact create an issue from a comment thread, we send that service a title they write and, as they choose, a link to the thread, the artifact's name, the quoted text of the thread's anchor and the thread's first message. Those words, which may be someone else's, stay at the service under its own terms, even if the comment is later deleted here. Apart from the anchor quotation you choose to include, we do not send the artifact's content. We do not send readers' email addresses, other messages or review notes. We keep, with the thread, the issue's service, number and link, and record in the audit log which parts were sent, never the text. These services are destinations the workspace chooses, not our processors.
  • Slack link previews. A workspace's admins can connect a Slack app they create. We keep its bot token and signing secret encrypted, and the Slack workspace's name and ID. Slack sends us events about that app (its verification, uninstalls and links posted to Portifact's domains, with the channel, the poster's Slack user ID and the message's identifiers), which we use only to answer and never store or log. When a posted link is to one of the workspace's own public artifacts, we send Slack the artifact's name and type, never its content, and Slack keeps that preview in the message even after the artifact stops being public.
  • Single sign-on. A workspace's identity provider becomes a source of data: at each sign-in we receive an ID token from it and keep only the provider's stable identifier for you (linked to your account), your email address and your name, never the provider's tokens. We keep the provider's settings (issuer, client ID, the client secret encrypted, the domains it serves) as the workspace's data, record its setup and the people who join through it in the workspace's audit log, and delete the link with your account or when the provider is removed.
  • Directory provisioning (SCIM). For each person a workspace's directory provisions, we keep the email address, the directory's identifier for them, the names it sent, whether it marked them active and its groups, for that workspace only. For someone without an account, a pending membership holds the address until they sign in, or for 90 days after the directory last wrote them. A person whose existing account the directory adds is told by email, which cannot be turned off. A directory does not create a personal account or change its personal content or settings, but removing a person ends that account's sessions across Portifact, including for its personal space. Deleting a person's account keeps the directory's copy without the link to the account, as the workspace's own record.
  • Audit streams. An Enterprise workspace's admins can send its audit log continuously to an HTTPS endpoint they run: the same content the audit log shows, never comment text, content, credentials, full email addresses or IP addresses. We keep the endpoint's host in clear, its address and signing secret encrypted, and a 7-day delivery log with no content.
  • GitHub Actions federation. When a workspace trusts a GitHub repository, we receive ID tokens that GitHub signs for its workflow runs, and keep, for the audit log, the repository's numeric ID and name, the run's ID and attempt and the triggering event, and a hash of each token's ID until shortly after the token expires. We read and discard the other claims, including who started the run.
  • Data kept in the EU. For a workspace created in the EU, these are stored in Cloudflare's EU jurisdiction: its artifacts' files, versions and details, readers and listed groups, comments, approval reviews, share links, branding, the search index, the access log and analytics counts, usage and traffic counters, webhooks, integration connections, audit streams, and the audit events about its artifacts and their settings. These stay in our main databases, outside the EU: accounts, sessions and sign-in identities, the workspace's name and slug, memberships, groups and their members, invitations, verified email domains, policies, plan, service tokens, single sign-on and directory settings and copies, custom domain claims, the directory of artifact IDs and regions, abuse reports, and the audit events about members, invitations, groups, tokens, domains and the plan. Storing data in the EU is not a promise that it is processed or administered only in the EU: Cloudflare runs our code close to each request, the operator administers the service from North America, and emails go through Cloudflare Email Service. Notices about an EU workspace carry counts, dates and links, never artifact names, comment text or other people's addresses; invitations and directory emails name the workspace, and invitations also name the inviting admin. Webhooks, integrations and audit streams go where the workspace points them, which may be outside the EU.
