# 0022 Plan grace

- Status: accepted; the rule, storage, scans, read gates, operator control, audit events, the usage body's grace and the Plan page's grace notice shipped first (v0.35.0); the "Download your data" page, `portifact export` and the notice on every page of the space shipped second; the three grace emails ship in a third change
- Date: 2026-11-11
- Feature: premium plan acceptance criterion "downgrading a plan deletes no data and breaks no reads, exports, deletions or restores"; the maintainer's decision: 60 days of grace, the ability to download the data, then the current pruning

## Question

When an owner's entitlements drop (a plan's `valid_until` passes, the operator runs `remove-plan`, or `set-plan` lowers the plan, its seats or its overrides), what happens to its data today, and how do we keep everything the old plan kept, readable and downloadable, for 60 days, without adding a read to any request path and without a new deletion path?

## Decision in short

- **60 days, one constant.** `PLAN_GRACE_DAYS` in `packages/contracts/src/plans.ts`, next to the plans. A drop starts a grace; it lasts 60 days from the drop.
- **What a grace holds.** Retention follows the previous plan, everything else the current one. The retention limits are `GRACE_LIMITS`: `archiveMonths`, `retainedPreviousVersions`, `previousVersionRetentionDays`, `accessLogRetentionDays`, `auditRetentionDays`. During a grace `entitlements` returns, in `limits`, the highest of the current plan's value and each running hold's, so every existing reader, SQL condition and scan that keeps or lists data by those limits (`retention.ts`'s one condition, `pruneVersions`, `retentionExpiry`/`applyOwnerRetention`, the audit sweep and `auditPruneCutoff`, `purgeDate` at archive time) holds what the old plan kept without a new check. `features` stays the current plan's, so new work is refused exactly as before (publishing over the quota, new pins, share links, webhooks, searching, policies). `entitlements` also returns `grace`: its end, the highest plan held, the held limits and the paid features the old plan granted that the current one does not.
- **Reads of held data.** `requireHeldFeature`/`holdsFeature` (`packages/domain/src/plans.ts`) open a gate when the plan grants the feature or a running grace holds it. Used only by reads of held data: the analytics read, the audit log and its export, comparing retained versions, the version list's `versionDiff` flag, and `pinsLapse` (pinned artifacts stay pinned while a grace holds `pin`). Writes and new work keep `requireFeature`. Every line of both `GATED` tables is classified (`IN_GRACE` in `apps/app/test/plan-gates.test.ts`, `inGrace` in `packages/cli/src/plan-gates.test.ts`): the app table runs each line for an owner whose plan ended the day before and expects held lines open and the rest refused, and fails for a line nobody classified.
- **Rollback is allowed in a grace.** It is a write, but AGENTS.md already forbids putting rollback behind a plan limit, it uses objects already stored and counted, and it is how an owner gets a held version back as current. It goes through the retention condition, so it reaches exactly the versions the grace still holds.
- **Storage.** `owner_plans.grace` (migration `0060_owner_plan_grace`), a JSON list of holds (`GraceSlot`: plan, reason, from, until, the five limits, the features, for a natural expiry the `valid_until` it came from, and for a folded hold how many it holds). The column is plain text in the schema and only `readGrace` parses it, so no reader can throw on its value. A slot is bounded: `from` and `until` are valid dates, `until - from` is at most the grace length, and each limit is at most `RETENTION_LIMIT_MAX` (seven years for every length of time, 84 archive months, 10,000 previous versions); overrides are held to the same bounds, so a hold made from any valid plan is within them, and nothing can be held forever or break a date. On the same global row every reader already reads, so no join or read is added; the content Worker's delivery lookup and `actorAndPlanOn` select one more column of the row they already read. No payment columns.
  - Natural expiry needs no write: `entitlements` derives the hold from `valid_until` itself for 60 days. The grace scan (`maintainPlanGraces`) then stores it once, so its start is recorded once, and the hold reads the same before and after.
  - `set-plan` reads the row, decides with `graceAfterChange` (running holds, plus one for what the outgoing plan kept unless the new plan covers it; holds the new plan covers end early), and writes the row with its holds in one batch conditioned on the row it read (a change landing first makes it decide again, three times, then exit asking to run it again).
  - `remove-plan` keeps the row as `free` with its holds instead of deleting it; the grace scan deletes that row when its last hold ends, so the owner is back to "no row". With nothing held (the plan kept nothing above free) it deletes the row as before.
- **A second drop.** Each drop is its own hold with its own end: a second drop during a grace adds a hold for what the intermediate plan kept, from the second drop, and leaves the first unchanged, so the first is neither extended nor cut short. Past `PLAN_GRACE_MAX_SLOTS` (8) running holds, the two ending first are folded into the later one, which keeps the highest of both until its own end and records how many holds it holds (`folded`); the earlier one is recorded as ended with reason `folded`. The bound: a fold can lengthen a value's hold by at most the grace length after the newest drop folded in, never shorten it; holding a value forever would take a real drop at least every 60 days indefinitely. `plan <owner>` shows a folded hold as `(folded: N holds)`. `plan-grace.test.ts` (domain) checks these with examples and property tests.
- **One start and one end per hold.** Whoever stores a hold first records its start in the same batch: the operator's change records every live hold it stores for the first time (the outgoing plan's, a natural expiry's the scan had not stored yet with reason `expired`, the stand-in for an unreadable grace with reason `unreadable`), and the scan records a natural expiry's when it stores it. Every hold then ends once: `renewed`, `operator`, `expired` or `folded`. Tested over the orderings (scan first, `remove-plan` first, a lowering first, `--no-grace` first, two scans).
- **Raising or renewing.** A grant that covers a hold (every held limit and feature) ends it early with nothing lost; a partial raise keeps the rest of the hold.
- **The access log's expiry.** `retentionExpiry` dates each row from the steps of retention in force over time: the plan's own until 60 days after its `valid_until`, each hold until its end, then the free plan's. So a row is never dated before the hold that keeps it ends, and no job is needed when a hold ends: the prune deletes it on its date. `applyOwnerRetention`'s SQL is the same steps as a `case`. A plan with an end now keeps its rows 60 days past it (it capped at `valid_until` before).
- **Grace's end.** Today's behavior through the existing scans, no new deletion path: once the held values are no longer in `limits`, `pruneVersions`, `pruneAccessEvents`, the audit sweep and `unpinLapsedPlans` do what they did on the day of the drop before this change. An owner above the new plan's quotas keeps everything it has (the limit columns already hold the larger of plan and usage); only new work is refused. Nothing is deleted to fit a quota.
- **What is not held.** The search index (derived; dropped at the next scan and rebuilt by `scheduleSearchIndexing` on a grant, so nothing is lost), custom domains (their own 7-day downgrade rule, unchanged), and every feature that is new work (share links opening, webhooks, streams, integrations, `domain` mode and groups admitting, branding shown). Those are refused at once, as before, and come back with the plan.
- **Operator.** `set-plan` and `remove-plan` start a grace by default; `--no-grace` ends every running hold and starts none (abuse), recorded as the operator. `plan <owner>` shows the grace, its end, what it holds and each hold. Suspensions and account or workspace deletion are unaffected: deletion deletes the row with everything else.
- **Audit.** `plan.grace_started` (`reason`: `lowered`, `removed`, `expired`) and `plan.grace_ended` (`renewed`, `operator`, `expired`), details limited to the reason, the plan held and the end date, global kinds (they describe the global plan row). The operator's are in the change's own batch before the row's write, on the row it read; the scan writes a natural expiry's start and every expired or folded end in its own batch per owner. Every write of the scan (its events, the grace rewrite and the delete of a removed plan's row) is conditioned on the exact row it read: the same `updated_at` (every operator write moves it) and the same grace text, so an operator's change between the scan's read and its batch (other overrides, seats or note) is never overwritten or deleted; the next run decides from the new row. A removed plan's row is deleted only when it holds nothing and is still exactly what removal leaves (free, one seat, no end, no overrides). The other plan scans: `alignPlanLimits` and `unpinLapsedPlans` condition on `updated_at` with one database and, with a separate regional database, cannot condition on the global row (documented windows: a grant's own regional half rewrites the columns, and an unpinned artifact keeps a 30-day expiry and can be pinned again); `pruneVersions` conditions on `updated_at` with one database and re-reads the plan right before each delete with a separate one. The grace scan does not move `updated_at`; it only ends holds, so a stale read by those scans holds more, never less.
- **Restore.** `export-plans` writes each row's holds; `apply-plans` writes each exported row back exactly (end and holds), without deciding a grace from the rewound row, so a restore neither shortens nor restarts a grace. A row whose plan ended and holds nothing is skipped, as an ended plan was before.
- **Unreadable graces.** A value that does not read as a valid list of slots (bad JSON, a slot out of bounds) must never cause deletion, so it is read as holding: a stand-in hold with `RETENTION_LIMIT_MAX` for every retention limit and no paid reads (reads are not deletion), shown as running until 60 days from now, and whose access log hold never ends. The grace scan never rewrites or deletes such a row; it logs `plan_grace_unreadable` with the owner's ID only, on every run while it lasts, and the operator's next `set-plan` or `remove-plan` stores the stand-in as a readable hold (recorded as started, reason `unreadable`), which then ends after 60 days like any other. `export-plans` writes the stand-in for it, and `apply-plans` refuses a file with a slot out of bounds, naming the line and applying nothing. The delivery lookups pass the column's text through untouched in both layouts.
- **Deploying.** The previous release capped each access log row of a plan with an end at the end itself. A one-time pass (`redateDatedPlans`, in the maintenance of every region) writes an `owner_retention` job for every owner whose plan has an end not past its grace, 100 a run per region from `maintenance_cursors`, then marks the region done for good (one read a run from then on), so those rows are re-dated to the grace rule and survive into the grace.
- **Costs.** No D1 read or write added to a request path: the grace rides the plan row's existing reads (`write-costs.test.ts`, `idempotency-regions.test.ts`, `introspection-cost.test.ts` and the content Worker's delivery counts are unchanged). The operator's `set-plan` and `remove-plan` read the row first (one call each, pinned in `scan-costs.test.ts`); the grace scan is one call a run when nothing is due (the cursor and the first page in one batch), at most three pages of 100 a run, one batch per owner it changes; the re-dating pass two calls on its first run and one a run after it; all pinned in `scan-costs.test.ts`. The audit sweep also reads rows holding a grace.
- **Two regions.** `owner_plans` is global and so is the grace; the regional scans already read plans from the global database. `test/two-regions.test.ts` holds an EU workspace's versions for its grace after a lowering under the strict guard and prunes them after.

## Second delivery: taking the data out

- **The page.** `/data` in the dashboard ("Download your data", `screens/download-data.tsx`), offered to the space's admins (role action `plan`: a personal space's owner, a workspace's admins; an editor reads that it is for admins, and still downloads each artifact from its history as before). It lists every artifact of the space a page at a time from the artifact list, and on opening one its retained versions a page at a time, each with the download buttons the history already has (the version download route, published files and sources); each artifact's access log as CSV and JSON (the existing whole-log export, for roles with `access_log`); a link to the audit log's export (for roles with `audit`; the export itself stays gated on `auditExport` and held in a grace); for a personal space, the account's JSON export; and the CLI command for taking everything at once. It lists and links: files come straight from storage through the existing presigned links, and nothing is bundled on the server (a Worker cannot hold or stream tens of gigabytes within its limits).
- **What goes on which date.** While a grace runs the page shows, per kind, how many items go and from when, from one new read, `GET /api/v1/grace-forecast` (`getGraceForecast`, `packages/services/src/grace-forecast.ts`; the kinds and dates from `graceForecastSteps` in `packages/domain/src/plans.ts`): previous versions retained now that the plan's own retention no longer keeps at the end of the first hold that keeps them, access log entries and analytics day counters that expire by then (their rows are already dated to it), audit events past the plan's own retention then, and pinned artifacts when the plan has no `pin`. Counts only, no names. It is authorized like the usage body (`usage`: admins, editors and service tokens), is not plan gated (so it is in neither `GATED` table), and runs only on this route: no read is added to any other request path. The dashboard asks for it only while the usage body it already holds carries a grace. An unreadable grace forecasts nothing: it holds everything and deletes nothing. The route answers the owner's opaque ID too (for the export's marker) and is rate limited per person or token, 30 a minute, after authorization (`GRACE_FORECAST_LIMITS`).
- **`portifact export <folder>`** (`packages/cli/src/export.ts`). Every artifact of the space (`--workspace <slug>` or the personal space), every retained version through the version download route, each version's sources when it has some, each artifact's access log as JSON Lines and the space's audit log as JSON Lines when the caller may read them, and `manifest.json` (`ExportManifest`, `packages/contracts/src/data-export.ts`) written last from what was verified, naming what was left out and why (`forbidden`, `plan_required`, `not_found`). Layout: `artifacts/<id>/v<number>/published|source/<path>` (`exportVersionFolder`). Each file streams to a `.part` file while it is hashed and is renamed into place only when its size and SHA-256 match, so memory stays bounded and nothing unverified is kept; four downloads at once; storage links are renewed and transient failures retried as `pull` does. A second run keeps every complete, verified file, fetches the rest again (a corrupted or partial file included) and rewrites the logs and the manifest, so an interrupted export resumes. The folder must be new, empty or an earlier export of the same space (a `.portifact-export` marker), so it never writes into an unrelated folder. Paths follow `pull`'s rules (canonical, relative, no `..`, no `.git` segment) plus, on Windows, no device names (`con`, `nul`, `com1`...), forbidden characters or trailing dots and spaces (`exportPathSegments`, `packages/domain/src/data-export.ts`), and the resolved target must stay inside its version's folder. `--dry-run` prints the counts and bytes, and during a grace what goes on which date, and writes nothing. A service token exports what it reaches: the artifacts and versions its restriction reaches (published files and sources, like `pull`); it never reads the access log (a person's action) and reads the audit log only where its role allows, so the manifest marks those skipped. It never reads comments, which are not part of the export.
- **Writing the folder safely** (review of the second delivery). The folder may hold anything an earlier run, the user or another program left, links included, so every write goes through `SafeFolder` (`packages/cli/src/safe-folder.ts`): each folder on the way down is checked with `lstat` and created one level at a time, refused when it is a link or its real path leaves the export folder; a file is written into a temporary file of its own under `.portifact-tmp/` (a random name, created exclusively with `wx` and `O_NOFOLLOW` where the platform has it; artifact paths always live under `artifacts/`, so no artifact file can share it), put in place with `rename` (which replaces a name and never writes through it, and only a regular file is replaced), and counts only once it is read back from its place with the right size and SHA-256. A `.portifact-export.lock` file, created exclusively, keeps two exports out of one folder (`folder_in_use`, exit 6; see the second review below for how a dead run's lock is taken over), and the `.portifact-export` marker holds the owner's opaque ID (`ownerId` in the forecast's answer), so another space's export is refused. Paths go through the same rules as `pull`: `validateDownloadPaths`, the publish rules with the stricter `downloadCollisionKey` (case folded through upper then lower case on the decomposed form, so `ſ`/`s` and `ß`/`ss` collide as Windows and macOS store them; such a pair is refused before anything is written), and, on Windows, `windowsNameProblem` (device names with any extension or trailing spaces, `com0` and `lpt0`, `conin$`, `conout$`, `clock$`, forbidden characters, trailing dots and spaces), which `pull` now applies too. Publishing is unchanged: `validateManifestPaths` keeps `publishCollisionKey` (lower case of the canonical NFC path, as before), so a manifest with `straße.html` next to `strasse.html` still publishes and artifacts that already hold such pairs keep updating; only reading them onto a disk refuses them. The two keys are separate functions on purpose and must not be merged. Names from the server are printed through the CLI's one sanitizer (`plain`, `oneLine` in `packages/cli/src/plain.ts`), never raw. An artifact deleted or purged while the export runs is recorded as skipped (`not_found`) and the rest is exported (exit 0); a part the server asks to wait for (`rate_limited`, the audit export's limit) is recorded as skipped and the export exits 8 with everything else written, so running it again completes it. The access log streams to disk a page at a time.
- **Second review of the export.**
  - **Every trusted read walks the folders too.** Checking that a file is already verified, reading the marker and reading a file back after `rename` go through the same per-level `lstat` walk as writing, without creating anything; a link at any parent level is refused (exit 2).
  - **The lock comes first and is taken over only when it is provably dead.** The run takes `.portifact-export.lock` before it reads or writes anything else in the folder. The lock records the process ID, the host and a token. A lock whose process no longer runs on this host (`process.kill(pid, 0)` answering `ESRCH`) is moved aside atomically under the run's own name, checked to be that dead run's, and replaced by a new exclusive create. A live, foreign-host or unreadable lock, or anything other than a regular file at that name, is refused (`folder_in_use`, exit 6) and never deleted.
  - **Stopping the export.** On Ctrl-C or `kill` the run removes its lock (only if it is still its own) and exits 130 (SIGINT) or 143 (SIGTERM), through the injected `Runtime`.
  - **Disk errors and oversized bodies.** A file system error (`EACCES`, `ENOSPC`, a failing `rename` or `mkdir`) stops at once as `write_failed` (exit 1), never retried; only the request and reading its body are. A body longer than its listed size is cut off before more is written.
  - **The manifest.** An earlier run's `manifest.json` is removed when a run starts, and the new one is written only when the run ends, so a folder without `manifest.json` holds an unfinished export.
  - **Exit codes.** 0 complete; 8 complete except for parts the server asked to fetch later (`rate_limited`; run it again); 1 a hard failure; 2, 5, 6 and 7 as everywhere in the CLI; 130 and 143 when stopped.
  - **Server text.** Every `CliError` message passes through `plain` in its constructor: an API error's message, a path, a name. Escape sequences, C0 and C1 controls and every `\p{Cf}` format character (bidirectional overrides, isolates and marks, zero-width characters including ZWJ, the BOM, the soft hyphen, tag characters) are removed, line feeds and tabs kept. `--json` output carries the same cleaned text, so no raw escape byte is printed there either.
  - **What remains (residual).** Between the check of a parent folder and the `rename`, a process that can write to the export folder could swap that folder for a link; Node has no `openat`/`renameat` to close that race. On Windows `O_NOFOLLOW` does not exist, so the checks rest on `lstat` alone. The export folder must therefore not be writable by another user: export into a folder only you can write to.
- **No MCP tool.** Moving a space's data out means gigabytes and many files written to disk: an agent should not carry that inline through tool results, and `portifact export` is the path for an agent that runs commands. MCP keeps `get_usage` (with the grace) and the per-version reads.
- **The notice on every page of the space.** While the usage body the shell already loads carries a grace, every page of the space shows it (`GraceNotice` in `screens/shell.tsx`): until when what the previous plan kept is held, and for admins the two actions, the download page and the plan request (`planRequestHref`); an editor is told to ask an admin. No request is added. "Hide for today" hides it in this browser until the next UTC day (`localStorage`, never for good); the download page itself does not repeat it. It is a design system `Callout` with no live role, shown with one polite live region that stays mounted with the shell, so moving between pages re-inserts nothing and it is announced once per change of what it says (`Callout`'s `live` prop), in both themes and the 8 languages.

## Notices (third delivery)

The three emails (grace start, 14 and 3 days before the end) through the outbox, one per owner, stage and hold, on the capacity ladder with expiry notices, without an opt-out (a service notice about deletion), in the account's language and obeying `noticeDetail(region)`, are the next change. Until then the notice on every page, the Plan page, `portifact usage` and `get_usage` carry the grace.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Columns for the previous plan, seats and overrides.** One previous plan cannot keep two drops with their own ends, and a lowering during a grace would have to either extend the first hold or cut it. A list of holds keeps each exact.
- **Holds inside `overrides`.** No migration, but a release rolled back to the previous code would fail to parse the overrides of any owner in a grace and read them as none, dropping an Enterprise owner's audit retention: a deletion on rollback. A separate column is ignored by older code.
- **A grace checked in every reader.** Many call sites, easy to miss one, and the miss deletes data. Folding the hold into `entitlements` means every existing reader of retention is covered by construction.

## Risks

- Deleting something a grace should hold is the worst outcome. The tests that rule it out: each held kind survives day 59 and goes on day 61 through the existing scans (versions, access log rows and counters, audit events, pins) in `packages/services/test/plan-grace.test.ts`, `analytics.test.ts`, `audit-export.test.ts`, `pins.test.ts`, `version-retention.test.ts` and `two-regions.test.ts`; the domain property test that during a grace no retention value is below the previous plan's.
- A reader that selects the plan row without `grace` still sees a natural expiry's hold (it comes from `valid_until`) but not a lowering's. Every reader of `owner_plans` in `packages/services` and `packages/db` selects it; a new one must too.
- Rolling back to the previous release: it ignores the column, so owners in a grace from a lowering or removal lose the hold and are pruned as before (no worse than today), and a removed plan's `free` row reads as free.
