# 0012 Content search

- Status: accepted, implemented in T8
- Date: 2026-11-10
- Spike: [spikes/content-search](../../spikes/content-search)

## Question

Owners on a plan with the `contentSearch` feature (Team and above) search inside the text of their artifacts, not only names. Can SQLite's FTS5 hold that index in D1, next to the artifacts in the owner's regional database, with the repository's Drizzle migrations, guarded D1 batches, outbox and backups? Which tokenizer serves the product's eight languages (English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Russian, Japanese, Chinese)? What does it cost in storage and per query, and what does it do to backups?

## Tokenizers

| Query in text                             | unicode61 | trigram             | unicode61, CJK segmented            |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| en `revenue`                              | yes       | yes                 | yes                                 |
| es `cancion` in "canción", `ANO` in "año" | yes       | yes                 | yes                                 |
| pt-BR `migracao`, fr `deja`, de `uber`    | yes       | yes                 | yes                                 |
| de `strasse` in "Straße"                  | no        | no                  | no                                  |
| ru `отчет` in "Отчёт"                     | no        | no                  | yes (ё folded to е before indexing) |
| ja `会議` in "東京都の会議資料"           | no        | no (two characters) | yes                                 |
| ja `会議資料`                             | no        | yes                 | yes                                 |
| zh-CN `中文` in "我们的中文文档"          | no        | no (two characters) | yes                                 |
| `road` in "roadmap"                       | no        | yes                 | no                                  |

unicode61 makes one token of a run of Han or kana characters, so a Chinese or Japanese word inside a sentence is never found. trigram finds substrings of three characters or more, which misses most two-character Chinese and Japanese words and costs twice the storage. Segmenting CJK runs ourselves (a one-byte separator, U+001F, between adjacent CJK characters before indexing, and a phrase of single characters for a CJK query term) gives unicode61 substring matching for CJK and word matching everywhere else, at about a third more storage for CJK text. German `ß` is not folded to `ss` by any of them: a known limit. Matching is by whole words outside CJK, with no prefix search.

## Recommended design

FTS5 holds up. The index lives in the owner's regional database, with the artifacts.

1. **Tables.** `search_artifacts` (one row per indexed artifact: owner, version, bytes, partial), `search_chunks` (one row per chunk of about 4,000 characters of a file's text, keyed by artifact, path and sequence) and the FTS5 table `search_text(owner, body)` with `unicode61 remove_diacritics 2`, whose rowid is the chunk's ID. `owner` holds one token per row (`o` and the owner ID in hex), and every MATCH starts with `owner : <token> AND (...)`, so a search never reads another owner's rows. Chunks keep `snippet()` cheap.
2. **Writes.** Only the `search_index` outbox job writes the index. `commitPublish` and `rollbackArtifact` write it in their batch on the switch condition, `restoreArtifact` on its guard, `transferArtifact` on its unchanged condition, each only when the owner's entitlements they already loaded include the feature. The job reads the version's files from R2 (Markdown and TXT sources at the entrypoint; HTML pages of sites through a bounded extractor), applies the caps and replaces the artifact's rows in one batch, every statement conditioned on the version still being current, the artifact active with the owner it read, and the owner's total under its cap. A job for an old version is `stale`; for an indexed version, `done`.
3. **Removal.** Triggers on `artifacts` delete an artifact's `search_artifacts` row when its state leaves `active` (archived artifacts are not searchable) or its owner changes; the cascade and a trigger on `search_chunks` delete the full-text rows. Purges and account and workspace deletions delete or claim the artifact rows, so they need nothing else, and neither does `applyTombstones` after a restore: it claims resurrected artifacts for purging, which fires the trigger (tested).
4. **Safety net.** The 15-minute maintenance writes jobs for active artifacts of entitled owners whose current version is not indexed (`scheduleSearchIndexing`, which also covers a plan gained later and reinstated artifacts) and deletes the index of owners whose plan lost the feature (`dropLapsedSearchIndexes`, conditioned on the plan row it read, like `unpinLapsedPlans`).
5. **Queries.** The MATCH expression is built only from runs of letters, marks and digits, each quoted and joined with AND, so quotes, operators, column filters, `NEAR` and parentheses are separators and no input can produce a syntax error or a wider search. One result per artifact (its best chunk by bm25), ordered like the artifact list (`updated_at`, ID) with its cursor; the snippet comes from `snippet()` with private-use markers and is returned as plain text with UTF-16 ranges.
6. **Caps** (`SEARCH_LIMITS` in contracts): 2 MiB read per file, 512 KiB of text per file, 200 files and 4 MiB of text per version, 32 MiB per owner. What exceeds a cap is not indexed and the result says `partial`.

## Decisions on the open points

- **Who searches.** Only actors who read every artifact of the owner: admins and editors (new role action `search`). A workspace `member` gets `forbidden`, a non-member what a missing workspace gives. Searching for members and outside readers, filtered by each artifact's access policy, is a non-goal of this change.
- **Service tokens.** Allowed (`search` is in `TOKEN_ACTIONS`): a token with `artifacts:read` already downloads every version it reaches (decision 0011), and the search applies the same reach (`own`, key prefix) in the query.
- **Archived artifacts** are not searchable; restoring indexes them again.
- **Audit log.** A search is a read and records nothing.
- **Backups.** Time Travel stays the backup and covers the index. `wrangler d1 export` of a regional database now needs `--table` for every regular table (the index is derived data and can be rebuilt by the scan); documented in operations.

## Cost

At 1.3 bytes of database per byte of text, the per-owner cap is about 42 MiB of database at most, so a regional database (10 GB) holds the full index of about 230 owners at the cap besides everything else. Most owners index far less. If paid owners grow past that, the next step is a separate search database per region (see alternatives). Queries read the owner's matching rows only; a job is about 3 statements plus 2 per 1 MB of text.

## Alternatives rejected

- **trigram**: finds substrings of three characters or more only (most two-character CJK words are missed) at twice the storage.
- **A separate search database per region**: keeps `wrangler d1 export` of the control database working, but no batch would span it and the artifacts, so the guarded write (the version still current) and the trigger-based removal would need another protocol and a deletion fan-out. Kept as the path if index size outgrows the regional database.
- **Whole files as rows**: `snippet()` cost grows with the hits in a row (42 s for one large file).
- **Building snippets in the service from the stored text**: reads up to the whole file per result.

## Open risks

- Time Travel restores FTS5's shadow tables with the rest of the database: checked on 2026-11-10 on a scratch D1 database, where the migrations, the triggers, a `MATCH` with a snippet and a restore to a bookmark all behaved as in workerd. The full backup drill with tombstones over an indexed artifact is still to run (operations, "Content search").
- The index shares the regional database with the artifacts. It moves to a search database of its own per region when it nears 1 to 2 GB in a region, well before the 10 GB limit.
- A phrase across a chunk cut is not found; words are cut only where there is no white space in the last fifth of a chunk.
- Rebuilding the `artifacts` table in a future migration drops the two triggers; that migration must recreate them (noted in the migration and AGENTS.md).

## Changes after review

An independent review of the first implementation found these, all fixed with tests:

- **Query cost.** 100 repeated terms took 2 to 7 seconds on a shared database. Repeated words now count once, a query holds at most 8 different words and 16 characters per Chinese or Japanese run (`SEARCH_LIMITS.queryTerms`, `queryPhraseTokens`, refused with `invalid_input`, never cut), and an owner-level rate limit (`search-owner:<owner>`, 300 a minute) sits beside the per-actor one. The worst accepted query on an owner at its cap took about 20 ms locally (budget 1.5 s in the test).
- **Restricted tokens.** A token limited to its own artifacts or a key prefix reads the full-text table only at the chunk IDs it reaches (`rowid in (...)` with the MATCH), so rows outside its reach cost nothing and timing reveals nothing about them; the test measures D1's rows read with ten times more matching rows outside the reach.
- **Owner column.** The user's words match only the body (`owner : <token> AND body : (...)`).
- **Job memory and limits.** The job reads with a running text budget and stops reading when it is spent; one job reads at most 8 MiB and 40 objects and leaves the rest to a continuation job (`next_file`). An object missing from R2 marks the artifact partial.
- **Extractor.** `/>` is honored only in `svg` and `math`; scripts follow the tokenizer's escaped and double-escaped states; templates nest.
- **Scans.** Both scans resume from a cursor in `maintenance_cursors` with four queries a run.
- **CLI.** Snippets, names and paths go through `plain()`, so no bidi control or line separator reaches the terminal.
