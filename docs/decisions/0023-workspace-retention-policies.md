# 0023 Workspace retention policies

- Status: accepted (the maximum life of artifacts, then comment retention in a second change)
- Date: 2026-11-11
- Feature: premium plan E3, last part (`policies` in `PLANS`, Enterprise, live): "Retention policies: maximum life of an artifact, automatic archive and comment retention"

## Question

A workspace's admins want their own data-minimization rules: no artifact stays online longer than some time, stale artifacts are archived on their own, and old comments go away. These rules archive and delete customers' data on a schedule, so the worst outcome is losing something the workspace did not mean to lose. What does each rule measure, what does it archive or delete, how does it compose with the expiry, pins, the archive, the plan and its grace, how is it enforced without a new read on any request path, and what keeps a restore from bringing back a rule an admin had removed?

## Facts: an artifact's life today

Read from the code, not assumed:

- **Expiry.** Every publish (`commitPublish`), rollback (`rollbackArtifact`, a new version), renewal (`renewArtifact`), restore (`restoreArtifact`) and unpin (`unpinArtifact`, and `unpinLapsedPlans` when a plan loses `pin`) sets `artifacts.expires_at` to now plus `LIMITS.activeDays` (30), the same on every plan. Nothing else moves it. Delivery treats an active artifact past `expires_at` as archived at once (`effectiveState`), so nothing depends on the scan's timing.
- **Pins** (P2, feature `pin`): a pinned artifact has `expires_at` null and never expires; pinning needs the plan, unpinning never does; archiving clears the pin; `unpinLapsedPlans` unpins the artifacts of owners whose plan (and grace, decision 0022) lost `pin`, with a fresh 30-day expiry.
- **Archive.** `archiveExpired` (every 15 minutes, per region, 50 a run) archives active artifacts past their expiry, with `archived_at` the expiry itself and `purge_at` fixed then from the owner's `archiveMonths` (6 on free, 12 on paid, held by a grace). It writes a `lifecycle_events` row and no audit event; a manual archive (`archiveArtifact`) records `artifact.archived`. The lifecycle triggers free the active slot. The content search triggers drop the artifact's index (decision 0012). Custom domains stay attached (only purge, suspension and deletion release them). Archived means: not served, every version, byte, comment, reader, share link and access log row kept, restorable by an admin or editor until `purge_at` (if a slot is free).
- **Restore** brings it back at the same URL with the same access for another 30 days and re-indexes it.
- **Purge.** `purgeArchived` claims archived artifacts past `purge_at` as `purging` and `deleteArtifactData` deletes them for good: the artifact row and, by cascade, versions, files, readers, threads and messages, share links, review requests; its R2 objects through `releaseOrphans`; its access log and view counters through `erase_access_events`; an `artifact` tombstone so a restore cannot bring it back. Nothing of a purged artifact can be recovered.
- **What can be undone:** an expiry by renewing (before it) or restoring (after it, until `purge_at`); a manual archive by restoring; nothing after the purge.

## Decision in short

- **One setting, the maximum life of artifacts** (`artifactMaxDays`, 7 to 3,650 days, null for none, in `workspaces.policy` with its effective date `artifactMaxDaysFrom`). An artifact of the workspace is archived that many days after its **last publish** (a rollback counts, it is a new version) **or restore**, whatever its renewals. It only caps `expires_at`; archiving is the usual expiry, never a new path, and nothing is deleted by it.
- **Automatic archive of inactivity is not a second setting.** Today's expiry already archives an artifact 30 days after its last publish, renewal or restore when nobody renews it. Measured honestly on the data every workspace has (no per-delivery write, analytics only on some plans and only as daily counts, the access log only for `restricted` artifacts), "inactivity" would be "time since the last publish, renewal, restore or comment". A shorter inactivity window and a maximum life counted from the last publish differ only in whether a renewal or a comment keeps an artifact alive, and a renewal is exactly what a retention rule exists to override. Two settings that look different and behave almost the same would mislead admins, so there is one, named for what it measures: "Archive each artifact a number of days after its last publish or restore".
- **Comment retention ships separately** (see "Comment retention, designed" below): it deletes rows for good and needs its own tombstones, so it gets its own reviewable change.
- **Nothing happens at the save.** A new or shorter maximum takes effect `retentionNoticeDays` (7) after the save; the server sets `artifactMaxDaysFrom`, ignoring what a client sends (`retentionTiming`). No deadline is ever earlier than that date. A longer one keeps the date the current one had (lengthening during the notice period never makes it bind sooner); removing it clears it. Loosening applies at once.
- **Pins:** while a maximum life is set (pending or in effect), pinning is refused (`policy_refused`, `checkPolicyPin`), and the policy's job unpins pinned artifacts with their deadline. A pin means "never expires", which the admin's rule rules out; refusing it is predictable, and the Policies page and the artifact page say so.
- **Restore and moving in start a new life**, like a publish: an archived artifact restored by an admin or editor, or an artifact moved into the workspace (`transferArtifact`), gets `min(its plan's expiry, its deadline counted from now)`, so it is never archived sooner than the maximum's days (at least 7) after the restore or the move; a pinned artifact that moves in is unpinned with a renewal's 30 days, capped the same way. Both are deliberate acts, logged, and the same person could publish a new version anyway. The person moving it is told before confirming, in the dashboard's dialog, by when the target's maximum would archive it; the response (and the CLI) carries the new `expiresAt`.
- **Renewals are capped** at the deadline counted from the last publish or restore, and a renewal that the deadline would put in the past is refused (`policy_refused`, "publish a new version"), never turned into an archive.
- **A cap never writes an expiry in the past.** `policyExpiry` and its SQL twin floor the capped expiry at `now`: an active artifact that is not already past its expiry and comes under the maximum late (a reinstatement after a suspension, a pin lapsing with its plan, a write that read the old policy) is archived at the next expiry run from the moment it was caught, never with an `archived_at` before it.
- **Plan and grace.** Setting or shortening needs `policies` in the plan; lengthening or removing never does. A lapsed plan keeps the maximum running: it is the customer's own data-minimization rule, and stopping it silently would break their compliance. The grace of decision 0022 holds what a plan kept (the archive length among it); it does not suspend the workspace's own rule. They compose simply: the maximum life decides when an artifact is archived, the plan or its grace how long the archive is kept (`purge_at`, fixed at archiving).

## How the cap works

`maxLifeDeadline(life, lifeStart) = max(from, lifeStart + days)` and `policyExpiry(policy, planExpiry, lifeStart, now) = min(planExpiry, max(deadline, now))` in `packages/domain/src/workspace-policies.ts`; their SQL twins `maxLifeDeadlineSql` and `cappedExpirySql` are in `packages/services/src/max-life.ts` (compared on a real D1 over 400 generated cases in `workspace-retention.test.ts`). The life start is the later of the current version's `committed_at` (its `created_at` for an older row) and `artifacts.life_started_at` (migration 0062, written by `restoreArtifact` and `transferArtifact`), so the cap reads no other table and needs no backfill. Whenever a cap brings an expiry forward, the plan's expiry it replaced is kept in `artifacts.uncapped_expires_at` (same migration; `uncappedExpiry` and `uncappedExpirySql` beside the cap on every write path), and null when the maximum did not bind. Properties (tested with fast-check): the cap never extends an expiry, never puts a deadline before the effective date, never writes an expiry before now, and never gives a just-published artifact less than 7 days.

Every write path computes it with the policy it already has, so no request gains a read or a round trip: `Actor.policy` comes with the membership (`authorizeUploadOn` in `commitPublish`, `managedArtifact` in `rollbackArtifact`, `authorizeOn` in the lifecycle actions). Publish, rollback and restore start the life now and compute in JS; renew and unpin keep it and compute in the update statement itself (`cappedExpirySql` reads the row's own columns). Measured: a renewal and an archive cost the same D1 calls with and without a maximum life (`packages/services/test/workspace-retention.test.ts`), and the existing request-path cost tests (`write-costs`, `introspection-cost`, the keyed adapters, the content Worker) are unchanged.

## Enforcement

The maximum only moves `expires_at` earlier; `archiveExpired` archives as for any expiry. Two places move it for existing artifacts:

- **The `workspace_policy` job**, written in the save's global batch (as decision 0015's). It now runs on the workspace's own region (`workspaceRegionDb`): with a separate EU database the job used to run only on the default one, which left EU workspaces to the scan. One step (`enforceMaxLife`, its own batch, 50 artifacts) unpins pinned artifacts and caps the expiry of those expiring after their deadline, each recomputed from the row as it is now, so an artifact published or restored since the selection keeps its later deadline; it moves the lifecycle generation (a racing renewal answers `version_conflict`).
- **The scan** `enforceWorkspacePolicies` (every 15 minutes, per region, capped and resumed by its cursor as in decision 0015) finds workspaces with an artifact over its deadline in the same one regional query (the flags travel in its single JSON parameter: 4 bound parameters whatever the number of workspaces) and runs a step, catching a write that read the old policy.

Statements select their own rows (`overMaxLifeIds`, `giveBackIds`, at most 10 bound parameters each, never a list of IDs). With the workspace and its artifacts in one database (production today), every statement of the step is conditioned on the revision read (`exists (... policy_revision = ?)`, through `bothSides`), so a policy changed between the read and the write stops the batch. With a separate regional database no batch can say that: the step reads the revision again after its batch, and if it moved, runs the give-back again with the policy now in force. The window of a stale cap ends with the step itself.

**Any sequence of saves (the give-back).** A cap must never outlive the rule that set it, whatever the order of saves: set, shorten, lengthen, remove, in any number of steps. Each step first gives back, then caps. The give-back selects the active, unpinned artifacts with a kept plan's expiry (`uncapped_expires_at`) whose expiry is earlier than `min(that plan's expiry, the current deadline, never before now)`, raises them to it, and forgets the kept expiry once nothing caps it any more; without a maximum the target is the plan's expiry itself. So after any sequence every artifact the policy capped is at `min(its plan's expiry, the current deadline)`, never before the current policy's effective date, and at its plan's expiry once there is no maximum. The give-back is idempotent (the target is stored, not recomputed from the clock), so the scan runs it as well as the job: a lost or reordered job changes nothing. Every job step runs the give-back, whatever the policy, so the removal's own job gives the time back at once; the stale-cap sweep below covers everything a job cannot reach. A review of the first version of this change found the problem this solves: the give-back then matched only artifacts whose expiry was exactly the deadline of the maximum being replaced, so set 90 days, shorten to 30, then remove left the first cap in place (an artifact archived on day 7 with no policy at all). The alternative, carrying the replaced maximum on every change and matching exact deadlines, was rejected: it is right only one step back, depends on every job running in order, and cannot tell a cap from an artifact's own expiry that happens to equal it. Artifacts already archived are not restored by a loosening; an admin restores them.

**Trail.** Each step records `policy.enforced` by the system with counts (`deadlinesSet`, `deadlinesExtended`); each unpin `artifact.unpinned` by the system with `policy: artifactMaxDays` and its `lifecycle_events` row; each artifact archived while a cap held it (it kept a plan's expiry) `artifact.archived` by the system with `policy: artifactMaxDays` in the archive's own batch (other expiries record nothing, as before). Search, active slots and custom domains follow from the archive as for any expiry. **No new webhook event:** `artifact.expiring` already fires from the expiry notice scan before an artifact archives, and no automatic archive has an event today; one can be appended to `WEBHOOK_EVENTS` later without changing this design.

**Expiry notices** need no new kind: the policy moves `expires_at` during the notice period (never before the effective date), so the usual notice goes out three days before, with `noticeDetail(region)` deciding names as for any expiry. The notice does not say the deadline comes from the policy: that would need the policy in the notice job (a read per owner) and a new text in 8 languages for a fact the artifact page and the Policies page already show; left out.

## The invariant

After one maintenance run with no pending job, every active artifact's `expires_at` and `uncapped_expires_at` are what its owner's current policy gives: for an artifact still live, `min(its plan's expiry, the current deadline)` (the plan's own without a maximum, or in a personal space), with the plan's expiry kept exactly when the cap binds; an artifact already past its expiry is due by its plan or the current maximum, never by a removed or older rule. That holds for any interleaving of saves, lost jobs, suspensions, reinstatements, moves and renewals, including a write that read the policy just before it changed (in either direction). Three parts keep it:

- **The steps** (a job, or the scan for a workspace that sets a maximum) cap what the current maximum asks for and give back what it no longer does.
- **The stale-cap sweep** (`sweepStaleCaps`, in each region's scan run) covers every owner the steps do not visit: a workspace whose maximum was removed (its job lost, or an artifact suspended or racing while it ran), and a personal space an artifact moved to. One statement reads the saved cursor with the first 20 owners, by ID, that hold a live capped artifact and do not set a maximum (`staleCapOwners`). With the workspace table in the same database (one region, production today) "do not set a maximum" is decided for every workspace inside that statement; with a separate regional database the workspaces the scan's page read with a maximum (at most `scanWorkspaces`, 500) come as the one JSON parameter, and one global read drops the found owners that set one beyond that page (each takes a slot of the 20 and is skipped; the cursor moves past it, so it never stalls the sweep). With nothing capped the first statement is the only call (`runMaintenance` 58 to 59 in `scan-costs.test.ts`). Then the idempotent give-back, one batch of at most 50 per owner (with `policy.enforced` for a workspace, held to "still no maximum" when the workspace and its artifacts share a database), until the run's budget of 200 artifacts is spent (`STALE_CAPS_SWEEP`); the cursor resumes after the last owner read, so an owner with more caps than one batch gets its next batch on the next pass and never holds up the owners after it. It does not re-cap: an artifact a write left uncapped under a maximum set meanwhile (the other direction) belongs to a workspace with a maximum, which the steps already find (the scan's rank 4).
- **The expiry run** (`archiveExpired`, first in each maintenance run) never archives by a stale cap: a capped artifact due now whose current target (its plan's expiry, or the current deadline if earlier) is still in the future gets it back instead, in the archive's own batch (the give-back moves the generation, so the archive then matches nothing; no extra call), and an archive is credited to the policy (`artifact.archived` with `policy: artifactMaxDays`) only while the owner still sets a maximum.
- **Until the expiry run.** An artifact whose stale cap passes before a sweep reaches it is shown as archived by delivery (`effectiveState` reads `expires_at`) until the next expiry run gives the time back: at most one maintenance interval, 15 minutes. Nothing is archived or deleted meanwhile; its row stays `active`.

`packages/services/test/workspace-retention.test.ts` tests it as a property over four fixed seeds, nine seeded sequences each, of seven random steps over three artifacts, against a model that keeps each artifact's plan expiry and life start, checked after one maintenance run (the expiry run, then the scan): live artifacts at what the current policy gives, nothing archived by that run while the owner's current policy gives it later. Two of every three sequences end with a maximum applied and then removed with its job lost, one of them letting the earliest stale cap pass first, and counters require that each seed reaches both a live stale cap before the scan and a passed one before the expiry run, so the test cannot pass by missing either fix. Removing either fix alone (the sweep's call, or the expiry run's give-back) fails all four seeds.

## The preview, the confirmation and the admins' notice

The preview (and the save, refused again if it grew) counts, only for a new or shorter maximum: active artifacts it archives the day it takes effect (`artifactsAtStart`), those it archives within 30 days after that earlier than their own expiry would have (`artifactsSoon`), and pinned ones it unpins (`pinsLifted`). They are `PolicyImpact` fields, defaulting to 0 so a confirmation sent by an older dashboard still parses (and is then refused until confirmed). A new or shorter maximum writes one `retention_notice` job in the save's global batch; the jobs Worker emails every admin once (`sendRetentionNotice`: claimed before sending, put back on a failed first send, suppressed addresses skipped, `checkNoticeCapacity`, `recordEmailsSent`, moot once the maximum was removed or replaced by one with another effective date; one lengthened before the relay writes no notice of its own and keeps the date, so the first notice goes out with the days in force and the counts they give, read again from the workspace's region), in the admin's language among the 8, with counts, the number of days and the date, never a name, so the email is the same in every region. The Policies page shows every member a banner while it waits for its date, and the rule in effect after.

## Restores

The danger here is the opposite of decision 0015's: re-applying a tighter rule is what archives. So `apply-policies`:

- always applies a file's longer or removed maximum, alone if the rest of the item is held back for lifting a restriction (`liftedFields` never counts the maximum life as a lifted restriction);
- applies a file's new or shorter maximum with the effective date the file stores, never an earlier one (a file without one, edited by hand, gets the notice period);
- removes a maximum life the restore brought back from a workspace the file does not list (exported after the restore point, so it had no policy then), reported as `retentionCleared`; the rest of that workspace's policy is only listed, as before.

The job it writes gives back what the restored maximum capped. Between the restore and `apply-policies`, the scan may cap or archive with the restored rule: archiving is reversible until `purge_at` (months away), and operations.md says to run `apply-policies` right after the tombstones.

## Rollback

`storedPolicy` stores `artifactMaxDays` and its date only while set, so the previous release reads every other workspace's row; the cost falls only on workspaces that set a maximum, as with the network lists of decision 0020. What the previous release does with such a row: its policy schema is strict, so `policyOf` throws on the unknown fields wherever it parses the row, and the request fails with a 500 (`internal`). That is: every request acting on that workspace (`actorOn`: the API, MCP and the CLI, every role and its service tokens, publishing, access changes and reading its artifacts' pages in the dashboard), the workspace list for each of its members (`GET /api/v1/workspaces`, MCP `list_workspaces`, `portifact workspaces`: the whole list, so the dashboard's space menu fails for them), its Policies page, and opening its artifacts' share links (`ownerEntitlementsAndPolicy`). Delivery of its artifacts keeps working (the content Worker reads only single fields with `json_extract`), and its scan step logs `workspace_policy_unreadable` and skips it. It cannot be made tolerant from this side: the previous release's code is what fails. The only cheap alternative, keeping the maximum in columns of its own outside `policy`, would add columns to the membership's query and a second shape for every reader of a policy, for a rollback that the operator can prepare for in minutes. So the rule stays, and `docs/releasing.md` says what to do before rolling back: export the policies, apply a copy without the maximum (removing it is always applied, and its job gives the time back), wait until no `workspace_policy` or `retention_notice` row is left for those workspaces, roll back, and after rolling forward apply the original export with each maximum's effective date cleared, so it takes effect after a new notice period instead of archiving at once what passed its deadline meanwhile. The previous release reads the `workspace_policy` jobs this one writes (their payload is unchanged); it acks a `retention_notice` message as malformed, but the outbox row stays and is relayed again every `OUTBOX_REDELIVER_MS` (an hour), so such an email goes out late, after rolling forward; the wait step avoids that delay. `life_started_at` and `uncapped_expires_at` are columns the previous release ignores; writes it makes leave them as they were, which can only keep a stale kept expiry (the give-back raises toward it, never lowers).

## Comment retention

Shipped in a second change, after the three reviews of the maximum life. Unlike the maximum life, it deletes rows nobody can bring back, so the design starts from what those reviews found: a rule must never act after it changed, whatever the order of saves, jobs, scans and restores; every statement selects its own rows within D1's limits, through an index the planner reaches without statistics; everything fails closed; and each safety claim has a test that fails when the code that makes it true is removed.

### Facts

- A thread (`annotation_threads`) belongs to one artifact and version and holds its messages (`annotation_messages`, by foreign key with `on delete cascade`, as do `comment_notices` and `thread_issues`). Its `updated_at` moves on every reply, edit, resolution and reopening (`touchThread`), so it is the thread's last activity.
- A version that stops being current records when (`versions.superseded_at`): a publish sets it on its base version and a rollback on the version it replaces, each in its own batch. A transfer changes no version. A pruned version's row is deleted, so its `superseded_at` is gone with it; every publish and rollback also writes a `lifecycle_events` row (`published`, `rolled_back`), which is never pruned.
- Deleting a thread already exists (a moderator's `deleteAnnotationThread`): a `thread_deleted` row in `annotation_events` (clients drop the thread at their next refresh) and the row deleted; the `annotation_usage` triggers give the owner's quota back for every message and the target. Comments are not in the content search index. Approval review notes are `review_verdicts`, not comments, and are out of scope.
- Nothing about a deleted thread survives a D1 Time Travel restore today: a moderator's deletion comes back with it (outside this change, noted below).

### The invariant

For any sequence of saves (set, shorten, lengthen, remove), publishes, rollbacks, lost jobs, moves, suspensions, reinstatements, plan changes and restores, a thread is deleted only when, at the moment of the statement that deletes it, its workspace's comment retention in force allows it: the rule is set, its effective date has passed, the plan includes `policies`, the artifact is still the workspace's and active or archived, the thread is not open on the artifact's current version, and its clock plus the days, and its artifact's last move into the workspace plus `commentMoveNoticeDays` (7), are both past (`threadDeletable`, `threadDeadline`, `threadClock`). Nothing else ever deletes for it. A deleted thread never comes back after a restore, and nothing is deleted after a restore until the policies are applied again.

### What is deleted

- **Whole threads, never single messages.** Half a conversation misleads; a hole in a thread would need the placeholder rule of deleted accounts, and the reason that rule exists (keeping the replies of people who did not leave) does not apply to a rule about age. So the unit is the thread, with every message in it, and no reply is ever left without what it answers.
- **The clock.** A resolved thread's clock is its last activity. An open thread's clock is its last activity or the moment its version stopped being current, whichever is later (`threadClock`): an open thread is kept while its version is current, so the moment a publish or a rollback replaces it, the thread gets the whole period from then, never less. Otherwise a publish by any editor or CI key would turn exempt threads, counted nowhere and confirmed by nobody, into ones deleted at the next scan. The moment comes from `versions.superseded_at`; when the version's row is gone (pruned) or never had it, from the first publish or rollback of the artifact after the thread was created (`lifecycle_events`, never pruned): a thread is created on its version after that version's publish, so this is the moment it stopped being current, never earlier and stable whatever is published or pruned later (the earliest retained version after the thread would move forward as versions are pruned, restarting the clock on an artifact published more often than its plan keeps versions); only for data older than both, the current version's commit. A transfer does not touch it (the move notice below applies).
- **Open threads on the current version are kept.** They are what reviewers are waiting on. Resolved threads are deleted wherever they are.
- **Suspended artifacts are left alone** for the operator; archived ones are not (the rule is about comments, not about whether the artifact is online).
- **Bounds:** 30 to 3,650 days (`commentMinDays`, `commentMaxDays`): a month at least, so a mistake has time to be noticed.

### The mechanism

- **One definition.** What may be deleted at a moment is one SQL condition (`deletable` in `packages/services/src/comment-retention.ts`, the twin of `threadDeletable`): the scan's selection, the preview's and confirmation's counts, the second notice's date and counts and the notice job's recount all use it. It carries a range on `updated_at` (`<= now - days`) and `state in ('open', 'resolved')`, so `annotation_threads_owner_state_updated_idx` bounds the read to one workspace's threads old enough to be due (`owner_id=? AND state=? AND updated_at<?`, asserted with `explain query plan`). The range is a correct pre-filter because the clock is never earlier than `updated_at`, so no deadline is earlier than `updated_at` plus the days; open threads kept by their version's clock are inside the range and filtered after it, the one read the index cannot avoid.
- **Setting it.** Only admins, only through the dashboard session, as every policy; no MCP tool, CLI command or bearer route. Setting or shortening needs `policies` in the plan; lengthening or removing never does and applies at once. The server sets `commentRetentionFrom` (`commentTiming`): a new or shorter rule takes effect 7 days after the save; a longer one keeps the date it had. The preview counts the threads and the messages deleted on the effective date (`threadsAtStart`, `messagesAtStart`) and the threads deleted within 30 days after (`threadsSoon`); the save needs them confirmed and is refused again if they grew. The dialog and the Policies page say that open threads on a current version are not counted and get the whole period once their version is replaced.
- **Only the scan deletes.** `enforceCommentRetention`, every 15 minutes, per region: one global batch reads its cursor and up to 100 workspaces of the region with a rule (with their plan rows and whether their second notice was decided); with no rule in the region that is the only call. `json_valid` comes before `json_extract` in that statement, so one malformed stored policy never fails it for the region (the maximum life's stale-cap sweep, which reads every policy in one statement too, got the same guard). Then one batch per workspace of at most 50 threads, the oldest activity first, until the run's 200 (`commentDeleteBatch`, `commentDeleteRun`), resuming from the cursor, so a large workspace never starves the others. No save and no job deletes anything: a removal has nothing to undo and nothing to run.
- **Every delete is held to the rule in force.** Each batch's statements (the `policy.enforced` event, the `thread_deleted` events, the tombstones or their job, and the delete itself) select the same threads (`dueThreadIds`). With the workspace and its artifacts in one database (production today), each statement is also conditioned on the workspace's policy revision and its plan row being the ones the scan read (`inForceSql`), so a save or a plan change between the scan's read and its delete makes the batch delete nothing. With a separate regional database no statement can span the two databases: the revision and the plan row's `updated_at` are read again from the global database in one statement right before each batch. The worst window there is one round trip: a removal or a plan change saved after that second read and before the regional batch lets that one batch (at most 50 threads) still run; nothing after it. A plan that expires by its date is decided at the scan's read, within the run.
- **It fails closed.** Any error while deciding whether deletion is allowed means nothing is deleted for that workspace in that run: the guard record missing, unreadable, different or unreachable; an unreadable policy; a failed batch.
- **Moves.** An artifact moved into a workspace (`artifacts.life_started_at`, set by the move and by a restore from the archive) comes under the rule only `commentMoveNoticeDays` (7) after the move, never at the next scan. Moved out, the rule stops at once: every statement requires the artifact and the thread to be the workspace's (the artifact's `owner_id` as well as the thread's, the condition a write that authorized on an owner must carry).
- **A lapsed plan suspends the rule.** When the scan finds a rule on a workspace whose plan no longer includes `policies`, it deletes the guard record and then removes the rule (`policy.changed` by the system, reason `plan_lapsed`), and deletes nothing: the comments are kept until an admin sets it again, with a new notice and confirmation. The opposite of the maximum life's choice, because nothing a lapse leaves behind can be undone here. The plan grace of decision 0022 holds data a plan kept; it does not keep a deleting rule running.
- **The trail.** Each batch records `policy.enforced` by the system with `threadsDeleted` and `messagesDeleted` (counts only), a `thread_deleted` event per thread in `annotation_events` (as a moderator's deletion, so clients drop it), and a `comment_thread` tombstone per thread. No webhook event: there is none for a moderator's deletion either, a payload would have nothing to carry but IDs, and the admins were emailed; one can be appended later. Comment notices waiting for a deleted thread go with it by foreign key.
- **The notices.** A new or shorter rule writes a `comment_retention_notice` job (`set`) in the save's batch: every admin is emailed once during the notice period, with the date and the counts. The second (`soon`) is decided once per effective date by the first scan that sees it, and that decision is marked whatever it is (`comment_retention_warned:<workspace>` holds the date): the first deletion is the earliest deadline of the threads the rule may ever delete (never before the effective date), read once; when it is at least `commentWarnAfterDays` (14) after the save and still ahead, the job is written to go out `commentWarnDays` (3) before it, otherwise the first notice was recent enough. A lengthening keeps the date and its decision; the job recounts what is due on the date it announces when it sends, with the same definition, and drops itself when nothing is. Both go through the usual machinery (read and checked before the claim, so an unreadable policy leaves the job to run again; claimed before sending; suppressions; `checkNoticeCapacity`; `recordEmailsSent`; eight languages) and carry counts, days and dates only, the same in every region. A rule removed, or replaced by one with another date, makes a pending notice moot. A thread moved in after the decision can be deleted before the date it announced; it has its own 7-day move notice.

### Restores

- **A deleted thread never comes back.** Each deletion writes a `comment_thread` tombstone: in the same batch with one database; with a separate regional database, through a `comment_tombstones` outbox job written in the regional batch on the same selection, carrying the deleted IDs (at most 50), which writes the tombstones in the global database, idempotent and retried by the outbox until done, so a crash after the deletion never leaves a thread a restore could bring back. `export-tombstones` carries them and `apply-tombstones` deletes the threads again (a regional entry, `commentThreads`). They are pruned after 30 days, the D1 Time Travel window, like group tombstones.
- **The restore guard: agreement on a per-save token.** Every save that sets or changes a comment retention (its days or its date) stores a fresh random token in the policy, next to the rule (`commentRetentionToken`; `storedPolicy` keeps it only while the rule is set, and it is never shown or taken from a client), and writes a record with the same token, days and date to the workspace's region's R2 bucket (`system/comment-retention/<workspace>`, `{"token": ..., "days": ..., "from": ...}`), which no D1 restore rewinds. The scan deletes for a workspace only when, in that run, right before its batch, it read that record and its token, days and date are the ones in the policy the batch is held to (`guardAgreement`), so a token can never vouch for another rule (a file edited by hand, say). No record, an unreadable one, another token, no bucket or any R2 error: nothing is deleted for that workspace in that run, and the reason is logged as an enum (`comment_retention_held`). Nothing else decides; there is no shared counter.
- **The invariant: a D1 restore cannot make D1 hold a token equal to R2's unless D1's rule is the very rule of the last save that wrote R2.** Tokens are random per save, and R2 holds only the last one. Restored to before a removal: D1 has an old token, R2 none. To before a lengthening or a re-set: D1 has the old token, R2 the new one. To before a lapse the scan recorded, or the operator's plan change: R2 has none. To after the last change: they agree, and that rule is the right one. Other workspaces, other regions and the operator's commands never touch a workspace's record, so there is nothing to arm, and nothing can arm it by accident.

Who writes the record, in which order, and what each failure leaves (D1 is the policy row, R2 the record):

| Writer                                                                                                                                       | Order                                                                                                                        | R2 fails                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | R2 answers, then D1 fails (or the process dies between)                          |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

**The residual, and its manual fix.** A record can outlive the rule it guards whenever one of its writes fails: both of a removal's deletes, a new rule's confirmation (its read of the policy or its delete), or a lengthening's R2 write that lands although R2 answered with an error. Each of these logs `comment_retention_guard_write_failed`. Such a record only matters for a restore of the global database to a moment when the policy held that record's token, made while the record is still there. So, on any `comment_retention_guard_write_failed`: read the workspace's stored policy (`npx wrangler d1 execute portifact-control --remote --command "select json_extract(policy, '$.commentRetentionDays'), json_extract(policy, '$.commentRetentionToken') from workspaces where id = '<workspace-id>'"`) and the record (`npx wrangler r2 object get portifact-artifacts/system/comment-retention/<workspace-id> --remote --pipe`), and if the policy sets no comment retention, or a rule whose token is not the record's (`commentRetentionToken` against the record's `token`), delete the record by hand once R2 answers: `npx wrangler r2 object delete portifact-artifacts/system/comment-retention/<workspace-id> --remote` (`portifact-artifacts-eu` and `--jurisdiction eu` for a workspace in the EU region, in both R2 commands). Deleting a record never arms anything; at worst it pauses a rule its admins then save again. Never write one by hand.

The scan never writes the record and no job ever does: a restore rewinds the outbox together with the rule, so a job retried after a restore would re-arm exactly what the guard must stop. `apply-policies` writes the exported policies, tokens included, as it writes the rest: a workspace whose exported token is R2's deletes again by itself; one that was skipped, changed after the export or missing from the file keeps disagreeing until an admin saves. An export taken after the restore by mistake holds the restored token, which still disagrees with R2 (unless nothing changed after the restore point, when it is the right rule): safe without anyone noticing, and useless. It still always applies a file's removed or longer rule (it only loosens), and writes no record for it: deleting the record there could only pause a rule its admins last saved, and writing one would arm whatever the file says, a stale file included. It never tightens deletion: a file whose comment retention is stricter than the stored one (shorter, new, or taking effect earlier while that date is ahead) leaves the stored one as it is, applies the rest of the item, and names the workspace (`commentHeld`, "kept their comment retention because the file's is stricter").

A rule that is set but not in force is shown on the Policies page with one sentence and the fix: save the rule again, which (like any new rule) starts a new notice period with its own confirmation. The page reads the record only while a rule is set, and the notice emails are not sent for a rule that is not in force. There is no email about the pause.

- **A save decides against the stored rule.** A removal or lengthening is decided against the policy the row holds, never against the guard's view of it: a removal always runs. The guard record changes only what a save that keeps the rule means: when the record is `missing` or `different` (not in force), saving the rule again sets it as a new rule (a new date, confirmation and token); when the record cannot be read (`unavailable`, `unreadable`), a save that keeps the rule as it is or removes it goes through with the stored rule untouched (no new date, no new token), and one that changes it is refused whole (`capacity_unavailable`), so a save is never half applied. The preview follows the same rules.
- **Concurrent saves.** Worked out for every interleaving, each a test (`comment-retention-guard.test.ts`, "concurrent saves"): after both finish, the scan may delete only under a rule the last completed save left set, and otherwise deletes nothing.

| First save    | Second save  | Interleaving                                                   | Result                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------- | ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A sets a rule | B removes it | B runs entirely while A's record is in flight, before it lands | A's confirmation reads B's removal and deletes A's late record: no rule, no record                                                                                                                |
| A sets a rule | B removes it | A's record landed, B runs before A's confirmation              | B's second delete takes A's record; A's confirmation finds no token of its own: no rule, no record                                                                                                |
| A sets a rule | B removes it | B after A completed                                            | B deletes the record before and after its D1 write: no rule, no record                                                                                                                            |
| A shortens    | B lengthens  | B runs entirely while A's record is in flight                  | B's rule is stored with its record; A's late record overwrites it, and A's confirmation, finding B's token, deletes A's record: B's rule stored, not in force until saved again (deletes nothing) |
| A shortens    | B lengthens  | B after A's record landed                                      | B's record and rule: in force, B's rule                                                                                                                                                           |
| B lengthens   | A shortens   | A runs entirely while B's record is in flight                  | A's rule is stored, B's late record lands, B's D1 write loses the revision (`version_conflict`): A's rule stored, not in force (deletes nothing)                                                  |

- **The matrix, checked on paper before building, and as a property test.** Every change (set, shorten, lengthen, remove, a lapse, the operator's plan change) x {R2 answers, R2 fails, R2 answers then D1 fails, D1 answers then the process dies} x a restore to before it or after it, with and without a scan in between, in both database layouts. Two consequences shaped the design: a lengthening must be refused when its R2 write fails (above), and the token changes only when the rule does, so saving another field keeps it (a restore to before an unrelated save then pauses nothing, and agreement still means the rule of the last save that wrote R2). Two concurrent saves can end with D1 holding one save's token and R2 another's: they never agree on a rule neither holds, and the page tells the admins.
- **Rejected: an explicit restore marker.** A pre-restore operator command writing a marker that `apply-policies` acknowledges would also work, but protects nothing when the operator skips the step (and every earlier design here failed through a missed writer); the token agreement needs no step and no writer outside the saves.
- **What it costs.** One R2 read per workspace per run, only for a workspace with threads due in that run (a `limit 1` select first, then the read, then the batch); none when nothing is due; no R2 write on any scan. One R2 write per save that changes the rule, per lapse and per operator plan change of a workspace with a rule. `runMaintenance`'s D1 calls stay 62: the epoch read rode in the scan's first batch, so removing it removed a statement, not a call.

### Rollback

Policies store the two fields only while set, so the previous release reads every workspace that never set comment retention; for one that did, its strict schema throws and it answers 500 wherever it parses that row (the same list as for the maximum life above), so the release notes' procedure applies to both. The previous release runs no comment retention scan, so it deletes nothing; it ignores the guard records in R2 and the token in the policy of a workspace it cannot read anyway; it cannot run a `comment_retention_notice` or `comment_tombstones` job (acked as malformed, the outbox row relayed again after rolling forward: the email goes out late, and with a separate regional database the tombstones are written late, so drain the EU outbox of `comment_tombstones` rows before rolling back); its `export-tombstones` writes `comment_thread` lines that its own `apply-tombstones` refuses (an unknown kind), so a restore under the previous release needs those lines dropped from the file, and brings deleted threads back.

### Not built, and why

- **Single-message deletion** (above): not safe to make predictable.
- **A note for reviewers outside the workspace in the viewer.** Members see the rule on the Policies page and every admin is emailed; a reader from outside the workspace is told only by the legal texts. Showing it in the viewer's composer needs the viewer's lookup to carry the workspace's rule, which is one more global read for a separate regional database: a choice for the maintainer, not made here.
- **Tombstones for a moderator's deletion.** Out of this change; a restore brings those back today, as before.
- **A sweep of stale guard records (follow-up).** The scan could list each region's `system/comment-retention/` prefix and delete the record of any workspace whose stored policy sets no rule, or a rule with another token, which would close the residual above without anyone reading the logs. About 2,900 class A operations a month per region (one list per 15-minute run, plus a delete per stale record). Not built in this change: the manual fix covers the residual while it stays rare.

2. A lapse moved the epoch but never reached the bucket (raised only by a run that was going to delete), and a failed bucket write after a removal left the same hole: every run now raises the bucket when the database is ahead before anything else, lapses raise it at once, and a property test restores the global database at random points of random histories.
3. A publish turned exempt open threads into threads deleted at the next scan, outside the confirmed counts: the open-thread clock above.
4. The second notice could go out after deletions started, with a wrong date, and was re-read every run for workspaces with nothing due: decided once per effective date, from the earliest deadline.
5. With a separate regional database the tombstones were written outside the outbox: now the `comment_tombstones` job.
6. One malformed policy stopped the scan for the region: `json_valid` (and in the maximum life's sweep).
7. An unreadable bucket mark read as no mark: it pauses.
8. The artifact owner condition was not pinned by a test; the EU path re-read only the revision: both fixed.

### Review of comment retention's second version

### Review of comment retention's third version

A third check found the guard itself unsound: after a restore the epoch was behind the mark and the scan paused, but any later bump of the one shared, clock-driven epoch (another workspace's removal, a lapse the paused scan recorded, a deleting run in the other region, the operator's `remove-plan` during the documented restore order) wrote `now`, past the old mark, and re-armed the restored rule before `apply-policies`. "Epoch behind mark" is not a monotone signal, and each new writer was one more place to get it wrong. The epoch, the marks, re-arming, `raiseBeforeLoosening`, `advanceGuard`, `openGuard` and `apply-policies`' exit 1 were removed and replaced by the token agreement above. The reviewer's four reproductions are tests (`comment-retention-guard.test.ts`), with the matrix as a property test over both layouts and a real rewind of the workspace row, its plan row and its outbox rows. The clock (with its `lifecycle_events` fallback) and the deletion itself were confirmed unchanged.

### Review of comment retention's fourth version

The token design was confirmed; the save path was not. A save decided its loosening against the policy as the guard record saw it, so a removal while R2 could not be read, a removal of a rule that was not in force, and a removal racing a new rule's late R2 write were all answered OK and saved nothing, leaving the rule to delete once the stores agreed. And the record held only the token, so a file edited by hand (a shorter rule, the token kept) was applied and armed at once. The operator's plan commands got their own tests, and say the rule is paused when the record went but the plan change failed.

### Review of comment retention's fifth version

Safe to merge after five small fixes, each from a failing test: a new rule's confirmation now runs whatever its R2 write answered (a write can land and still throw), and catches its own failures, logging `comment_retention_guard_write_failed` and answering with the save's real result; the residual above was widened to every failed guard write, with the manual fix; the refusal while the guard cannot be read or written carries its own detail (`error.details.reason: comment_retention_guard`), so the Policies page says the rule could not be changed and nothing was saved; a removal while the record holds another token is a test, and so are the unreadable shapes of a record and a confirmation for a workspace that is gone. The date beside a kept rule is always set (`commentTiming`), so the fallback for a missing one was removed.

A committed test asserts these plans through `explain query plan` on the test D1, and fails if the unary plus is removed.
2. **The property test did not catch the removal of its own fixes.** It now runs four seeds, ends most sequences with a removal whose job is lost, counts both kinds of stale cap, and checks what the run archived; each fix removed alone fails it. 3. **The sweep could stall behind one large owner,** and workspaces with a maximum outside the scan's page took its owner slots: bounded per run by artifacts now (above), and "sets a maximum" is decided for every workspace in the statement when the databases are one.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Counting from creation.** It would archive a living document published yesterday because it was created a year ago. The last publish is what "life" means for a document that changes; creation would be the stricter option only for a compliance rule about first publication, which nobody asked for.
- **The pin wins.** Then any editor could exempt anything from the admins' rule.
- **A pinned artifact still reaches the maximum.** It breaks the invariant that a pinned artifact has no expiry (every reader of `expires_at` relies on it) for a pin that then means nothing.
- **Refusing to restore past the maximum.** An admin restoring an archived artifact is making the same deliberate choice as publishing it again; refusing it only sends them through a publish.
- **Archiving at the effective date without moving deadlines in the notice period.** Artifacts already past the maximum would be archived the minute it takes effect with no expiry notice to their owners.
- **A new deletion or archive path for the policy.** Archive is already one transition with its triggers, slots, search and audit; a second path would drift from it.
- **Capping in the delivery path.** It would add a read per delivery request for the policy; the cap lives in `expires_at`, which delivery already reads.
