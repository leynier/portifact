# 0017 SCIM provisioning for workspaces, switched off

- Status: accepted for the first delivery (`Users` only); `Groups` and role mapping follow in a second delivery
- Date: 2026-11-10
- Builds on: [0004](0004-workspace-model.md) (workspace model, section 6 and decision point 3), [0010](0010-workspace-access-modes.md) (verified email domains), [0011](0011-service-tokens.md) (how a workspace credential is modeled), [0014](0014-workspace-groups.md) (groups), [0016](0016-workspace-sso.md) (single sign-on and `workspace_removals`)

## Context

Premium plan E2 lets an Enterprise workspace's identity provider (Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace and others) add and remove the workspace's people over SCIM 2.0 (feature `scim` in `PLANS`). Decision 0004 found that `@better-auth/scim` cannot run on D1 (it needs native transactions) and chose bearer-only routes over the services, ending sessions with Better Auth's `internalAdapter.deleteUserSessions`. It left two questions to the maintainer: whether a directory may create a dormant account (open question 2, a legal one), and whether a directory exempts the human check (open question 3).

## Decision

1. **SCIM never creates a Portifact account.** "No account exists without the current terms accepted" ([legal.md](../legal.md)) stands, and the human check is never skipped, so open questions 2 and 3 do not arise in this delivery. A `POST /Users` for an address without an account creates a **pending membership**: a `workspace_invitations` row with `source = 'directory'`, holding a seat like any invitation, that becomes a membership when the person first signs in (through single sign-on or any other method) with that verified address, after the terms hook let the session through (`claimDirectoryMemberships`, called from the session `create.after` hook in `apps/app/src/auth/auth.ts`, only with `SCIM_ENABLED`). The pending row lasts `SCIM.pendingDays` (90) from the directory's last write, which renews it; then the maintenance scan drops it (`pruneExpiredInvitations`) and, once nothing links the directory's copy of the person to an account, that copy too (`pruneDirectoryUsers`).
2. **An existing account joins at once, and is told.** If an account with the address exists, the person is added as a member in the same batch (`member.joined` with `via: "directory"`), and the same batch writes a `directory_notice` outbox job: the jobs Worker sends that account one email (in its language, from `packages/emails`) saying its organization's directory added it to the workspace, with a link to the workspace and to its Members page to leave. The job claims its row before sending and puts it back on a failed send, skips suppressed addresses and people removed since, stops under the notice capacity (well before sign-in codes), counts the email, and reaches one person at most once per workspace a day (`rate_limits`, counted at send time). A pending row never sends it; a person without an account gets nothing unless the workspace turned invitation emails on. The rule and its limits: the workspace proved the domain (decision 0010), so its directory speaks for who at that domain belongs to the workspace, as its single sign-on does (decision 0016); every Portifact account's address is verified; and joining a workspace only gives the person what the workspace shares with its members, never anything of theirs to the workspace. **A directory acts only on addresses at the workspace's verified email domains** (exact match, no subdomains): a create, or any change of `userName`, to another address is refused with `400 invalidValue` whatever `active` says, and an account is reached through its address only while that address's domain is verified for this workspace (re-checked inside the batch). A deactivation of an unchanged address is still accepted after the domain lapsed only when the directory's row is already linked to the account by an earlier valid provisioning (`scim_users.user_id`), so a lapsed domain never keeps a provisioned person in; an unlinked row then answers `400 invalidValue`, and a `DELETE` of it removes only the directory's own row and pending membership. A pending person and a member look the same to the directory except `active`, which the directory itself set: no response, status or timing branch tells the directory whether an address has a Portifact account.
3. **Invitation emails are off by default.** A workspace's admins can turn on the invitation email for people provisioned without an account (`workspace_scim.invitation_emails`). Off by default because identity providers already tell people about new apps, a bulk sync of thousands would spend the month's email budget and the per-workspace invitation limit at once, and the person joins at their first sign-in either way. When on, the email is the invitation email (its link also works through the normal accept step), best effort: it respects the notice capacity, suppressions and the invitation rate limits, and a refusal or failure never undoes the pending membership.
4. **Deactivation and deletion.** `active: false` (PUT, or PATCH in Okta's or Entra's dialect) or `DELETE` removes the membership and, by cascade, the person's group memberships (each with a `workspace_group_member` tombstone), withdraws the directory's pending membership (`workspace_invitation` tombstone; an admin's invitation to the address is not the directory's to withdraw), and, **only when the account was a member**, writes the `workspace_removals` row so single sign-on does not bring the person back (decision 0016) and a `workspace_member` tombstone (`<workspace>:<user>`) so a restore cannot either. **A deprovisioning signs the person out of Portifact entirely**: every session of the account ends through Better Auth's API (`internalAdapter.deleteUserSessions`, so the hooks run and the OAuth tokens bound to the sessions, the CLI's and MCP clients' included, go). Not just the workspace, because a session cannot be scoped to one workspace: an open tab or a bound token may already hold the workspace's content, and the person's organization asked for it to stop now. The person signs in again at once for their personal space and other workspaces. **It happens once per actual removal**: the batch that deletes a membership of this workspace sets `scim_users.sessions_pending`, the sessions end after the batch and the mark is cleared; a deactivation that removes nobody (the person already out, a repeat, a retry that already succeeded) ends nothing, and a retry after a failure to end them (`503`) ends them exactly for that removal. It revokes nothing else of the person's account and never deletes it: their personal space is theirs. `DELETE` ends the sessions before it deletes the directory's copy (with a `scim_user` tombstone), so a failure leaves a row the directory's retry finds again.
5. **The last admin.** The existing rule and trigger apply: deactivating or deleting the workspace's last admin is refused with `400 mutability` and a detail that says why, and nothing is written.
6. **Roles.** SCIM adds people as `member`, and never changes a member's role (an existing member keeps theirs). Mapping a directory group to `editor` or `admin` arrives with `Groups` in the second delivery, only through a mapping admins configure; a directory payload never sets a role directly, never creates the first admin and never demotes the last one.
7. **Removals by hand win over routine syncs.** An admin removing a member or withdrawing a pending membership, or the person leaving, marks the directory's copy (`scim_users.removed_at`, with `removal` saying `admin` or `self`). A routine sync (a PUT or PATCH that keeps `active: true`) does not undo either. An **explicit** re-provisioning does: turning `active` from false to true lifts an admin's removal (the directory's admin saying, in so many words, that the person belongs again), but never the person's own leaving; only a new `POST` after the directory deleted the user lifts that. A directory can therefore still bring back someone who left, explicitly, which is what a company's directory is for; it cannot do it by toggling, so a person who left is not pulled back by every sync and is told each time they are added (point 2). The Portifact admin's removal holds against everything else, including single sign-on.
8. **Seats.** Members plus pending invitations, each address counted once (an admin's invitation and the directory's pending row for the same address hold one seat, and neither counts for an address that is already a member); checked inside the batch that takes one, together with the plan row read (`seatLimit`). Beyond the seats the directory gets `403` with a detail, and nothing is written (the directory's copy of the user included).
9. **An admin's invitation stays the admin's.** A directory never changes an invitation an admin sent: not its source, its role or its expiry (the unique index on `workspace_invitations` includes `source`). Provisioning the same address creates the directory's own pending row beside it, always `member`, without a second seat. At sign-in the directory's row makes the person a member; the admin's invitation still needs the person's acceptance to give its role, and accepting it upgrades a `member` to that role (`member.role_changed` by the person).
10. **Failed authentications.** Each failed authentication at `/scim/v2` (no or malformed bearer, an unknown, revoked or expired token) is counted atomically per IP (`cf-connecting-ip`) in `rate_limits`: at most 20 are answered `401` in 10 minutes, the rest `429` with `Retry-After: 600`. Keyed by IP only, and a request that authenticates is never counted or refused by it, so nobody can lock a workspace's real directory out. A failed request costs at most two D1 calls (the token lookup and the counter).
11. **The credential.** A separate table `scim_tokens` (global), not a `kind` on `service_tokens`: every path that resolves an actor reads `service_tokens` (`actorOn`, `resolveOwnerScope`, `listUserWorkspaces`), and a SCIM token must never resolve to a role on artifacts; keeping it out of that table makes that true by construction. ID `sc_` and 32 hex characters; secret `pfsc_` and 43 base64url characters (256 bits), shown once, only its SHA-256 stored, a `portifact-scim-token` rule in `.gitleaks.toml`; an expiry of 1 to 366 days (365 by default); `last_used_at` written at most once an hour; at most 2 per workspace so one can replace another without a gap. Created and revoked by admins (role action `scim`) through the dashboard session only (`/api/v1/workspaces/:slug/scim/tokens`), never a bearer route, MCP tool or CLI command. Creating needs `scim` in the workspace's plan; revoking never does. Revoking writes a `scim_token` tombstone; the operator finds and revokes a leaked one (`find-scim-token`, `revoke-scim-token`).
12. **Authentication.** Only `/scim/v2/*` accepts a SCIM token, and it accepts nothing else: cookies are never read, a service token, an OAuth token or a made-up secret answers `401` with `WWW-Authenticate: Bearer realm="SCIM"`. A SCIM token sent to `/api/v1` or `/mcp` is an unknown OAuth token there (`401`). Unknown, revoked or of a deleted workspace: `401`; expired: `401` with a detail; the workspace's plan without `scim` (lapsed, removed, lowered): `403`. The token names the workspace, so one base URL serves every workspace, and resource IDs are random (`su_` and 32 hex characters, never derived from an address): another workspace's ID answers `404`.
13. **The protocol.** By hand over the services (`packages/domain/src/scim.ts` for parsing, PATCH and resources; `packages/services/src/scim.ts` for the use cases; `apps/app/src/scim.ts` as the adapter), at `https://app.portifact.com/scim/v2`, which is what an admin pastes as the base URL (Okta) or tenant URL (Entra), with the token as the bearer token. Served: `ServiceProviderConfig`, `ResourceTypes`, `Schemas`, and `Users` (GET by ID, list with `filter=userName eq "…"` or `externalId eq "…"` and `startIndex`/`count` capped at 100, POST, PUT, PATCH with `add`/`replace`/`remove`, path-less replaces, Entra's capitalized ops, `active` as `"True"`/`"False"`, dotted keys and value-filtered paths that are ignored, and DELETE). `userName` is the email address, lowercased; `externalId` and the display names are kept and echoed back only to the directory; everything else a directory sends (emails, phone numbers, Entra's enterprise extension) is accepted and not stored. Responses are `application/scim+json` with the RFC's error body (`schemas`, `status` as a string, `scimType`, `detail`); `ETag` is not implemented and the configuration says so. A repeated `POST` for the same `userName` answers `409 uniqueness`; PUT, PATCH and DELETE are idempotent.
14. **Writes.** Guarded global batches: each write claims the directory's row with a per-request `write_id` in its first statement, and every other statement is conditioned on that claim, so of two concurrent writes only one applies (the other answers `409`). The claim itself holds only while the token exists and has not expired, the address's domain is still verified, the plan row is the one read and, when the write takes a seat, a seat is free. `stillAdmin` does not apply: a token is nobody.
15. **Limits.** 300 requests a minute per token and 600 per workspace in `rate_limits` (`429` with `Retry-After: 60`); pages of at most 100; bodies of at most 64 KiB (`413`); at most 50 PATCH operations; at most 5,000 directory users per workspace, which bounds every list and count to one workspace's index.
16. **Audit.** A new actor type, `directory`, stored by `auditEvent` with the SCIM token's ID (never a secret) and shown by the token's current label (`{label} (directory)`), or as a revoked SCIM token once it is gone. New kinds: `scim_token.created` (the days), `scim_token.revoked`, `member.provisioned` (role and the address's domain, never the address) and `scim.changed` (the setting). Existing kinds where they fit: `member.joined` with `via: "directory"` (by the directory, or by the person when they claim a pending membership at sign-in), `member.removed` and `member.invitation_revoked` with the directory as actor. All are global kinds.
17. **Switched off.** Nothing is reachable unless the app Worker has `SCIM_ENABLED="true"`, which no `wrangler.jsonc` sets (the e2e stack and the test suites do): every `/scim/v2` path answers `404`, the settings routes `capacity_unavailable`, the Members page hides the section and sign-ins claim nothing. `scim` is not in `AVAILABLE_PLAN_FEATURES`.

## D1 calls per request (worst case)

Every SCIM request first authenticates (one indexed read of the token with the plan, and at most one `last_used_at` write an hour) and counts two rate limits (two upserts): 4. A failed authentication costs at most 2 (the read and the per-IP counter). A write that removed a membership adds one write to clear `sessions_pending` after the sessions ended.

| Request                        | After authentication                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Total                                              |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Discovery documents            | none                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | 4                                                  |
| `GET /Users`, `GET /Users/:id` | a count and a page (bounded by the workspace's index), or one read                                                                                                                                                                                                    | 6                                                  |
| `POST`, `PUT`, `PATCH`         | the user, the verified domains, the uniqueness check, the linked member, the account, the membership, the pending row, the plan row, the batch and a read back: at most 10; a refusal adds at most 5 to explain itself; the invitation email (only when on) at most 7 | 21, plus Better Auth's own calls when sessions end |
| `DELETE`                       | the user, the linked member, the removal batch, a read back, the deleting batch                                                                                                                                                                                       | 9, plus Better Auth's own calls to end sessions    |
| A sign-in with `SCIM_ENABLED`  | the account and its pending directory rows; per pending row (at most one per workspace, 10 read) the plan row and one batch                                                                                                                                           | 2 when nothing is pending                          |

Ending sessions is Better Auth's `deleteUserSessions`, which reads the account's sessions and deletes them with its hooks.

## Interplay

- **Single sign-on.** A person provisioned without an account who signs in through the workspace's single sign-on is claimed by the session hook (or by `joinWorkspaceThroughSso`, which consumes the pending row when the account is new or the provider vouched for the address) and gets the role the directory asked for. A person the directory deactivated cannot come back through single sign-on (`workspace_removals`). An admin's manual removal is not undone by the next sync (point 7).
- **Groups.** Not served in this delivery: the dashboard's groups are unaffected, and `/scim/v2/Groups` answers `404`, which Okta and Entra treat as "groups not supported" when `ResourceTypes` does not list them. The second delivery maps workspace groups to SCIM groups and marks directory-managed groups so the dashboard refuses editing them.
- **Workspace policies and email domains.** Nothing is bypassed: provisioning needs a verified domain, re-checked in the batch; a domain that lapses stops new provisioning at once (pending rows already written wait for the sign-in like any invitation); policies bind the members SCIM adds like any other.
- **Account deletion.** The directory's copy keeps the address and loses the account link (`scim_users.user_id` is set null); the memberships go with the account. The directory's next write treats the address as one without an account.
- **Workspace deletion.** Tokens, directory users and settings go with the workspace (foreign keys); a `scim_token` tombstone is unnecessary for them because `applyTombstones` deletes every workspace that has a tombstone.
- **D1 Time Travel restore.** `exportTombstones` carries `scim_token`, `scim_user`, `workspace_invitation` and `workspace_member` markers; `applyTombstones` deletes the revoked tokens, the deleted directory users and the withdrawn pending memberships again, and memberships the directory ended that are older than their tombstone (a later membership of the same person survives), writing their `workspace_removals` row again so single sign-on does not let them back.

## Alternatives rejected

- **`@better-auth/scim`.** Cannot run on D1 (decision 0004, section 2).
- **A `kind` column on `service_tokens`.** Every actor resolution would have to exclude it, and one missed path would give a directory a role on the workspace's artifacts.
- **Dormant accounts created by the directory.** A legal question (decision 0004, open question 2); the pending membership gives the same experience without an account nobody accepted terms for.
- **Matching a pending membership by the address at every request.** The claim at sign-in is one indexed read when nothing is pending, and no request path pays for it.

## What was not verified

No real directory was used: the tests are table tests built from RFC 7643 and 7644 and from the request shapes Okta and Entra document, an HTTP suite against the Worker, and an e2e spec that plays the directory against the stack. Okta's and Entra's validators, their sync rates, Google Workspace and the PATCH dialects as they really arrive must be checked before turning it on ([releasing.md](../releasing.md), "SCIM").
