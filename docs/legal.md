# Legal pages and terms acceptance

Portifact publishes three legal documents on `portifact.com` and records, for every account, which version of them it accepted. A lawyer reviewed the texts on 2026-11-08, as plan section 17 requires before registration opens; have any material change reviewed again before it ships.

## Pages

| Path              | Source                         | Covers                                                                                                                                    |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `/terms`          | `apps/web/terms.html`          | Operator, 18+ eligibility, accounts, quotas, content license, lifecycle, enforcement, disclaimers, liability, Florida law, changes        |
| `/privacy`        | `apps/web/privacy.html`        | Data collected (including the Turnstile human check), uses, cookies, processors (Cloudflare, Google, GitHub), retention, rights, children |
| `/acceptable-use` | `apps/web/acceptable-use.html` | Prohibited content and behavior, coding agents, abuse reports, enforcement                                                                |

- They are static Vite entries like the landing (`apps/web/vite.config.ts`), load only the shared stylesheet and no script, and use design-system token utilities only. `apps/app/src/site.ts` serves them on `portifact.com` with the strict `PAGE_CSP`, `no-cache`, `nosniff` and `X-Frame-Options: DENY`; the app host answers 404 for them.
- Each page shows the version and the effective date. `apps/web/src/legal.test.ts` checks that both match `TERMS_VERSION`, so the pages and the code cannot drift.
- The pages were written once from a generator that is not kept; edit the HTML directly and keep the structure (one `h1`, a table of contents whose links match the `section` ids, captioned tables, the legal footer with `aria-current`).
- The landing footer and the footers of `/login`, `/device`, `/consent` and `/report` link all three pages; the landing footer also links the report form and the abuse address. The sign-in notice links them too (see "Acceptance at sign-in").
- English is the only authoritative text. The localized copies (`/<code>/terms`, `/<code>/privacy` and `/<code>/acceptable-use` for every language besides English, such as `/es/terms` or `/zh-cn/privacy`, prerendered by `apps/web/src/i18n/site.ts`) translate only the chrome around `main` (title, header, footer, language switcher), keep `main` in English with `lang="en"`, show a note in the page's language that the English text is the one that applies, and point their canonical link at the English page. Never put translation markers inside `main` (`src/i18n/site.test.ts` fails) and never bump `TERMS_VERSION` for a translation. The sign-in notice on the login page is translated with the same conditions and the same three links (`src/auth/i18n/catalog.test.ts`).
- Write them in plain language and describe only what the product does today; mark planned features as planned. When a feature ships or a rule changes (quotas, retention, cookies, processors), update the affected page in the same change.
- The report form (`app.portifact.com/report`, linked from the landing footer, from `/__portifact/report` on every artifact, from the "Report this artifact" footer of rendered Markdown and TXT documents and from the review viewer), suspension and its notice, and the dashboard's data export and account deletion are live and described in the Terms ("Reports and enforcement", ending the account), the Acceptable Use Policy ("Reporting abuse", enforcement) and the Privacy Policy (rights, retention of deleted accounts and abuse reports). Keep them in sync with [operations.md](operations.md) and the account deletion rules in [security.md](security.md).

Contact addresses used by the pages: `abuse@portifact.com`, `privacy@portifact.com` and `legal@portifact.com`. Cloudflare Email Routing on `portifact.com` forwards all three to the operator's inbox (set up 2026-11-08); a new address needs its own routing rule.

## Acceptance at sign-in

No account is created and no session is issued for an account that has not accepted the current `TERMS_VERSION` (`packages/contracts/src/terms-version.ts`), whatever the method: Google, GitHub or email code.

Consent is collected with a sign-in-wrap: there is no checkbox, and choosing a sign-in method is the acceptance. The conditions that make it valid are part of the product and are tested (`apps/web/src/auth/login.test.ts`, `e2e/tests/sign-in.spec.ts`):

- The notice is conspicuous and shown before any action: a bordered block in full-contrast text the size of the buttons' labels, right above the methods, never collapsed or behind a link. It reads "By continuing with Google, GitHub or an email code, you confirm that you are 18 or older, you agree to the Terms of Service and the Acceptable Use Policy, and you have read the Privacy Policy", with each document linked (opening in a new tab).
- It sits next to every sign-in button: the Google, GitHub, "Email me a code" and "Sign in" (code step) buttons follow it on the same card, and each names it in `aria-describedby`, so a screen reader reads the notice with the button. It stays on screen in the code step.
- The action records the acceptance: every method posts the acceptance before it starts, and the Worker records the accepted version and time per account, exactly as below.
- The review screen for accounts on an older version uses the same pattern: "By selecting Agree and continue, you confirm..." above the button that records it.

Sign-in-wrap is the maintainer's choice of 2026-11-08 instead of the earlier required checkbox; the lawyer reviewed the texts with the checkbox, so have them confirm sign-in-wrap is enough before relying on it. The Terms say "You accept these terms when you continue with Google, GitHub or an email code at sign-in, below the notice that says so"; that sentence changed with the mechanism without a new `TERMS_VERSION`, because what users accept did not change.

1. Before any method, the login page posts `{ version, acceptTerms: true, confirmAdult: true }` (`TermsAcceptanceInput`) to `POST /api/auth/accept-terms`, a Portifact endpoint in the auth allowlist with the exact-Origin check and a per-IP limit. The body states what the notice says; the page sends it only when the user chose a method.
2. Without a session, the Worker answers with `__Host-pf_terms` (`Secure; HttpOnly; SameSite=Lax; Path=/`, 30 minutes): the version and the acceptance time, HMAC-signed with a key derived from `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`. It survives the provider redirects because the callback is a top-level navigation.
3. Better Auth database hooks (`apps/app/src/auth/terms.ts`) enforce it. `user.create.before` refuses to create an account without a valid, current, unexpired cookie and stores `terms_version` and `terms_accepted_at` on the new user. `session.create.before` refuses a session for an account whose stored version is not current unless the cookie is present, and then records it. Refusals are `APIError` `FORBIDDEN` with code `terms_not_accepted`: the email-code sign-in answers 403, and provider callbacks redirect to `/login?error=terms_not_accepted`. `session.create.after` clears the cookie.
4. With a session, the endpoint records the acceptance on that account directly, keeping the time of an earlier acceptance of the same version.
5. Sessions that already exist are checked too: the dashboard routes, the `/api/v1` session lookup and `/read/<id>` treat a session of an account without the current version as no session, so the visitor lands on `/login`, which shows "Review the terms to continue" (the notice with "Agree and continue", or sign out) instead of forwarding to `next`.

The Privacy Policy also describes the email suppression list (undeliverable address records with their retention) and the daily email counts; keep them in sync with `SUPPRESSION_TTL_MS` (`packages/domain/src/email.ts`) and "Identity" in [security.md](security.md). They were added on 2026-11-08 without a new `TERMS_VERSION`: they describe processing that protects delivery for everyone and changes no one's rights.

The Privacy Policy says that owners get an expiry notice a few days before their artifacts expire, at most one a day, unless they turn it off in Settings, and that undeliverable addresses get none; keep it in sync with "Expiry notices" in [operations.md](operations.md). The policy announced these notices as planned since its first version; they went live without a new `TERMS_VERSION`, because they use the address already kept for sign-in, only for the service, with an opt-out, and change no one's rights.

The Privacy Policy and the Terms say who sees a comment: its artifact's creator always, and the other people with access only as the creator's comment visibility allows (by default, only the threads they or the creator started). This narrowed what other readers see when comment visibility shipped, on the version of 2026-11-08 without a new `TERMS_VERSION`, because it only reduces who reads a comment and changes no one's rights; keep it in sync with "Comment visibility" in [security.md](security.md).

The Privacy Policy describes the access log (what is recorded, that only the artifact's creator sees it, 30 days, erasure with the artifact or the visitor's account); keep it in sync with "Access logs" in [security.md](security.md) and `ACCESS_LOG` in `packages/contracts`. Access logs were listed as planned, with the same 30 days, since the policy's first version; they went live on the version of 2026-11-08 without a new `TERMS_VERSION` pending the maintainer's decision, recorded in the pull request that shipped them. Since the viewer with the Portifact bar, only `restricted` artifacts record openings: visits of public and `authenticated` artifacts are never recorded, even with a session, because any site can send a signed-in visitor to them; the policy says so, which narrows what is collected and needs no new acceptance.

New accounts also need a solved human check (Cloudflare Turnstile, `__Host-pf_human`), enforced next to the acceptance in the same `user.create.before` hook; see "Human check" in [security.md](security.md). The Privacy Policy lists it under what is collected, the cookie table and Cloudflare's processing; keep them in sync if the check changes.

The Privacy Policy lists the two preference cookies (`__Secure-pf_theme`, `__Secure-pf_lang`) that `portifact.com` and `app.portifact.com` share, and says they never reach `portifact.dev`; keep it in sync with "Cookies and sessions" in [security.md](security.md). They were added on the version of 2026-11-08 without a new `TERMS_VERSION`: they are strictly functional, hold only the theme or language the visitor chose and no identifier, are set only when the visitor chooses, and change no one's rights.

The Terms and the Privacy Policy describe a fixed lifecycle: an artifact expires 30 days after its last publish or renewal and is kept archived for 6 months before it is deleted. Since pins and archive length by plan (premium plan P2), both depend on the owner's plan: a paid plan can pin an artifact so it does not expire, and an archive is kept for the plan's `archiveMonths` when it is archived (12 on paid plans). The free plan, and every account without a plan, still behaves exactly as the pages say, so the texts were left unchanged and `TERMS_VERSION` was not bumped; change them in one go with the other plan-dependent terms when paid plans are offered.

The Privacy Policy's retention table says previous versions of an artifact are kept "up to two, for 7 days after a newer one is published". Since version retention by plan (premium plan P3) that is the free plan's value: Pro keeps up to 50 for 90 days and Team up to 100 for a year (`PLANS`), and a lower plan prunes down to its own values. Until then the count of two was not enforced (only the seven days were); it is now, so the free plan matches the page exactly. The page and `TERMS_VERSION` are unchanged; describe the plan-dependent retention with the other paid-plan terms.

`terms_version` and `terms_accepted_at` are Better Auth `additionalFields` with `input: false`, so no request body can set them; `get-session` returns them to the login page.

Bearer tokens issued to the CLI and MCP clients before an account accepted are not revoked; the account is asked at its next browser sign-in or dashboard visit.

## Pending changes to the legal texts

Changes that shipped and that the legal pages do not describe yet, to be written in one go with the next update of the texts (and a `TERMS_VERSION` bump if the change is material):

- Comment notices (premium plan P5): a new kind of email. The Privacy page must say that on artifacts whose owner's plan includes them, Portifact emails the owner side and the people who took part in a thread a digest of new comment activity, at most every 10 minutes; that the email holds the artifact's name, counts of new threads, replies and resolutions and links, never what was written or who wrote it; that it is on by default and turned off in the settings, per artifact with a mute, or with the one-click unsubscribe link in every email; and what is stored for it: pending activity per recipient and thread (IDs, counts and a time, deleted once the digest is scheduled, with the thread, the artifact or the account), the mutes, the setting and when it last changed, all part of the account export. The Terms may also mention it among the paid features.
- Share links (premium plan P4, [decision 0008](decisions/0008-share-links.md)). The texts must say:
  - **Terms.** An owner on a paid plan can create links that let anyone holding them read an artifact without an account; the owner is responsible for who gets a link and its password, revokes a link to stop it, and links stop while the plan does not include them. Visitors through a link accept the Acceptable Use Policy by using the artifact, like any anonymous visitor of a public artifact.
  - **Privacy.** For a link Portifact keeps its label, creation, optional expiry, a hash of the token and of the optional password, and the day it was last opened; visitors through a link are not identified: the owner's access log records only that the link was used in a 30-minute window (kept 30 days), and nothing about the visitor (no IP, no account). Opening a link with a password may load Cloudflare Turnstile after repeated wrong passwords, and the visitor's IP is used for rate limits in short-lived counters, as on the sign-in page.
  - **Acceptable Use.** Share links may not be used to distribute content the policy forbids, to get around a suspension, or to publish material openly while pretending it is private; a suspension ends every link to the artifact, and reports about a link are handled like reports about the artifact.

  These are material changes for the Privacy Policy (new data and a new processor use), so bump `TERMS_VERSION` then.
- Custom domains (premium plan P7, [decision 0006](decisions/0006-custom-domains.md)), once they are switched on. The texts must say:
  - **Terms.** An owner on a paid plan can serve an artifact from a domain they control; they must keep the TXT record that proves it, and Portifact removes the domain (never the artifact) when the proof is gone for three daily checks, when the plan no longer includes it, when the artifact is suspended or deleted, or when someone else proves control of the name. Portifact serves the artifact there through Cloudflare, which issues the certificate; the owner is responsible for their DNS, and the artifact keeps its Portifact address.
  - **Privacy.** For a custom domain Portifact keeps the hostname, the artifact and owner it serves, the verification value and when it was verified and checked; hostnames are shared with Cloudflare to issue certificates, are global data even for a workspace in another region, and are part of the account export. Removed domains are deleted.
  - **Acceptable Use.** A custom domain may not impersonate another person, brand or Portifact itself, and the policy's rules apply to everything served on it; abuse on a customer's domain is reported like abuse on the artifact and ends with the domain's removal.

- Artifact analytics and the access log by plan (premium plan P8, [decision 0007](decisions/0007-artifact-analytics.md)). The Privacy page must say: owners on a plan with analytics see, per artifact, page views per UTC day and how many of them came with a signed-in visitor, as counts that identify nobody; for artifacts shared with listed people, they also see how many distinct signed-in readers opened it per day, from the access log, which already names those readers to them; anonymous visitors are never identified (no IP address, cookie or identifier is stored for a view); the access log and the counts are kept 30 days on the free plan and up to one year on paid plans, and after a plan change the shorter retention applies to what was kept; counts go with the artifact and openings with the artifact or the visitor's account, as today. The section on the access log ("30 days") must change with it. Keeping data that names visitors for up to a year instead of 30 days is a material change by the rules below: it goes in the wave's single `TERMS_VERSION` bump (premium plan L4), after a lawyer's review, before the first paid plan is granted. The Terms may also list analytics among the paid features. Until then only owners granted a paid plan by the operator keep openings longer than 30 days.

- Webhooks (premium plan P6): a customer-chosen destination receiving activity data. The Privacy page must say that an owner on a plan with webhooks can have Portifact send, to an HTTPS address the owner chooses (their own server, Slack or Discord), events about their artifacts: the artifact's name and links, event types, times, counts, version numbers, the access mode and the display name of the owner's member who acted; never comment text, quotes or readers' email addresses; that this data then leaves Portifact under the owner's responsibility, and for an EU workspace leaves the region ([decision 0005](decisions/0005-regions.md), section 6); and what is stored: the destination (sealed), the host, the chosen events, pending events (IDs and counts, deleted when delivered) and a 7-day delivery log (time, outcome and HTTP status), deleted with the webhook or the account and listed, without the address or secret, in the account export. The Terms should say that the owner is responsible for the destinations they choose and must not use webhooks to send requests to systems they are not allowed to, and may list webhooks among the paid features.

- Owner branding (premium plan P9): owners on a paid plan can show their name, logo, accent color and link on the viewer's bar above their artifacts. The texts must say:
  - **Terms.** The owner is responsible for having the rights to the name, logo and link they show (trademarks); a brand may not imitate Portifact or another person or organization; Portifact may suspend a brand without notice, which brings back the default bar, and the bar always says the page is hosted on Portifact. Branding stops while the plan does not include it and the brand is kept until the owner deletes it.
  - **Acceptable Use.** Impersonation through a brand (another company's name or logo, Portifact's own, or a look-alike) and links that lead to phishing or malware are forbidden, and reports about a branded bar are handled like reports about the artifact.
  - **Privacy.** For a brand Portifact keeps the name, color, link and the logo file (counted in the owner's storage), part of the account export and deleted with the brand or the account; visitors' browsers load the logo from the artifact's own address, with no referrer and no cookie.

  The Terms and Acceptable Use changes are material (new obligations for owners), so they go in the wave's single `TERMS_VERSION` bump.

## Changing the texts

1. Edit the pages. A change to wording that does not alter anyone's rights can keep the version.
2. For a material change, set `TERMS_VERSION` to the new effective date and update the version and date on all three pages (the test fails until they match). Every account is then asked to accept again at its next sign-in or dashboard visit.
3. Announce material changes in advance, as the pages promise.
