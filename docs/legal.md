# Legal pages and terms acceptance

Portifact publishes four legal documents on `portifact.com` and records, for every account, which version of the terms it accepted. A lawyer reviewed the version of 2026-11-08. The version of 2026-11-13 was prepared and reviewed without legal counsel, by the maintainer's decision of 2026-11-12; its product statements were checked against the source at the commit of the change that introduced it. Whoever changes them must check every claim against the code and the documents linked here, and follow "Operating decisions".

## Acceptance at sign-in

No account is created and no session is issued for an account that has not accepted the current `TERMS_VERSION` (`packages/contracts/src/terms-version.ts`), whatever the method: Google, GitHub, email code or a workspace's single sign-on ([decision 0016](decisions/0016-workspace-sso.md), switched off until its release checks pass).

Consent is collected with a sign-in-wrap: there is no checkbox, and choosing a sign-in method is the acceptance. The conditions that make it valid are part of the product and are tested (`apps/web/src/auth/login.test.ts`, `e2e/tests/sign-in.spec.ts`):

- The notice is conspicuous and shown before any action: a bordered block in full-contrast text the size of the buttons' labels, right above the methods, never collapsed or behind a link. It reads "By continuing with Google, GitHub or an email code, you confirm that you are 18 or older, you agree to the Terms of Service and the Acceptable Use Policy, and you have read the Privacy Policy", with each document linked (opening in a new tab).
- When the address entered belongs to a workspace's single sign-on, the login page shows the "Continue with single sign-on" step and swaps the notice, in the same place and form, for one that reads "By continuing with Google, GitHub, an email code or single sign-on, you confirm..." with the same three links; both of that step's buttons name it in `aria-describedby`, and choosing either posts the acceptance first. The provider's callback is a top-level navigation, so the acceptance cookie reaches it as for Google and GitHub.
- It sits next to every sign-in button: the Google, GitHub, "Email me a code" and "Sign in" (code step) buttons follow it on the same card, and each names it in `aria-describedby`, so a screen reader reads the notice with the button. It stays on screen in the code step.
- The action records the acceptance: every method posts the acceptance before it starts, and the Worker records the accepted version and time per account, exactly as below.
- The review screen for accounts on an older version uses the same pattern: "By selecting Agree and continue, you confirm..." above the button that records it.

Sign-in-wrap is the maintainer's choice of 2026-11-08 instead of the earlier required checkbox. It rests on the notice-and-assent test US courts apply to sign-in-wraps (a conspicuous notice next to the button and an action that records assent; Meyer v. Uber, 2d Cir. 2017; Berman v. Freedom Financial, 9th Cir. 2022), which the conditions above keep; existing accounts re-accept with "Agree and continue", a clickwrap. The Terms say "You accept these terms when you continue with Google, GitHub or an email code at sign-in, below the notice that says so".

The "Review the terms to continue" screen's text (`login.termsUpdateBody`) links the Terms' "What changed in this version" section (`/terms#what-changed`, opening in a new tab), so a re-acceptance is informed. Keep that section's id.

The flow:

1. Before any method, the login page posts `{ version, acceptTerms: true, confirmAdult: true }` (`TermsAcceptanceInput`) to `POST /api/auth/accept-terms`, a Portifact endpoint in the auth allowlist with the exact-Origin check and a per-IP limit. The body states what the notice says; the page sends it only when the user chose a method.
2. Without a session, the Worker answers with `__Host-pf_terms` (`Secure; HttpOnly; SameSite=Lax; Path=/`, 30 minutes): the version and the acceptance time, HMAC-signed with a key derived from `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`. It survives the provider redirects because the callback is a top-level navigation.
3. Better Auth database hooks (`apps/app/src/auth/terms.ts`) enforce it. `user.create.before` refuses to create an account without a valid, current, unexpired cookie and stores `terms_version` and `terms_accepted_at` on the new user; the `terms_acceptances_sign_up` trigger (migration 0068) adds the acceptance to the account's history in the same insert. `session.create.before` refuses a session for an account whose stored version is not current unless the cookie is present, and then records it with `recordTermsAcceptance`, as given at sign-in. Refusals are `APIError` `FORBIDDEN` with code `terms_not_accepted`: the email-code sign-in answers 403, and provider callbacks redirect to `/login?error=terms_not_accepted`. `session.create.after` clears the cookie.
4. With a session, the endpoint records the acceptance on that account directly ("Agree and continue" on the review screen), keeping the time of an earlier acceptance of the same version.
5. Sessions that already exist are checked too: the dashboard routes, the `/api/v1` session lookup and `/read/<id>` treat a session of an account without the current version as no session, so the visitor lands on `/login`, which shows "Review the terms to continue" (the notice with "Agree and continue", or sign out) instead of forwarding to `next`.

`terms_version` and `terms_accepted_at` are Better Auth `additionalFields` with `input: false`, so no request body can set them; `get-session` returns them to the login page. They are the fast path the hooks and gates read: the latest version the account accepted and when.

### History of accepted versions

`terms_acceptances` (global, `packages/db/src/schema.ts`) keeps every version each account accepted: the account, the version, when, and how (`action`): `sign_up` when choosing a sign-in method created the account, `sign_in` when an existing account chose one below the notice, `review` for "Agree and continue" on the review screen, and `backfill` for the acceptance an account held when the history started (migration 0068 copied it; how it was given was not recorded). One row per account and version: a later acceptance of the same version changes neither the row nor `users`. A row is never written apart from the acceptance it records: the sign-up row comes from a trigger on the insert of the account, and `recordTermsAcceptance` (`apps/app/src/auth/terms.ts`) updates `users` and inserts the row in one D1 batch, the row conditioned on `users` holding the version. The rows cascade from `users`, so account deletion removes them; the account export lists them (`account.termsAcceptances`). A D1 Time Travel restore needs nothing for them: the history is rewound with `users`, so the two agree, an account whose acceptance falls in the rewound window is simply asked again, and the tombstones of deleted accounts remove their rows by the same cascade.

### API and MCP clients

A person's OAuth token acts for them only under the current terms. The provider adds the version the token's account accepted to the token's claims when it validates it (`customAccessTokenClaims`, from the user row it already reads, so no read is added), and it is recorded on the request context with the credential (`termsAccepted`). An account without the current version may still leave and reduce, never act anew. The rule is `usableWithoutCurrentTerms` (`packages/domain/src/terms.ts`), shared by the API's gate (`apps/app/src/api/access.ts`, with each route named in `apps/app/src/api/terms-operations.ts`) and MCP's (`register` in `apps/app/src/mcp/tools.ts`, by the tool's name). It stays open for:

- Every operation of the read scopes, `artifacts:read` and `annotations:read` (the API's read routes, the MCP read tools and `tools/list`), and listing share links. This is deliberate and has no time limit: reading and downloading is how a person takes their data out before deciding (`portifact export`, `pull`, `annotations export`), and it reaches everything those scopes reach, including access logs and other people's comments on the account's artifacts, which the person could already read and which creates nothing new under terms they did not accept.
- The operations that only remove or reduce (`TERMS_OPEN_OPERATIONS`): deleting and archiving an artifact, unpinning it, removing readers or groups, revoking a share link, removing a custom domain, deleting one's own comment or a thread on one's own artifact, cancelling an approval review request, abandoning an upload, and deleting a webhook.
- Changes that can widen or narrow, only when they narrow, decided in the service from the request's credential: an access change (`accessChangeOnlyNarrows` in `updateAccess`: a mode whose readers are a subset of the current one's, removing readers or groups, turning indexing or embedding off, comments back to the owner only) and a webhook change (`webhookUpdateOnlyNarrows` in `updateWebhook`: turning it off).
- `GET /api/v1/me`, listing and revoking the account's own tokens (`/api/v1/tokens`, `portifact tokens`), token revocation at `/api/auth/oauth2/revoke` (`portifact logout`) and sign-out.

The same reason keeps reads, exports, deletion and access reduction out of the plan and budget gates (AGENTS.md, "Plans" and "Budget protection"). Everything that creates, publishes, widens, invites or sends is refused with `terms_not_accepted` (403) and a message from `termsRefusalMessage` that names where to accept (the app's origin, or the dashboard when a service refuses), says that no app or agent can accept for the person, what keeps working, and to update the CLI or open the dashboard when an older CLI reports it as unexpected. `apps/app/test/terms-operations.test.ts` classifies every `/api/v1` route that is not a GET and every MCP tool outside the read scopes, and fails for a new one until it is classified. The CLI exits 9 with the message; MCP answers a refused tool with the API's error body as the tool's result, with a sentence telling the agent to ask the user, so the agent never accepts for them. The same token works again as soon as the account accepts in the dashboard, without a new sign-in. Service tokens, SCIM tokens and share links do not act as a person and are not asked.

The dashboard is unchanged: a session without the current version counts as none for `/api/v1` and the dashboard pages, and the login page asks for the acceptance. The `/api/v1/account` endpoints (export, deletion, sessions, connected apps) answer such a session, but the dashboard pages that call them are reached only after accepting, and deleting the account needs a sign-in in the last 10 minutes (`FRESH_SESSION_MS`), which a person who will not accept cannot get, because every sign-in records an acceptance. So today a person who will not accept takes their data out and unpublishes with the CLI or an MCP client, and asks `privacy@portifact.com` to export or delete the account (the Privacy Policy's rights section); a way to leave from the review screen is a follow-up that needs a sign-in that proves the person without accepting.

Released CLIs from before this check do not know `terms_not_accepted`: they read the refusal as an unexpected response, print that the server sent a response the CLI did not expect and to try again, and exit 1 (calls that retry try again first). They do not crash, and their read commands keep working. A person who sees that message on every command should update the CLI, which then prints the instruction, or open the dashboard, which asks for the acceptance. Newer CLIs say the same in their unexpected-response message.

## Operating decisions

This document records operating decisions and publication requirements. It is publicly readable. Keep the legal pages factual and consistent with these decisions, and never write on a page that Portifact "follows" or "complies with" a law; describe what it does.

### Facts and decisions

- **Counsel.** A lawyer reviewed the version of 2026-11-08. The version of 2026-11-13 was prepared and reviewed without legal counsel, by the maintainer's decision of 2026-11-12, after two adversarial reviews; its product statements were checked against the source at the commit of the change that introduced it, and against the production configuration where noted ("What the pages state").
- **Operator.** An individual, administering the service remotely from North America. The country and address are not published. They are provided privately on request to customers needing the transfer clauses and to consumers entitled to the seller's details, before the order is concluded (the maintainer's final decision). Florida law and courts, subject to a consumer's mandatory law and home courts; no arbitration clause and no class waiver. A change of operator is announced by a new version and does not remove existing rights or liabilities.
- **Acceptance.** Sign-in-wrap for new accounts, clickwrap for re-acceptance, with a "What changed" section at the top of the Terms and the Privacy Policy. Visitors without an account follow the Acceptable Use Policy as a condition of access; browsing alone is not relied on for liability or indemnity.
- **This version's effective date.** Production has exactly two accounts, both the maintainer's, so the version of 2026-11-13 takes effect on its date with no advance notice. Every later materially adverse change gets the 30 days ("Changing the texts").
- **Paid plans.** Requested by email; the required information, including the written confirmation (plan, seats, currency, total with tax we must collect, dates, payment method, refund terms, and the withdrawal information for consumers), is sent before the customer becomes bound or pays; paid outside Portifact, prepaid, no automatic renewal, no stored payment method, fixed seats (a member of any role or a pending invitation takes one). Price changes are emailed and published 30 days before they can apply to a new period. The confirmation is part of the agreement and controls price, seats and period. Refunds: in full within 14 days of the first purchase as an extra benefit beside any statutory withdrawal right; pro rata by day when we end a plan without the customer's breach, withdraw a paid feature or shut down; billing errors in full; paid within 14 days to the original payment method (another only by agreement, with no fee). The operator's checklist is "Granting a paid plan" in [operations.md](operations.md).
- **Liability.** The greater of US$50 and 12 months' fees before the event, with indirect damages, lost profits, revenue and goodwill excluded (data loss is not excluded as such; the cap limits it). The cap never limits promised refunds or mandatory consumer remedies, and no exclusion or limit restricts liability or remedies under the standard contractual clauses, between their parties or toward data subjects. The disclaimer yields to express commitments and to what the law does not allow to be excluded. Indemnity only from business customers, only for claims caused by their content, infringement or breach, never for our own fault. Enterprise liability is negotiated in its signed agreement.
- **Ownership and control.** A workspace's customer controls the access, management and retention of its content; that does not transfer copyright or other ownership.
- **Roles.** Roles follow the activity: Portifact is the controller for account administration, sign-in, billing records, platform security and abuse handling (also when those records concern a workspace) and for personal spaces used for one's own purposes; the customer is the controller, or a processor with its controller's authorization, of the content and processing it directs, in a workspace or a personal space used for work, and Portifact its processor under the DPA, which applies without signature from the first processing. Notices under the DPA go to the customer's contact: the data protection contact it named, or else the workspace's admins, or for a personal space the account holder.
- **DPA.** The 2021/914 clauses only where a transfer is in their scope and after the private annex is completed (both parties' details, acceptance and date, the UK start date, the Clause 13 authority); Module Two for a controller, Module Three for a processor acting with its controller's authorization; docking only by accession; general authorization with 14 days' notice, and an earlier start only with the customer's specific written authorization; no independent dispute body; Irish law and courts. UK Addendum B1.0 (in force 21 March 2022) with both parties able to end it; Swiss changes without displacing the GDPR. Departures required by law only within what the GDPR, the UK GDPR and the transfer safeguards allow. Audits by information and proportionate audits including inspections. Breach notice without undue delay, with no hour count. Return or deletion at the customer's choice; after a return, remaining copies are deleted and completion is confirmed in writing. Precedence: mandatory law and the transfer clauses, then the DPA for personal data, then a signed agreement or the paid-plan confirmation for commercial matters, then the Terms.
- **Sub-processors.** Cloudflare is the only direct sub-processor for customer content, with a link to its own list. Cloudflare's published terms give 30 days before a change of its sub-processors but require our objection within ten days of its notification: monitor its announcements promptly, do not calculate our customer notice by waiting until 14 days before the change, track both deadlines, and resolve affected customers' objections before their data reaches the proposed provider. Mail to `abuse@`, `privacy@`, `legal@` and `hello@` is forwarded by Cloudflare Email Routing to the operator's mailbox, hosted by Google; the pages say so without the address and claim no contract with Google. A payment provider, when one is used, handles payment under its own terms and is named in the confirmation.
- **Notice and takedown.** Both the report form and the email route carry the full notice of illegal content (the form keeps the exact address, the explanation, the name, never asked for child sexual abuse or exploitation reports, and the statement of good faith); every email notice is acknowledged and every notice answered, by email when the reporter gave an address; restrictions made with the operator tool come with a statement of reasons emailed and shown in the dashboard no later than when they take effect (scope, duration, facts, notice or own review, automation, ground, review and redress), withheld only with `--withhold-notice` where the law permits; a person decides content restrictions; urgent crimes and child sexual abuse or exploitation are reported to the authorities, and the content is preserved with a legal hold as the law requires, which nobody outside the operator can detect, keeps its access log, analytics, comments and the identities of its creator and publishers until it ends, and survives a restore. The steps are under "Reports" in [operations.md](operations.md). Duties that apply only to online platforms are not taken on.
- **Copyright** (maintainer). Full notice (the six elements of 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(3)), counter-notice (512(g)) and repeat infringer process in appropriate circumstances, without claiming a designated agent or a safe harbor. Trademark complaints are separate.
- **Cookies and storage.** All strictly needed for sign-in, security and requested viewing functions, or a preference the visitor chose; no advertising or analytics trackers. The pages list every cookie and every browser storage item.
- **Sensitive data.** Portifact is not designed for health records, payment card data, government identifiers, special categories at scale or children's data; the Acceptable Use Policy and the DPA forbid them without a written agreement that names the categories and safeguards.
- **Export, backups and switching.** The pages describe what each export tool returns and that the operator returns the rest on request (today only cancelled approval requests, data a person's role does not let them export, and what a switching customer asks for that no tool covers); they do not promise that every item stays 30 days after a plan ends, because data keeps ageing during the grace. Where the EU Data Act applies, a switching customer gets the Act's periods; the operator keeps its paid plan in force at no extra charge through the transition and for 30 days after it, and because extending the plan does not stop normal expiry, also makes, verifies and keeps a separate export of the data needed for the switch (including what the tools omit) for at least 30 calendar days after the transition, then deletes it ([operations.md](operations.md), "A customer asks to switch or leave"). The Terms carry an export register of every category, tool, format and limit. The pages say that a restore re-applies the markers of deleted comments and threads, ended memberships, withdrawn invitations, email notices turned off, muted artifacts, sign-outs, revoked app tokens and disconnected apps (kept 30 days, the Time Travel window), that a comment deleted in the last minutes before the restore point may not have its marker yet (a region's `comment_tombstones` job), and that readers and groups removed from an artifact write no marker; that lifecycle and moderation records are deleted a year after the artifact or account is gone (`HISTORY_RETENTION_DAYS`, chosen to cover the six-month complaint window, appeals and repeated abuse) and comment-action records lose a deleted author's ID; that expired sessions, sign-in code records and tokens are deleted a day after they expire and app registrations without an account once 30 days old with no consent, token or device code (never approved, or disconnected by everyone); and that records kept for repeated requests hold a thread's ID, never comment text. A legal hold, when built, plugs into `historyHeld` (`packages/services/src/history-retention.ts`).

### What is not appointed or registered

- No EU or UK data protection representative and no DSA legal representative has been appointed. Whether an appointment is required depends on the relevant law's territorial scope and exceptions, not on payment or account count alone. The pages must not claim that a representative has been appointed.
- No designated copyright agent is registered, so Portifact does not rely on the hosting safe harbor of 17 U.S.C. 512(c). Registration is possible without a company; a later registration does not cover earlier conduct. The pages never claim a designated agent or a safe harbor.
- No lawyer reviewed this version of the texts.
- The operator is an individual and is personally liable; a future company does not remove rights or liabilities that already exist.
- The default databases and bucket have no jurisdiction: Cloudflare placed them in `WNAM` without guaranteeing it, so the pages say "today in western North America" and that the standard service has no contractual storage restriction.
- The operator's mailbox is a consumer Gmail account without data processing terms. The mailbox rules in [operations.md](operations.md) ("Reports", step 8) limit what it holds.
- Reminder emails (grace, retention, expiry) are attempted, not guaranteed; the pages say so and the dashboard shows the dates. Statements of reasons and report outcomes are sent by the product when the operator acts with the operator tool, and wait for the next cycle rather than being dropped when the month's emails run out; other notices the law or a contract requires are sent by the operator by hand, by any available contact method.

## Changing the texts

1. Edit the pages and check every claim against the code. A change to wording that does not alter anyone's rights or obligations can keep the version; moving a feature out of "Features not yet available" follows the check in that section.
2. For a material change, set `TERMS_VERSION` (`packages/contracts/src/terms-version.ts`) to the new effective date, update the version and date on all four pages (the test fails until they match), and rewrite "What changed in this version" in the Terms and the Privacy Policy. Every account is then asked to accept again at its next sign-in or dashboard visit.
3. A change that is materially worse for anyone, or that changes the processing of their data in a way they would not expect, takes effect at least 30 days after it is emailed to every account's address and announced on the pages. A change may take effect at once only when it is needed for an urgent security problem or to comply with the law (explain it as soon as allowed), or when it adds a feature without making anyone's rights or processing worse. A new sub-processor needs 14 days' notice to every customer's contact (DPA), or the customer's specific written authorization for an earlier start. Mandatory rights to refuse a change, end the contract and get a refund stay.
4. Update this document in the same change: the pages table, "What the pages state", "Keep in sync" and "Features not yet available".
