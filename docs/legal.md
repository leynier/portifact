# Legal pages and terms acceptance

Portifact publishes three legal documents on `portifact.com` and records, for every account, which version of them it accepted. A lawyer reviewed the texts on 2026-11-08, as plan section 17 requires before registration opens; have any material change reviewed again before it ships.

## Pages

| Path              | Source                         | Covers                                                                                                                                    |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `/terms`          | `apps/web/terms.html`          | Operator, 18+ eligibility, accounts, quotas, content license, lifecycle, enforcement, disclaimers, liability, Florida law, changes        |
| `/privacy`        | `apps/web/privacy.html`        | Data collected (including the Turnstile human check), uses, cookies, processors (Cloudflare, Google, GitHub), retention, rights, children |
| `/acceptable-use` | `apps/web/acceptable-use.html` | Prohibited content and behavior, coding agents, abuse reports, enforcement                                                                |

- They are static Vite entries like the landing (`apps/web/vite.config.ts`), load only the shared stylesheet and no script, and use design-system token utilities only. `apps/app/src/site.ts` serves them on `portifact.com` with the strict `PAGE_CSP`, `no-cache`, `nosniff` and `X-Frame-Options: DENY`; the app host answers 404 for them.
- Each page shows the version and the effective date. `apps/web/src/legal.test.ts` checks that both match `TERMS_VERSION`, so the pages and the code cannot drift.
- The pages were written once from a generator that is not kept; edit the HTML directly and keep the structure (one `h1`, a table of contents whose links match the `section` ids, captioned tables, the legal footer with `aria-current`).
- The landing footer and the footers of `/login`, `/device`, `/consent` and `/report` link all three pages; the landing footer also links the report form and the abuse address. The sign-in notice links them too (see "Acceptance at sign-in").
- English is the only authoritative text. The localized copies (`/<code>/terms`, `/<code>/privacy` and `/<code>/acceptable-use` for every language besides English, such as `/es/terms` or `/zh-cn/privacy`, prerendered by `apps/web/src/i18n/site.ts`) translate only the chrome around `main` (title, header, footer, language switcher), keep `main` in English with `lang="en"`, show a note in the page's language that the English text is the one that applies, and point their canonical link at the English page. Never put translation markers inside `main` (`src/i18n/site.test.ts` fails) and never bump `TERMS_VERSION` for a translation. The sign-in notice on the login page is translated with the same conditions and the same three links (`src/auth/i18n/catalog.test.ts`).
- Write them in plain language and describe only what the product does today; mark planned features as planned. When a feature ships or a rule changes (quotas, retention, cookies, processors), update the affected page in the same change.
- The report form (`app.portifact.com/report`, linked from the landing footer, from `/__portifact/report` on every artifact, from the "Report this artifact" footer of rendered Markdown and TXT documents and from the review viewer), suspension and its notice, and the dashboard's data export and account deletion are live and described in the Terms ("Reports and enforcement", ending the account), the Acceptable Use Policy ("Reporting abuse", enforcement) and the Privacy Policy (rights, retention of deleted accounts and abuse reports). Keep them in sync with [operations.md](operations.md) and the account deletion rules in [security.md](security.md).

Contact addresses used by the pages: `abuse@portifact.com`, `privacy@portifact.com` and `legal@portifact.com`. Cloudflare Email Routing on `portifact.com` forwards all three to the operator's inbox (set up 2026-11-08); a new address needs its own routing rule.

## Acceptance at sign-in

No account is created and no session is issued for an account that has not accepted the current `TERMS_VERSION` (`packages/contracts/src/terms-version.ts`), whatever the method: Google, GitHub, email code or a workspace's single sign-on ([decision 0016](decisions/0016-workspace-sso.md), switched off until its release checks pass).

Consent is collected with a sign-in-wrap: there is no checkbox, and choosing a sign-in method is the acceptance. The conditions that make it valid are part of the product and are tested (`apps/web/src/auth/login.test.ts`, `e2e/tests/sign-in.spec.ts`):

- The notice is conspicuous and shown before any action: a bordered block in full-contrast text the size of the buttons' labels, right above the methods, never collapsed or behind a link. It reads "By continuing with Google, GitHub or an email code, you confirm that you are 18 or older, you agree to the Terms of Service and the Acceptable Use Policy, and you have read the Privacy Policy", with each document linked (opening in a new tab).
- When the address entered belongs to a workspace's single sign-on, the login page shows the "Continue with single sign-on" step and swaps the notice, in the same place and form, for one that reads "By continuing with Google, GitHub, an email code or single sign-on, you confirm..." with the same three links; both of that step's buttons name it in `aria-describedby`, and choosing either posts the acceptance first. The provider's callback is a top-level navigation, so the acceptance cookie reaches it as for Google and GitHub.
- It sits next to every sign-in button: the Google, GitHub, "Email me a code" and "Sign in" (code step) buttons follow it on the same card, and each names it in `aria-describedby`, so a screen reader reads the notice with the button. It stays on screen in the code step.
- The action records the acceptance: every method posts the acceptance before it starts, and the Worker records the accepted version and time per account, exactly as below.
- The review screen for accounts on an older version uses the same pattern: "By selecting Agree and continue, you confirm..." above the button that records it.

Sign-in-wrap is the maintainer's choice of 2026-11-08 instead of the earlier required checkbox; the lawyer reviewed the texts with the checkbox, so have them confirm sign-in-wrap is enough before relying on it. The Terms say "You accept these terms when you continue with Google, GitHub or an email code at sign-in, below the notice that says so"; that sentence changed with the mechanism without a new `TERMS_VERSION`, because what users accept did not change.

1. Before any method, the login page posts `{ version, acceptTerms: true, confirmAdult: true }` (`TermsAcceptanceInput`) to `POST /api/auth/accept-terms`, a Portifact endpoint in the auth allowlist with the exact-Origin check and a per-IP limit. The body states what the notice says; the page sends it only when the user chose a method.
2. Without a session, the Worker answers with `__Host-pf_terms` (`Secure; HttpOnly; SameSite=Lax; Path=/`, 30 minutes): the version and the acceptance time, HMAC-signed with a key derived from `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`. It survives the provider redirects because the callback is a top-level navigation.
3. Better Auth database hooks (`apps/app/src/auth/terms.ts`) enforce it. `user.create.before` refuses to create an account without a valid, current, unexpired cookie and stores `terms_version` and `terms_accepted_at` on the new user. `session.create.before` refuses a session for an account whose stored version is not current unless the cookie is present, and then records it. Refusals are `APIError` `FORBIDDEN` with code `terms_not_accepted`: the email-code sign-in answers 403, and provider callbacks redirect to `/login?error=terms_not_accepted`. `session.create.after` clears the cookie.
4. With a session, the endpoint records the acceptance on that account directly, keeping the time of an earlier acceptance of the same version.
5. Sessions that already exist are checked too: the dashboard routes, the `/api/v1` session lookup and `/read/<id>` treat a session of an account without the current version as no session, so the visitor lands on `/login`, which shows "Review the terms to continue" (the notice with "Agree and continue", or sign out) instead of forwarding to `next`.

The Privacy Policy also describes the email suppression list (undeliverable address records with their retention) and the daily email counts; keep them in sync with `SUPPRESSION_TTL_MS` (`packages/domain/src/email.ts`) and "Identity" in [security.md](security.md). They were added on 2026-11-08 without a new `TERMS_VERSION`: they describe processing that protects delivery for everyone and changes no one's rights.

The Privacy Policy says that owners get an expiry notice a few days before their artifacts expire, at most one a day, unless they turn it off in Settings, and that undeliverable addresses get none; keep it in sync with "Expiry notices" in [operations.md](operations.md). The policy announced these notices as planned since its first version; they went live without a new `TERMS_VERSION`, because they use the address already kept for sign-in, only for the service, with an opt-out, and change no one's rights.

The Privacy Policy and the Terms say who sees a comment: its artifact's creator always, and the other people with access only as the creator's comment visibility allows (by default, only the threads they or the creator started). This narrowed what other readers see when comment visibility shipped, on the version of 2026-11-08 without a new `TERMS_VERSION`, because it only reduces who reads a comment and changes no one's rights; keep it in sync with "Comment visibility" in [security.md](security.md).

The Privacy Policy describes the access log (what is recorded, that only the artifact's creator sees it, 30 days, erasure with the artifact or the visitor's account); keep it in sync with "Access logs" in [security.md](security.md) and `ACCESS_LOG` in `packages/contracts`. Access logs were listed as planned, with the same 30 days, since the policy's first version; they went live on the version of 2026-11-08 without a new `TERMS_VERSION` pending the maintainer's decision, recorded in the pull request that shipped them. Since the viewer with the Portifact bar, only `restricted` artifacts record openings: visits of public and `authenticated` artifacts are never recorded, even with a session, because any site can send a signed-in visitor to them; the policy says so, which narrows what is collected and needs no new acceptance.

New accounts also need a solved human check (Cloudflare Turnstile, `__Host-pf_human`), enforced next to the acceptance in the same `user.create.before` hook; see "Human check" in [security.md](security.md). The Privacy Policy lists it under what is collected, the cookie table and Cloudflare's processing; keep them in sync if the check changes.

The Privacy Policy lists the two preference cookies (`__Secure-pf_theme`, `__Secure-pf_lang`) that `portifact.com` and `app.portifact.com` share, and says they never reach `portifact.dev`; keep it in sync with "Cookies and sessions" in [security.md](security.md). They were added on the version of 2026-11-08 without a new `TERMS_VERSION`: they are strictly functional, hold only the theme or language the visitor chose and no identifier, are set only when the visitor chooses, and change no one's rights.

The Terms and the Privacy Policy describe a fixed lifecycle: an artifact expires 30 days after its last publish or renewal and is kept archived for 6 months before it is deleted. Since pins and archive length by plan (premium plan P2), both depend on the owner's plan: a paid plan can pin an artifact so it does not expire, and an archive is kept for the plan's `archiveMonths` when it is archived (12 on paid plans). The free plan, and every account without a plan, still behaves exactly as the pages say, so the texts were left unchanged and `TERMS_VERSION` was not bumped; change them in one go with the other plan-dependent terms when paid plans are offered.

The Privacy Policy's retention table says previous versions of an artifact are kept "up to two, for 7 days after a newer one is published". Since version retention by plan (premium plan P3) that is the free plan's value: Pro keeps up to 50 for 90 days and Team up to 100 for a year (`PLANS`), and a lower plan prunes down to its own values. Until then the count of two was not enforced (only the seven days were); it is now, so the free plan matches the page exactly. The page and `TERMS_VERSION` are unchanged; describe the plan-dependent retention with the other paid-plan terms.

`terms_version` and `terms_accepted_at` are Better Auth `additionalFields` with `input: false`, so no request body can set them; `get-session` returns them to the login page.

Bearer tokens issued to the CLI and MCP clients before an account accepted are not revoked; the account is asked at its next browser sign-in or dashboard visit.

## Pending changes to the legal texts

Changes that shipped and that the legal pages do not describe yet, to be written in one go with the next update of the texts (and a `TERMS_VERSION` bump if the change is material):

- Audit streams (premium plan E3, [decision 0019](decisions/0019-audit-stream.md)), built and switched off. When they are switched on, the texts must say: an Enterprise owner's admins can send the owner's audit log continuously to an HTTPS endpoint they choose and run; what is sent is the audit log as the Audit page shows it (the kind of change, when, who acted by display name and an opaque ID, what it was about, artifact names while the owner still has them, and the change's counts, settings, hosts and email domains), never comment text, content, credentials, full email addresses or IP addresses; once sent, the data is held by the customer's endpoint under the customer's own terms and may be outside the EU, also for an EU workspace; Portifact keeps only the endpoint's host in clear, its URL and signing secret encrypted, and a delivery log of seven days with no content. The Privacy page lists the customer's endpoint as a destination the customer chooses, not a processor.
- EU data residency (premium plan E5, [decision 0005](decisions/0005-regions.md), "Two-region audit"). The code is ready for a second region but none exists, and the feature is not offered (`euResidency` is not in `AVAILABLE_PLAN_FEATURES`). Before the first EU workspace, the maintainer writes: the promise itself (for a workspace created in the EU, its files, artifacts' metadata, comments, access log and analytics counters are stored in the EU; processing in the EU is not promised; the region is chosen at creation and never changes, and artifacts never move between regions); what stays global and where (accounts, sessions, sign-in identities, the workspace's name and slug, memberships, groups, invitations, plans, service tokens, custom domain claims, single sign-on providers, the artifact directory of IDs and regions, tombstones, abuse reports, budget and email counters); that notices about an EU workspace carry no artifact names or comment text; that webhooks and integrations go where the customer points them; a data processing addendum, the list of sub-processors with the location of each (Cloudflare D1 and R2 in the EU jurisdiction, Workers wherever the request lands, Cloudflare Email Service) and the standard contractual clauses. These are the maintainer's to write and are material for the Privacy Policy and the Terms (a `TERMS_VERSION` bump).
  - **What an EU workspace's emails carry** (E5.7, decision 0005, "What leaves the EU"). Emails go through Cloudflare Email Service, outside the region. Notices Portifact sends on its own about an EU workspace's artifacts carry counts, dates and links into the app: the expiry notice says how many artifacts expire and when the first does and links the workspace's list of artifacts expiring soon; comment and review notices say "An artifact" with counts and a link; none carries an artifact's name, comment text or another person's address. Invitation and directory emails name the workspace and the inviting admin: the workspace's name is its identity, held in the global database with its memberships, and is not artifact metadata under the promise; say so explicitly. Sign-in codes go to the account's own address (identity, global). The texts must also say that the workspace's name and slug and its members' accounts (name, email, sign-in methods) are global, outside the EU.
  - **Destinations the customer chooses**, which leave the EU by the customer's choice and are said so in the dashboard for an EU workspace: webhooks (artifact and comment events, never comment text), issue integrations with GitHub and Linear (what the person creating the issue chooses to send, comment text and the quote only when they turn them on), and audit streams (the audit log as the Audit page shows it). Portifact does not control where those services store what they receive.
  - **Logs.** Workers observability is global. Portifact's own log lines carry IDs, counts and error labels, never artifact names, comment text, email addresses or URL paths, in any region. The platform's invocation logs are different: while they are on, Cloudflare records each sampled request's URL (an artifact's file path, a search in the artifact list) and uncaught exceptions, kept 7 days in Cloudflare's Workers Logs, whose documentation states no storage location ([Workers Logs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/observability/logs/workers-logs/); the [Data Localization compatibility](https://developers.cloudflare.com/data-localization/compatibility/) page says Workers logs are not available outside the US region under the Customer Metadata Boundary). Invocation logs are off in the app and content Workers since 2026-11-11 (`docs/releasing.md`, "EU region", step 13), so no request URL is recorded; turning them back on means stating this in the texts first.

- Workspace policies (premium plan E4, [decision 0015](decisions/0015-workspace-policies.md)). The Terms must say that an Enterprise workspace's admins can set policies that bind every member, admins and service tokens included (no public access, the access new artifacts start with, the email domains readers may have, whether share links need a password or exist at all, and their longest lifetime), that confirming a stricter policy changes existing artifacts (public artifacts become workspace-only, readers outside the domains are removed, links are revoked or stop opening), and that a policy keeps applying after the plan ends until an admin lifts it. The Privacy page must say that a policy stores email domains, never addresses, and that its changes are in the workspace's audit log. It adds no personal data or processor, so it is not a material change on its own.
- Plan grace ([decision 0022](decisions/0022-plan-grace.md)). When a paid plan ends, is removed or is lowered, what it kept (previous versions, the access log and analytics counters, the audit log, pins, the archive length) is kept for 60 days from that date and stays readable and downloadable; after that, what the plan the owner has now does not keep is deleted. The Terms and the Privacy page's retention section must say so, replacing any wording that a lower plan deletes at once. During the grace every page of the space says until when, and the "Download your data" page and `portifact export` let the owner take everything out; the Privacy page can name them as the way to get one's data. The notices about it (when the grace starts, 14 and 3 days before it ends) ship in a later change; they will be service emails about deletion, sent whatever the account's notice settings (no opt-out) and in the account's language. It keeps data longer than before and adds no personal data or processor, so it is not a material change on its own.
- Sign-in policies ([decision 0021](decisions/0021-workspace-session-policies.md)). A workspace's admins can ask its members to sign in again after some hours and limit which kinds of app act on it. They read when a session or an app's authorization signed in, which Portifact already kept (the session's creation time; each app token now carries the sign-in time of its grant), and store no new personal data. The Terms' description of workspace policies covers them; not a material change on its own.
- How a session signed in ([decision 0021](decisions/0021-workspace-session-policies.md), "SSO required"). Since migration 0061 Portifact stores, with each session and each app token, how the sign-in happened: an email code, Google, GitHub or a workspace's single sign-on (by the provider's ID). It is kept with the session or token and deleted with it, shown to the account on the Apps page and used only to apply a workspace's "SSO required" policy. The Privacy Policy's description of session data should name it at its next revision ("how you signed in"); the maintainer decides whether that is material.

- Content search (premium plan T8, [decision 0012](decisions/0012-content-search.md)). The Privacy page must say that for owners whose plan includes it, Portifact keeps a search index of the text of their active artifacts' current versions (Markdown and text documents and the visible text of HTML pages, up to set caps), in the same database and region as the artifacts; that only the owner side (a workspace's admins and editors, and its service tokens within their reach) can search it; that queries are not stored or logged; and that the index goes when an artifact is archived, deleted, purged or moved, when the plan no longer includes the feature, and with the account or workspace. It is derived from content the owner already stores and adds no processor, so it is not a material change on its own.

- Comment notices (premium plan P5): a new kind of email. The Privacy page must say that on artifacts whose owner's plan includes them, Portifact emails the owner side and the people who took part in a thread a digest of new comment activity, at most every 10 minutes; that the email holds the artifact's name, counts of new threads, replies and resolutions and links, never what was written or who wrote it; that it is on by default and turned off in the settings, per artifact with a mute, or with the one-click unsubscribe link in every email; and what is stored for it: pending activity per recipient and thread (IDs, counts and a time, deleted once the digest is scheduled, with the thread, the artifact or the account), the mutes, the setting and when it last changed, all part of the account export. The Terms may also mention it among the paid features.
- Share links (premium plan P4, [decision 0008](decisions/0008-share-links.md)). The texts must say:
  - **Terms.** An owner on a paid plan can create links that let anyone holding them read an artifact without an account; the owner is responsible for who gets a link and its password, revokes a link to stop it, and links stop while the plan does not include them. Visitors through a link accept the Acceptable Use Policy by using the artifact, like any anonymous visitor of a public artifact.
  - **Privacy.** For a link Portifact keeps its label, creation, optional expiry, a hash of the token and of the optional password, and the day it was last opened; visitors through a link are not identified: the owner's access log records only that the link was used in a 30-minute window (kept 30 days), and nothing about the visitor (no IP, no account). Opening a link with a password may load Cloudflare Turnstile after repeated wrong passwords, and the visitor's IP is used for rate limits in short-lived counters, as on the sign-in page.
  - **Acceptable Use.** Share links may not be used to distribute content the policy forbids, to get around a suspension, or to publish material openly while pretending it is private; a suspension ends every link to the artifact, and reports about a link are handled like reports about the artifact.

  These are material changes for the Privacy Policy (new data and a new processor use), so bump `TERMS_VERSION` then.
- Workspaces (premium plan T1) shipped without changing the pages or `TERMS_VERSION`; until the texts describe them, the operator creates workspaces only for customers who agreed to these terms separately. The texts must say:
  - **New personal data.** Memberships (which account belongs to which workspace, with which role and since when), pending invitations (the invited email address, the role, who invited and when it expires; only a hash of the link's token) and a `transferred` event on an artifact (who moved it and between which spaces). Members of a workspace see each other's name and email; invitations list the invited addresses to the workspace's admins.
  - **Emails.** Invitation emails go to addresses that may have no account, in the inviting admin's language; they count in the month's email budget and are refused for suppressed addresses. The address is kept only while the invitation is pending (7 days, or until it is accepted, declined or revoked); expired ones are pruned. Anyone signed in who opens the link sees which address it was sent to (and nothing else unless it is theirs), so they can switch accounts; the link is sent only to that address.
  - **Retention and deletion.** A membership goes with the account or the workspace. A deleted workspace's artifacts, objects, counters and plan are purged like a deleted account's, and its ID is kept as a tombstone. An account that is a workspace's only admin cannot be deleted until another member is made admin or the workspace is deleted. The account export includes the account's memberships and the pending invitations addressed to it, never other people's addresses.
  - **Moving artifacts.** When an artifact moves to another space, its access log (who opened it and when) and its view counters move with it: the people who manage the new space see who opened it before the move, and the rows are then kept by the new owner's plan.
  - **Control and content.** A workspace's admins and editors manage every artifact in it and see every comment; content moved into a workspace stays there when the member who published it leaves or deletes their account. Who may create workspaces (the operator, until billing), seats, and that a lower or expired plan removes nobody but refuses new members.
- Single sign-on for workspaces (premium plan E1, [decision 0016](decisions/0016-workspace-sso.md)) ships switched off; before it is turned on, the texts must say:
  - **Terms.** A workspace on a plan that includes it can let its people sign in through the organization's own identity provider for the workspace's verified email domains; the workspace's admins are responsible for that provider and for who it lets in; signing in this way is a sign-in method like the others, under the same acceptance (the notice names it); people who sign in this way join the workspace automatically, as members or with the role of a pending invitation, while it has a free seat, and are not removed when the identity provider removes them (the workspace's admins remove them, and someone an admin removed cannot rejoin through it until invited again); an existing account is linked to the provider only when the provider confirms the address or the person links it while signed in.
  - **Privacy.** The workspace's identity provider becomes a source of personal data: at each sign-in Portifact receives an ID token from it and keeps only the provider's stable identifier for the person (linked to the account), the email address and the name, never the provider's tokens. Portifact keeps the provider's settings (issuer, client ID, the client secret encrypted, the domains it serves) as the workspace's data, records in the workspace's audit log when it is set up, changed or removed and when someone joins through it, and deletes the link with the account or when the provider is removed. Automatic membership means the workspace's admins and editors see the person's name and email as for any member.

- Workspace and email domain sharing (premium plan T2, [decision 0010](decisions/0010-workspace-access-modes.md)) shipped without changing the pages or `TERMS_VERSION`. The texts must say:
  - **Privacy.** A workspace's admins can verify email domains by publishing a public DNS record; Portifact keeps the domain, the record's value and when it was verified and last checked, checks it daily through a DNS resolver (Cloudflare's DNS over HTTPS) and deletes it when it is removed or the record is gone for three checks. An artifact shared with a workspace is readable by its members, and one shared with its email domains also by anyone signed in with a verified address at those domains: Portifact compares the domain of the account's verified email with the workspace's domains when access is checked, and records nothing new about those visits (they are not in the access log). Admins see the verified domains; the account export lists them only to the workspace's admins.
  - **Terms.** The admins who verify a domain must be entitled to speak for it; the workspace is responsible for who holds addresses at its domains, and Portifact removes a domain when its record is gone or another workspace proves control of it.

  Neither changes what an account that does not use workspaces gives or sees; whether the Privacy change is material is for the lawyer's review of the wave (premium plan L4).
- Workspace groups (premium plan T3, [decision 0014](decisions/0014-workspace-groups.md)) shipped without changing the pages or `TERMS_VERSION`. The texts must say:
  - **Privacy.** A workspace's admins can put its members in named groups; Portifact keeps each group's name and which members are in it and since when, and which groups an artifact lists with their comment setting. Admins see each group's members; the workspace's editors see only group names and how many members each has. A member of a group an artifact lists can read it like a listed reader, and the access log records those openings as it does for listed readers. Group memberships go when the person leaves the workspace or deletes their account, and groups go with the workspace. The account export lists the groups the account is in (their names and when it was added), never the other members.
  - **Terms.** The admins who fill a group decide who reads every artifact that lists it.

  Neither changes what an account outside a workspace gives or sees; whether the Privacy change is material is for the lawyer's review of the wave (premium plan L4).
- Approval reviews (premium plan T6, [decision 0013](decisions/0013-approval-reviews.md)) shipped without changing the pages or `TERMS_VERSION`. The Privacy page must say that the people who manage an artifact on a Team plan can ask named people to review it by email address (kept with the request, like the reader list, and shown only to the people who manage the artifact); that a reviewer's verdict (approve or request changes), its optional note and when it was given are stored with the version it was given on, shown to the people who manage the artifact and to other reviewers only as far as the artifact's comment visibility shows comments, and kept as history after a new version, a cancellation or the reviewer losing access; that the status (counts, never names) can be shown to everyone who can open the artifact when the requester chooses; that a deleted account's verdicts and notes are deleted, while the address stays on the requests that named it as the owner's list; that notes are never in emails, webhooks or the audit log (which records only that a verdict was given, which and on which version); and that the account export includes the account's verdicts with their notes. The Terms may list reviews among the Team features.
- Review emails (approval reviews, [decision 0013](decisions/0013-approval-reviews.md), "Emails"): a new kind of email, under the comment notices' setting. The Privacy page must say that on artifacts whose owner's plan includes reviews and comment notices, Portifact emails a requested reviewer who has a Portifact account and a standing relationship to the artifact (the owner's own people, its reader list, a group it lists, or a verified domain it is shared with; one email per version, saying their review was asked, with the artifact's name, the version and a link; never a note or other reviewers' names), never an address that has no account or someone who can open the artifact only because it is public, and never because a service token asked; that one person receives at most 20 review emails a day, counted under their account; and emails the people who manage the artifact once per version when it is approved or changes are requested; that one setting and its one-click unsubscribe link cover comment and review emails; what is stored for it (which person was emailed about which artifact version and when, deleted with the version, the artifact or the account; jobs that hold account and request IDs until they run); and that review webhook events (for owners who set webhooks up) carry the status and counts, never names, addresses or notes. It adds no processor beyond the email service already used, so whether it is material is for the lawyer's review of the wave (premium plan L4).
- Custom domains (premium plan P7, [decision 0006](decisions/0006-custom-domains.md)), once they are switched on. The texts must say:
  - **Terms.** An owner on a paid plan can serve an artifact from a domain they control; they must keep the TXT record that proves it, and Portifact removes the domain (never the artifact) when the proof is gone for three daily checks, when the plan no longer includes it (after a 7-day grace following the plan's end, announced by email), when the artifact is suspended or deleted, when the operator blocks the name, or when someone else proves control of the name. Portifact sends no HSTS on customer domains and upgrades `http` itself; cookies Portifact sets there are the customer's visitors' sessions for that artifact only, and other cookies the customer's own sites set on the domain are the customer's responsibility. Portifact serves the artifact there through Cloudflare, which issues the certificate; the owner is responsible for their DNS, and the artifact keeps its Portifact address.
  - **Privacy.** For a custom domain Portifact keeps the hostname, the artifact and owner it serves, the verification value and when it was verified and checked; hostnames are shared with Cloudflare to issue certificates, are global data even for a workspace in another region, and are part of the account export. Removed domains are deleted.
  - **Acceptable Use.** A custom domain may not impersonate another person, brand or Portifact itself, and the policy's rules apply to everything served on it; abuse on a customer's domain is reported like abuse on the artifact and ends with the domain's removal.

- Artifact analytics and the access log by plan (premium plan P8, [decision 0007](decisions/0007-artifact-analytics.md)). The Privacy page must say: owners on a plan with analytics see, per artifact, page views per UTC day and how many of them came with a signed-in visitor, as counts that identify nobody; for artifacts shared with listed people, they also see how many distinct signed-in readers opened it per day, from the access log, which already names those readers to them; anonymous visitors are never identified (no IP address, cookie or identifier is stored for a view); the access log and the counts are kept 30 days on the free plan and up to one year on paid plans, and after a plan change the shorter retention applies to what was kept; counts go with the artifact and openings with the artifact or the visitor's account, as today. The section on the access log ("30 days") must change with it. Keeping data that names visitors for up to a year instead of 30 days is a material change by the rules below: it goes in the wave's single `TERMS_VERSION` bump (premium plan L4), after a lawyer's review, before the first paid plan is granted. The Terms may also list analytics among the paid features. Until then only owners granted a paid plan by the operator keep openings longer than 30 days.

- Webhooks (premium plan P6): a customer-chosen destination receiving activity data. The Privacy page must say that an owner on a plan with webhooks can have Portifact send, to an HTTPS address the owner chooses (their own server, Slack or Discord), events about their artifacts: the artifact's name and links, event types, times, counts, version numbers, the access mode and the display name of the owner's member who acted; never comment text, quotes or readers' email addresses; that this data then leaves Portifact under the owner's responsibility, and for an EU workspace leaves the region ([decision 0005](decisions/0005-regions.md), section 6); and what is stored: the destination (sealed), the host, the chosen events, pending events (IDs and counts, deleted when delivered) and a 7-day delivery log (time, outcome and HTTP status), deleted with the webhook or the account and listed, without the address or secret, in the account export. The Terms should say that the owner is responsible for the destinations they choose and must not use webhooks to send requests to systems they are not allowed to, and may list webhooks among the paid features.

- Owner branding (premium plan P9): owners on a paid plan can show their name, logo, accent color and link on the viewer's bar above their artifacts. The texts must say:
  - **Terms.** The owner is responsible for having the rights to the name, logo and link they show (trademarks); a brand may not imitate Portifact or another person or organization; Portifact may suspend a brand without notice, which brings back the default bar, and the bar always says the page is hosted on Portifact. Branding stops while the plan does not include it and the brand is kept until the owner deletes it.
  - **Acceptable Use.** Impersonation through a brand (another company's name or logo, Portifact's own, or a look-alike) and links that lead to phishing or malware are forbidden, and reports about a branded bar are handled like reports about the artifact.
  - **Privacy.** For a brand Portifact keeps the name, color, link and the logo file (counted in the owner's storage), part of the account export and deleted with the brand or the account; visitors' browsers load the logo from the artifact's own address, with no referrer and no cookie.

  The Terms and Acceptable Use changes are material (new obligations for owners), so they go in the wave's single `TERMS_VERSION` bump.

- Audit log (premium plan T10). The Privacy page must say that Portifact keeps, for every owner, a record of the changes made to it (publishing and lifecycle, access and readers as counts and email domains, share links, custom domains, webhooks, branding, members, invitations, roles and plan grants) with which account made each one and when, for 90 days (longer only under an Enterprise contract); that the admins of a workspace on a plan with the audit log see which member did what, naming members by display name and showing people who left as removed members; that it never holds comment text, content, full email addresses or IP addresses; that a personal space's record is part of the account export; and that it is deleted with the account or workspace. This is new personal data about members' actions, a material change for the Privacy Policy, so it goes in the Team wave's single `TERMS_VERSION` bump.
- Service tokens and publish keys (premium plan T4 and T5, [decision 0011](decisions/0011-service-tokens.md)). The texts must say:
  - **Terms.** A workspace's admins can create service tokens that act for the workspace from automation such as CI; the workspace is responsible for everything done with its tokens and for keeping them secret, and admins revoke a token to stop it; tokens stop while the plan does not include them and when they expire.
  - **Privacy.** For a token Portifact keeps its label, role, scopes, restriction, creation and expiry, who created it (cleared when that account is deleted), a hash of the secret (never the secret) and when it was last used, to the hour; versions and lifecycle events record the token as their author. Tokens are listed, without secrets, in the export of the workspace's admins, and deleted with the workspace. Publish keys are names the owner chooses for artifacts and are kept with the artifact.

  No new processor and no new data about visitors; the Terms change (responsibility for tokens) goes in the wave's single `TERMS_VERSION` bump.
- Audit log export and retention by contract (premium plan E3). The texts must say:
  - **Privacy.** The audit log is kept 90 days unless an Enterprise contract sets another period, from 30 days to seven years; a shorter period deletes older events, and the record is still deleted with the account or workspace. On Enterprise, the owner's admins can download the log as CSV or JSON Lines; the file holds the same thing the page shows (members by display name and opaque ID, never email or IP addresses), and starting a download is itself recorded in the log. Once downloaded, the file is the owner's to protect.
  - **Terms.** An Enterprise owner who exports the log is responsible for the copies it makes.

  A retention longer than 90 days is new personal data kept for longer, so it is material for the Privacy Policy and goes in the Enterprise wave's single `TERMS_VERSION` bump; until then, a contract that sets a longer retention must say so itself.

- SCIM provisioning (premium plan E2, [decision 0017](decisions/0017-workspace-scim.md)), switched off until the texts describe it. A workspace's directory becomes a source of personal data about people who may never have signed in. The texts must say:
  - **Privacy.** For each person a workspace's directory provisions, Portifact keeps the email address, the directory's identifier for them (`externalId`), the names it sent and whether it marked them active, for the workspace only and echoed back only to its directory. For someone without an account, a pending membership holds the address too, until they sign in (they then become a member, under the terms they accept at sign-in) or for 90 days after the directory last wrote them; the maintenance run then deletes the pending membership and the directory's copy. The directory's deletion deletes it at once. Deleting the workspace deletes all of it; deleting a person's account keeps the directory's copy without the account link, as the workspace's own record of its directory. Invitation emails to provisioned people are sent only when the workspace's admins turned them on.
  - **Terms.** A workspace's admins are responsible for what their directory sends and for having a basis to provision their people; a directory can add people to and remove them from the workspace and sign them out, never create a Portifact account or touch anything of a person's own.

  - **Membership through a directory.** A person whose existing account the directory adds becomes a member without an accept step and is told by email (with how to leave; leaving holds against the directory's routine syncs). From then on the workspace's admins see what they see of any member: the account's display name and email address on the Members page, the role and since when, the person's group memberships, and the audit log's events about them (joined through the directory, removed, the role); plus, in the directory's own resource, the `externalId` and names the directory sent. A deprovisioning signs the person out of Portifact entirely (every session and the tokens bound to them), which the Terms must say. A workspace's directory can also put a member in its groups, which decides which of the workspace's artifacts they read, and, through a mapping the workspace's admins configure, make them an editor or an admin of the workspace; the directory's group memberships of a person (pending people included) are kept with the directory's copy of them and go with it.
    This is new personal data about people without an account, so it is material for the Privacy Policy: bump `TERMS_VERSION` then.

- Issue integrations (premium plan T7, [decision 0018](decisions/0018-issue-integrations.md)), switched off until the texts describe them. The texts must say:
  - **Privacy.** On a plan with integrations, a space's admins can connect GitHub or Linear, services the customer chooses and contracts with, by pasting a credential of an account of theirs; Portifact keeps it encrypted, with the account name the service reports and the repositories or teams allowed, and deletes it when the connection is removed or the account or workspace is deleted. When the owner side creates an issue from a comment thread, Portifact sends to that service a title they write and, as they choose in the dialog, a link to the thread, the artifact's name, the quoted text of the thread's anchor and the thread's first message; the quote and the message are off unless they turn them on. Those words, which may be someone else's, leave Portifact and stay at the service under its own terms, even if the comment is later deleted in Portifact. Portifact never sends readers' email addresses, the artifact's content, other messages or review notes. For a workspace whose data is kept in the EU, what is sent leaves the EU. Portifact keeps, with the thread, the issue's service, repository or team, number and link, visible to whoever sees the thread, and records in the audit log which parts were sent, never the text.
  - **Terms.** The customer is responsible for the credential they connect, for the service's terms, and for having a basis to send commenters' words to that service; issues are created as the credential's account.

  GitHub and Linear are the customer's chosen destinations, not Portifact's processors, but sending comment text to them is new processing to describe, so it is material for the Privacy Policy: bump `TERMS_VERSION` then.

## Changing the texts

1. Edit the pages. A change to wording that does not alter anyone's rights can keep the version.
2. For a material change, set `TERMS_VERSION` to the new effective date and update the version and date on all three pages (the test fails until they match). Every account is then asked to accept again at its next sign-in or dashboard visit.
3. Announce material changes in advance, as the pages promise.
