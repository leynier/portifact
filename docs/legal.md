# Legal pages and terms acceptance

Portifact publishes three legal documents on `portifact.com` and records, for every account, which version of them it accepted. A lawyer reviewed the texts on 2026-11-08, as plan section 17 requires before registration opens; have any material change reviewed again before it ships. The version of 2026-12-10 (paid plans, workspaces, tokens and the audit log) was drafted without legal review and must not ship until a lawyer approves it; its date is a placeholder, set to the real effective date when it is approved and announced.

## Pages

| Path              | Source                         | Covers                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `/terms`          | `apps/web/terms.html`          | Operator, 18+ eligibility, accounts, quotas, plans, content license, lifecycle, workspaces, share links, branding, service tokens, features not yet available, enforcement, disclaimers, liability, Florida law, changes |
| `/privacy`        | `apps/web/privacy.html`        | Data collected (including the Turnstile human check), uses, cookies, processors (Cloudflare, Google, GitHub), workspaces, retention, rights, features not yet available, children                                        |
| `/acceptable-use` | `apps/web/acceptable-use.html` | Prohibited content and behavior, coding agents and service tokens, features not yet available, abuse reports, enforcement                                                                                                |

- They are static Vite entries like the landing (`apps/web/vite.config.ts`), load only the shared stylesheet and no script, and use design-system token utilities only. `apps/app/src/site.ts` serves them on `portifact.com` with the strict `PAGE_CSP`, `no-cache`, `nosniff` and `X-Frame-Options: DENY`; the app host answers 404 for them.
- Each page shows the version and the effective date. `apps/web/src/legal.test.ts` checks that both match `TERMS_VERSION`, so the pages and the code cannot drift.
- The pages were written once from a generator that is not kept; edit the HTML directly and keep the structure (one `h1`, a table of contents whose links match the `section` ids, captioned tables, the legal footer with `aria-current`).
- The landing footer and the footers of `/login`, `/device`, `/consent` and `/report` link all three pages; the landing footer also links the report form and the abuse address. The sign-in notice links them too (see "Acceptance at sign-in").
- English is the only authoritative text. The localized copies (`/<code>/terms`, `/<code>/privacy` and `/<code>/acceptable-use` for every language besides English, such as `/es/terms` or `/zh-cn/privacy`, prerendered by `apps/web/src/i18n/site.ts`) translate only the chrome around `main` (title, header, footer, language switcher), keep `main` in English with `lang="en"`, show a note in the page's language that the English text is the one that applies, and point their canonical link at the English page. Never put translation markers inside `main` (`src/i18n/site.test.ts` fails) and never bump `TERMS_VERSION` for a translation. The sign-in notice on the login page is translated with the same conditions and the same three links (`src/auth/i18n/catalog.test.ts`).
- Write them in plain language and describe only what the product does today; mark planned features as planned. When a feature ships or a rule changes (quotas, retention, cookies, processors), update the affected page in the same change.
- The report form (`app.portifact.com/report`, linked from the landing footer, from `/__portifact/report` on every artifact, from the "Report this artifact" footer of rendered Markdown and TXT documents and from the review viewer), suspension and its notice, and the dashboard's data export and account deletion are live and described in the Terms ("Reports and enforcement", ending the account), the Acceptable Use Policy ("Reporting abuse", enforcement) and the Privacy Policy (rights, retention of deleted accounts and abuse reports). Keep them in sync with [operations.md](operations.md) and the account deletion rules in [security.md](security.md).

Contact addresses used by the pages: `abuse@portifact.com`, `privacy@portifact.com`, `legal@portifact.com` (legal notices and questions about the terms only) and `hello@portifact.com` (requests for a paid plan and questions about plans, the address the pricing page and the dashboard's Plan page use, `PLAN_REQUEST_EMAIL` in `packages/contracts/src/plans.ts`). Cloudflare Email Routing on `portifact.com` forwards all four to the operator's inbox (the first three set up 2026-11-08, `hello@` checked 2026-11-10); a new address needs its own routing rule.

## Acceptance at sign-in

No account is created and no session is issued for an account that has not accepted the current `TERMS_VERSION` (`packages/contracts/src/terms-version.ts`), whatever the method: Google, GitHub or email code.

Consent is collected with a sign-in-wrap: there is no checkbox, and choosing a sign-in method is the acceptance. The conditions that make it valid are part of the product and are tested (`apps/web/src/auth/login.test.ts`, `e2e/tests/sign-in.spec.ts`):

- The notice is conspicuous and shown before any action: a bordered block in full-contrast text the size of the buttons' labels, right above the methods, never collapsed or behind a link. It reads "By continuing with Google, GitHub or an email code, you confirm that you are 18 or older, you agree to the Terms of Service and the Acceptable Use Policy, and you have read the Privacy Policy", with each document linked (opening in a new tab).
- It sits next to every sign-in button: the Google, GitHub, "Email me a code" and "Sign in" (code step) buttons follow it on the same card, and each names it in `aria-describedby`, so a screen reader reads the notice with the button. It stays on screen in the code step.
- The action records the acceptance: every method posts the acceptance before it starts, and the Worker records the accepted version and time per account, exactly as below.
- The review screen for accounts on an older version uses the same pattern: "By selecting Agree and continue, you confirm..." above the button that records it.

Sign-in-wrap is the maintainer's choice of 2026-11-08 instead of the earlier required checkbox; the lawyer reviewed the texts with the checkbox, so have them confirm sign-in-wrap is enough before relying on it. The Terms say "You accept these terms when you continue with Google, GitHub or an email code at sign-in, below the notice that says so"; that sentence changed with the mechanism without a new `TERMS_VERSION`, because what users accept did not change.

1. Before any method, the login page posts `{ version, acceptTerms: true, confirmAdult: true }` (`TermsAcceptanceInput`) to `POST /api/auth/accept-terms`, a Portifact endpoint in the auth allowlist with the exact-Origin check and a per-IP limit. The body states what the notice says; the page sends it only when the user chose a method.
2. Without a session, the Worker answers with `__Host-pf_terms` (`Secure; HttpOnly; SameSite=Lax; Path=/`, 30 minutes): the version and the acceptance time, HMAC-signed with a key derived from `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`. It survives the provider redirects because the callback is a top-level navigation.
3. Better Auth database hooks (`apps/app/src/auth/terms.ts`) enforce it. `user.create.before` refuses to create an account without a valid, current, unexpired cookie and stores `terms_version` and `terms_accepted_at` on the new user. `session.create.before` refuses a session for an account whose stored version is not current unless the cookie is present, and then records it. Refusals are `APIError` `FORBIDDEN` with code `terms_not_accepted`: the email-code sign-in answers 403, and provider callbacks redirect to `/login?error=terms_not_accepted`. `session.create.after` clears the cookie.
4. With a session, the endpoint records the acceptance on that account directly, keeping the time of an earlier acceptance of the same version.
5. Sessions that already exist are checked too: the dashboard routes, the `/api/v1` session lookup and `/read/<id>` treat a session of an account without the current version as no session, so the visitor lands on `/login`, which shows "Review the terms to continue" (the notice with "Agree and continue", or sign out) instead of forwarding to `next`.

The Privacy Policy also describes the email suppression list (undeliverable address records with their retention) and the daily email counts; keep them in sync with `SUPPRESSION_TTL_MS` (`packages/domain/src/email.ts`) and "Identity" in [security.md](security.md). They were added on 2026-11-08 without a new `TERMS_VERSION`: they describe processing that protects delivery for everyone and changes no one's rights.

The Privacy Policy says that owners get an expiry notice a few days before their artifacts expire, at most one a day, unless they turn it off in Settings, and that undeliverable addresses get none; keep it in sync with "Expiry notices" in [operations.md](operations.md). The policy announced these notices as planned since its first version; they went live without a new `TERMS_VERSION`, because they use the address already kept for sign-in, only for the service, with an opt-out, and change no one's rights.

The Privacy Policy and the Terms say who sees a comment: its artifact's creator always, and the other people with access only as the creator's comment visibility allows (by default, only the threads they or the creator started). This narrowed what other readers see when comment visibility shipped, on the version of 2026-11-08 without a new `TERMS_VERSION`, because it only reduces who reads a comment and changes no one's rights; keep it in sync with "Comment visibility" in [security.md](security.md).

The Privacy Policy describes the access log (what is recorded, that only the artifact's creator sees it, or a workspace's admins and editors, 30 days on free and a year on paid plans, erasure with the artifact or the visitor's account, share link openings recorded for the link); keep it in sync with "Access logs" in [security.md](security.md), `ACCESS_LOG` and `accessLogRetentionDays` in `PLANS` (`packages/contracts`). Access logs were listed as planned, with the same 30 days, since the policy's first version; they went live on the version of 2026-11-08 without a new `TERMS_VERSION` pending the maintainer's decision, recorded in the pull request that shipped them. Since the viewer with the Portifact bar, only `restricted` artifacts record openings: visits of public and `authenticated` artifacts are never recorded, even with a session, because any site can send a signed-in visitor to them; the policy says so, which narrows what is collected and needs no new acceptance.

New accounts also need a solved human check (Cloudflare Turnstile, `__Host-pf_human`), enforced next to the acceptance in the same `user.create.before` hook; see "Human check" in [security.md](security.md). The Privacy Policy lists it under what is collected, the cookie table and Cloudflare's processing; keep them in sync if the check changes.

The Privacy Policy lists the two preference cookies (`__Secure-pf_theme`, `__Secure-pf_lang`) that `portifact.com` and `app.portifact.com` share, and says they never reach `portifact.dev`; keep it in sync with "Cookies and sessions" in [security.md](security.md). They were added on the version of 2026-11-08 without a new `TERMS_VERSION`: they are strictly functional, hold only the theme or language the visitor chose and no identifier, are set only when the visitor chooses, and change no one's rights.

The Terms describe the plans ("Plans": the limits table, the paid features, how a plan is granted and what happens when it ends or is lowered) and the lifecycle by plan (30 days, pins on paid plans, an archive of 6 months on free and 12 on paid plans, fixed when archived); the Privacy Policy's retention table gives the same archive lengths and the previous versions each plan keeps. Keep them in sync with `PLANS` (`packages/contracts/src/plans.ts`), "Plans", "Version retention" and "Pins and archive length" in [operations.md](operations.md). The Terms list only features that are built: Team's reviews, integrations and content search and Enterprise's SSO, SCIM, audit export, policies and EU residency are in `PLAN_FEATURES` but not offered in the text. There is no checkout: the operator grants plans after a request to `hello@portifact.com`, at a price agreed with the customer, and tells the customer by email before a plan ends (a manual step; nothing sends it automatically). A lower plan deletes nothing of the owner's artifacts, comments or settings, but previous versions and access log rows it does not keep are pruned, and the Terms say so.

The Terms ("Workspaces") and the Privacy Policy describe workspaces: roles and what admins and editors see and do with members' content and comments, that content stays with the workspace, seats, the last admin, workspace and email domain access, memberships, invitations and their emails, moves of artifacts with their access log, deletion of a workspace and what the export holds; keep them in sync with "Owners, workspaces and roles" and "Workspace and email domain access" in [security.md](security.md), "Workspaces" in [operations.md](operations.md), `INVITATION_LIMITS` and `WORKSPACE_DOMAINS` in contracts.

The Terms ("Share links"), the Privacy Policy (what is kept, the link cookie, link openings in the access log, retention) and the Acceptable Use Policy describe share links; keep them in sync with "Share links" in [security.md](security.md) and `SHARE_LINKS`.

The Privacy Policy describes comment notices (who gets them, at most every 10 minutes, what they hold and never hold, the setting, the mute and the one-click unsubscribe, the pending activity kept); keep it in sync with "Comment notices" in [security.md](security.md) and [operations.md](operations.md) and `COMMENT_NOTICE`.

The Privacy Policy describes artifact analytics (counts per artifact and day, no identifier, distinct readers only for artifacts shared with listed people, retention as the access log); keep it in sync with "Artifact analytics" in [security.md](security.md).

The Terms ("Branding"), the Acceptable Use Policy and the Privacy Policy describe owner branding (rights to the name, logo and link, no imitation, the hosting notice, suspension, what is kept, the logo served without referrer or cookie); keep them in sync with "Owner branding" in [security.md](security.md) and "Brands" in [operations.md](operations.md).

The Terms ("Service tokens and publish keys"), the Acceptable Use Policy ("Coding agents") and the Privacy Policy describe service tokens and publish keys (the workspace's responsibility, the editor role, scopes, restrictions, at most 366 days, what is kept, a hash of the secret); keep them in sync with "Service tokens" and "Publish keys" in [security.md](security.md) and `SERVICE_TOKENS`.

The Privacy Policy describes the audit log (what is recorded and never recorded, admins of a workspace seeing which member did what, removed members, a deleted member's opaque ID kept until the event expires, 90 days, the personal space's export); keep it in sync with "Audit log" in [security.md](security.md) and `AUDIT_RETENTION_DAYS`.

The three pages have a "Features not yet available" section for webhooks and custom domains, which are built but switched off in production: they say what will apply once each is enabled, and that nothing of it is kept today. Keep them in sync with "Webhooks" and "Custom domains" in [security.md](security.md).

`terms_version` and `terms_accepted_at` are Better Auth `additionalFields` with `input: false`, so no request body can set them; `get-session` returns them to the login page.

Bearer tokens issued to the CLI and MCP clients before an account accepted are not revoked; the account is asked at its next browser sign-in or dashboard visit.

## Pending changes to the legal texts

Changes that the legal pages do not describe yet, to be written when they ship (with a `TERMS_VERSION` bump if the change is material):

- Webhooks and custom domains, when they are switched on: move them out of the "Features not yet available" sections into the body of the pages, check the text against the code at that moment, and announce it. The terms were accepted with the version of 2026-12-10, so switching them on needs no new version unless what they do changed.
- Payments: a checkout, a payment processor, invoices, taxes, refunds and automatic renewal. None exists; the Terms say plans are granted by request at an agreed price. A processor is a new party in the Privacy Policy and a material change.
- SSO and SCIM (Enterprise): not built and not described. They would add identity data from customers' providers and change who controls a member's account.
- EU data residency ([decision 0005](decisions/0005-regions.md)): not built. The Privacy Policy's "Where data is stored" says data may be processed anywhere Cloudflare runs; a region option changes it.
- Team's reviews, integrations and content search, and Enterprise's audit export and policies: in `PLAN_FEATURES` but not built, and not described.

## Changing the texts

1. Edit the pages. A change to wording that does not alter anyone's rights can keep the version.
2. For a material change, set `TERMS_VERSION` to the new effective date and update the version and date on all three pages (the test fails until they match). Every account is then asked to accept again at its next sign-in or dashboard visit.
3. Announce material changes in advance, as the pages promise.
